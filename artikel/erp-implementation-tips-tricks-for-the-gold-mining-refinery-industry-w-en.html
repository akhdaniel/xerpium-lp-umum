ERP Implementation Tips & Tricks for the Gold Mining & Refinery Industry with Xerpium

Gold mining and refining is one of the hardest industries to digitize. A single ounce of fine gold passes through permits, pits, haul cycles, stockpiles, assay labs, furnaces, purity certificates, vaults, and finally an invoice — each step owned by a different team, often recorded in a different spreadsheet. When ERP projects fail in this sector, it is rarely because the software lacks features. It fails because the implementation ignored how ore actually moves, how grade is actually measured, and how finance actually closes the books.

Xerpium, an Odoo-based enterprise ERP suite, provides a dedicated gold mining and refinery stack covering mine sites, blocks, production orders, stockpiles, assays, refinery jobs, bullion stock, price boards, sales, and HSE. The tips below focus on how to roll that stack out quickly, safely, and with measurable results.

Why Gold Mining ERP Projects Stall

Common Challenges

  • Fragmented data between pit and plant. Production tonnage lives in site spreadsheets, assay results in lab files, and refinery yields in another workbook — so recovery rate and loss rate are reconciled manually, often weeks late.
  • Unclear grade and inventory accuracy. Without weighted average grade tracking on stockpiles, estimated gold content becomes an argument rather than a number.
  • Compliance and permit exposure. IUP/IUPK/IPR permits, Permit to Work documents, and HSE incident records are tracked in binders, making expiry dates and audit trails hard to prove.
  • Costing blind spots. Fuel, equipment hours, maintenance, and chemical purchases are not tied to a cost center per site or block, so cost per ton is an estimate.
  • Change resistance at the face. Operators, shift supervisors, and lab analysts see ERP as additional paperwork unless entry is fast and tied to their daily routine.

Tip 1: Phase the Rollout Along the Gold Value Chain

Do not attempt a big-bang go-live across mining, refinery, sales, and accounting. Phase the implementation in the same order gold physically moves.

Phase 1 — Master data and site structure. Configure gold.mine.site with site codes, GPS coordinates, area in hectares, and permit management including permit number, type, issue date, expiry, and computed days remaining. Then build gold.mine.block with block code, mining type (open pit, underground, alluvial), geological zone, ore type, estimated reserve, and grade estimation that computes estimated gold in kilograms.

Phase 2 — Production and stockpile. Activate gold.production.order and gold.production.line so planned versus actual tonnage, achievement percentage, equipment, operator, and fuel usage are captured per shift. Link each order to a target stockpile so tonnage is auto-added when the order is marked done.

Phase 3 — Assay, refinery, and bullion. Bring in gold.assay.sample, gold.assay.result, gold.refinery.job, gold.purity.certificate, and gold.bullion.stock.

Phase 4 — Commercial and finance. Switch on gold.price.board, gold.sale.order, and the mining accounting features: auto-journal from production to stockpile, refinery costing capitalized into bullion inventory, revenue recognition with cost matching, and royalty calculation as a percentage of revenue.

Each phase delivers working output on its own, which keeps sponsors engaged.

Tip 2: Treat Master Data as a Project, Not a Task

Most delays in a gold mining ERP software rollout trace back to master data. Prioritize four datasets before configuration starts:

  • Ore types in gold.ore.type, classified by mineralogy and processing method (smelting, CIL, CIP, heap leach), since they are referenced by both blocks and production orders.
  • Equipment registry in gold.equipment — excavators, dump trucks, crushers, mills, pumps, loaders, drill rigs, bulldozers, conveyors — with asset code, brand, model, year, plate number, serial and engine number, current hours, purchase value, residual, and insurance expiry.
  • Cost centers and shifts via gold.cost.center and gold.shift, so every analytic account is linked per site or block and every day, night, or evening shift maps to a production order.
  • Employee records for operators, lab analysts, and technicians, since production lines, assay collection, and analyst assignment all reference HR data.

Clean this first and configuration becomes assembly rather than archaeology.

Tip 3: Design the Workflow Before You Configure It

Xerpium's Dynamic Stage capability lets you define configurable workflows for production and refinery, with Python hooks, server actions, RBAC, and automatic synchronization between stage and native state. Use a workshop to map real approval points: who releases a block from exploration to development to production, who signs off a quality review stage before a refinery job is completed, and who may revoke a purity certificate.

Mapping this early prevents the classic mistake of implementing a generic workflow and then rewriting it after go-live.

Tip 4: Target Quick Wins That Site Teams Feel Immediately

Adoption improves when the first deliverables remove real pain:

  • Permit expiry visibility — computed days remaining on every site permit.
  • Shift production entry — one form capturing equipment, operator, start and end time, auto-computed duration, tons extracted, fuel liters, and per-line notes.
  • Stockpile accuracy — capacity, current tonnage, weighted average grade recomputed after each addition.
  • Pending assay list — samples waiting for analysis, surfaced instead of chased.
  • Gold price lookup — buy price, sell price, computed spread, spot USD per troy ounce, USD/IDR rate, and spot IDR per gram per purity level.

Business Benefits

  • End-to-end traceability from block to bullion bar, with certificates linked to refinery jobs.
  • Accurate recovery and loss rates computed from input ore, grade, bullion output, slag, and other loss.
  • Cost per ton grounded in analytic accounts per site and block, including fuel and maintenance cost.
  • Faster financial close through automatic journals on production, refinery capitalization, and royalty calculation.
  • Stronger HSE and audit posture via incident reporting, safety inspections with scoring, and Permit to Work records.

Example Implementation Flow

A multi-site operator starts with two sites and six blocks in month one. Shift-based production orders go live in month two; each order links to a stockpile that auto-updates tonnage and weighted grade. In month three, assay samples and results feed grade data, and refinery jobs record smelting input and output with purity-based fine gold computation. By month four, bullion bars carry bar number, purity, fine gold, troy ounce weight, vault location, and certificate. Price board, sale orders, invoicing, and royalty postings complete the cycle — with dashboards showing production, recovery, and inventory in one view.

Conclusion

Successful ERP implementation in gold mining is less about ambition and more about sequence: clean master data, phased scope along the value chain, workflows designed before configuration, and early wins that site teams actually feel.
