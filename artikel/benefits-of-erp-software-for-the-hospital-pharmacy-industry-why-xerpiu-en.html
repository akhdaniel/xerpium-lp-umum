Benefits of ERP Software for the Hospital & Pharmacy Industry: Why Xerpium Matters

Hospitals and pharmacies operate under a level of complexity few other industries face. A single patient journey can touch registration, queue management, outpatient consultation, laboratory testing, radiology, pharmacy dispensing, inpatient admission, and billing — each with its own documentation requirements, clinical standards, and regulatory obligations. Without an integrated system, each of those touchpoints becomes an isolated record.

The consequences are familiar to most hospital administrators. Medical records live in one application, pharmacy stock in another, and billing in a spreadsheet. Nurses rewrite vital signs that were already recorded elsewhere. Finance teams reconcile patient bills manually at month-end. BPJS claims are compiled by hand, and SatuSehat reporting becomes a separate project rather than a by-product of daily operations. The result is slow service, revenue leakage, and compliance risk.

Xerpium, an Odoo-based ERP suite, addresses this by bringing clinical, pharmacy, and financial processes into one connected system. Below are the concrete benefits hospitals and pharmacies gain from that integration.

Common Challenges in Hospital and Pharmacy Operations

  • Fragmented patient data — medical records, insurance details, and billing history stored in separate systems with no single patient identity.
  • Manual queue and visit handling — patients wait without clear status, and registration staff re-enter the same data at every stage.
  • Pharmacy stock and expiry risk — no batch or expiry tracking, no minimum stock alerts, and weak control over narcotics and psychotropics.
  • Complex BPJS and insurance workflows — eligibility checks, SEP numbers, referrals, and claims handled outside the operational system.
  • Delayed financial visibility — room charges, procedures, and dispensed medicines reach the bill late, making revenue per department hard to measure.

Benefit 1: Operational Efficiency Across the Patient Journey

The most immediate benefit of an integrated hospital system is the elimination of repeated data entry. In Xerpium, the patient profile extends res.partner with MR number, NIK, BPJS number, and insurance details, with the medical record number generated automatically from a sequence. That single identity carries through every subsequent transaction.

Queue management assigns an automatic daily number per date and department, tracks arrival and call time, and moves patients through a clear status flow from Waiting to Called, In Progress, and Done or Skipped. A visit can be created directly from the queue, so registration data flows into the outpatient visit without rekeying. The visit itself captures vital signs, anamnesis, SOAP notes, and ICD-10 diagnoses in one record.

Pharmacy follows the same logic. A prescription moves through Draft, Confirmed, Dispensing, and Dispensed, and the dispensing record links back to that prescription — comparing quantity prescribed against quantity dispensed, recording the responsible pharmacist, and automatically generating a stock picking from the pharmacy location.

Benefit 2: Real-Time Data Visibility for Clinical and Management Teams

Decisions in a hospital are time-sensitive. Ward availability, lab results, and patient condition all need to be visible the moment they change.

Xerpium tracks ward status as Available, Occupied, or Maintenance, with capacity, rate per night, floor and building location, and a computed occupied count per ward. Room types cover VIP, VVIP, Kelas 1 to 3, ICU, NICU, Isolasi, and UGD. When a patient is discharged, ward status is released automatically.

On the clinical side, vital sign monitoring records blood pressure, pulse, temperature, respiratory rate, SpO2, blood glucose, urine output, pain scale, and consciousness level, each timestamped and attributed to the recording nurse. Lab orders progress through a defined workflow — Draft, Ordered, Sample Collected, Processing, Resulted, Verified — with result values, normal ranges, and flags for normal, low, high, or critical. Clinicians see status, not guesswork.

Benefit 3: Cost Control and Revenue Accuracy

Revenue leakage in hospitals usually comes from charges that never reach the bill. Xerpium's patient bill consolidates consultation, procedure, medicine, lab, radiology, room, and nursing lines into one document with subtotal, discount, tax, total, paid, and due amounts. Room charges are added automatically at admission discharge, procedures can be pushed onto the bill from the procedure record, and medicine costs flow in from dispensing at the product list price.

The accounting integration then generates an account.move out_invoice from the bill, computes insurance coverage against the amount due, and tracks payment as paid, partial, or unpaid. Because every line carries a department reference, management can analyse revenue per department or poliklinik rather than estimating it.

On the inventory side, the medicine catalog supports minimum stock alerts, expiry tracking, batch and lot numbers, and therapeutic classification — which directly reduces write-offs from expired stock.

Benefit 4: Compliance Built Into Daily Work with SIMRS & Hospital Software

Compliance is where integrated SIMRS and hospital software earns its keep. Xerpium includes BPJS Kesehatan integration with eligibility checks through VClaim and PCare APIs, BPJS referral creation, SEP numbers per visit, and claims for outpatient, inpatient, emergency, procedures, medicines, and lab — with claim status tracked from Draft through Submitted, Processing, Approved or Rejected, and Paid.

SatuSehat integration maps operational data to FHIR resources: Patient, Encounter, Practitioner, Location, Organization, Condition, Medication, and Observation, plus Composition, Clinical Impression, Service Request, Procedure, Allergy Intolerance, Medication Request, and Medication Dispense. API management handles OAuth 2.0 token caching, sandbox and production switching, automatic retry for 429/500/502/503 errors, request logging, and sync state per resource.

Clinical compliance is supported too — doctor masters record SIP number and expiry alongside STR, and the medicine master tracks BPOM registration and controlled substances such as narcotics and psychotropics.

Benefit 5: Scalability as the Facility Grows

A clinic that becomes a hospital should not need to replace its system. Xerpium's dynamic, configurable workflow stages for prescriptions and admissions allow processes to evolve without redevelopment. Multi-company API configuration supports hospital groups, while department structures covering Rawat Jalan, Rawat Inap, UGD, Lab, Radiology, Pharmacy, Surgery, and ICU can be extended as services expand.

Business Benefits at a Glance

  • Shorter patient waiting times through automated queue-to-visit flow
  • One lifetime patient record with visit, admission, and bill counts
  • Fewer dispensing errors through prescription linkage and allergy notes
  • Reduced expired stock through batch and expiry tracking
  • Faster, more accurate BPJS claims and SatuSehat reporting
  • Department-level revenue analysis for management decisions

Example Flow: From Queue to Paid Invoice

A BPJS patient arrives and receives an automatic queue number for the Rawat Jalan department. Eligibility is checked via VClaim and an SEP number is issued. When called, the queue entry creates an outpatient visit; the nurse records vital signs and the doctor documents SOAP notes with a primary ICD-10 diagnosis. A lab order is raised and moves to Resulted and Verified. The doctor issues a prescription, which the pharmacist dispenses with batch and expiry tracking, automatically generating the stock picking. Consultation, lab, and medicine charges flow into the patient bill, insurance coverage is applied, an invoice is generated in accounting, and the encounter is synced to SatuSehat as a FHIR Encounter.

Conclusion

The benefit of ERP in healthcare is not technology for its own sake — it is fewer handoffs, fewer errors, faster billing, and compliance that happens as a by-product of normal work.
