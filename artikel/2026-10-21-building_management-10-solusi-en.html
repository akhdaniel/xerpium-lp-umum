10 Best ERP Solutions for Building Management Operations

October 21, 2026

Last reviewed: 21 October 2026

Running a multi-property building portfolio on disconnected spreadsheets creates recurring operational risk: unit ownership and occupancy data drift, IPL and utility charges are billed late, maintenance requests lack SLA ownership, and facility or security events remain undocumented. When property, resident, billing, maintenance and accounting data do not share one system, staff re-enter data, audit trails weaken, and revenue leakage becomes difficult to detect. An ERP designed for building management connects these processes. The ten Xerpium solutions below cover the core operating cycle from property structure to utility billing.

The 10 Solutions at a Glance

  1. Property & Tower — Creates one hierarchy for properties, towers, floors, unit types and billing configuration.
  2. Unit Management — Tracks unit ownership, occupancy status, usage and automatic ownership history.
  3. Resident Management — Keeps resident identity, move history, family and vehicle records tied to units.
  4. Rental Contract — Manages commercial leases, deposit and annual rent billing, and unit status sync.
  5. Billing & IPL — Automates monthly IPL, meter consumption and mass invoice generation.
  6. Maintenance Request — Converts resident requests into SLA-prioritized work orders with photo records.
  7. Work Order — Plans work types, assignments, time, materials, checklists and completion documentation.
  8. Facility Booking — Controls facility capacity, hourly rates, resident booking and billing integration.
  9. Security Incident — Records incident type, location, workflow progress and action taken.
  10. Utility Management — Links meter readings, consumption calculations and tiered tariffs to billing.

1. Property & Tower

This solution centralizes the physical portfolio structure. It records property name, code, address, manager and property type—apartment, office, mixed, mall, school, hospital, hotel, industrial or government—and manages each tower with its unit allocation. Floor management captures floor numbers and codes, while unit type configuration covers studio, 1BR, 2BR, 3BR and penthouse. Product configuration for IPL, sinking fund and utility charges is also kept here. This removes inconsistent property master data and gives billing, maintenance and security modules a single reference point for every building asset.

2. Unit Management

Unit Management tracks each unit by name, code, property, tower, floor and unit type, and records size in square meters along with bedroom and bathroom counts. It supports residential and commercial usage and maintains unit status: Available, Occupied, Reserved, Maintenance or Inactive. Owner data is linked to the unit, including multi-owner history, and any owner change automatically creates an ownership history record. This closes the gap between physical occupancy and billing responsibility, helping building teams avoid charging the wrong party or losing move-in and move-out context.

3. Resident Management

Resident Management keeps identity, contact and unit association in one place. It stores KTP or passport details, phone and email, and classifies each resident as Owner, Tenant, Family or Staff, with Active, Moved Out or Blacklisted status. Move in and move out dates are tracked with history, and the module records family members within a unit as well as vehicle registrations per resident. Building operations teams use this to know who actually occupies each unit, support access control, and maintain accurate contact data for billing, incident and facility communication.

4. Rental Contract

Rental Contract manages commercial unit leases with clear financial and occupancy controls. It stores contract start and end dates with automatic overlap validation, annual rent, down payment and balance tracking. The module generates down payment invoices and annual rent invoices through accounting, and tracks contract status as Draft, Active, Expired or Cancelled. Unit status is synchronized automatically so a leased commercial unit becomes occupied and returns to available when the contract ends. This reduces lease administration errors, missed renewals and manual unit-status updates that cause double-booking or lost revenue.

5. Billing & IPL

Billing & IPL automates monthly IPL billing per unit or resident. It supports service charge, sinking fund, water, electricity, gas, internet and parking charge types. Meter reading data calculates consumption for utility billing, and invoices are generated automatically to accounting. Each bill carries a due date and outstanding balance tracking, while a monthly generation wizard creates bulk billing for all units. This replaces manual spreadsheet billing and helps prevent missed charges, late invoices and unclear outstanding balances that delay cash collection.

6. Maintenance Request

Maintenance Request lets residents create maintenance requests directly, then routes them through priority and SLA rules. Priorities are Low, Normal, High and Urgent, with automatic SLA response and completion deadlines; urgent is set to one hour for response and eight hours for completion. Requests can be assigned to technicians from HR, scheduled and dispatched, with checklist lines per request. Before and after photos document the work, and invoices or purchase orders can be created from maintenance activities. This turns reactive repair intake into a traceable, costed and SLA-managed process.

7. Work Order

Work Order structures maintenance execution after a request or planned task. It supports work order types including electrical, plumbing, civil, HVAC, cleaning, landscaping and IT, and assigns work to an employee or vendor contractor. Time tracking records technician working hours, material usage is linked to product inventory, and task checklists show completion status. Before and after photos provide visual documentation. This gives building managers a single view of who did what, which materials were consumed, and what remains open—reducing unbilled labor and material costs.

8. Facility Booking

Facility Booking manages shared amenities per property, such as gym, pool and function room. Each facility records maximum capacity, hourly rate and deposit requirements. Residents use a booking calendar to reserve facilities, and booking integrations automatically create invoices to billing. This prevents overbooking and manual payment chasing. It also gives management visibility over facility utilization and fee collection, so amenity revenue is captured and capacity rules are enforced without staff manually coordinating calls or spreadsheets.

9. Security Incident

Security Incident captures events such as theft, vandalism, fire, flood, noise, trespassing and accident. A user reports the incident with location details at property, tower or unit level, and the record moves through a workflow from Draft to Investigated, Resolved or Cancelled. The module records the action taken, creating an auditable trail of what happened and how management responded. This replaces scattered message threads and missing incident logs with centralized, reviewable security data that supports follow-up, reporting and preventive action.

10. Utility Management

Utility Management connects utility consumption directly to billing. It defines utility types such as water, electricity, gas and internet per property, and accepts meter readings manually or through import. The module calculates consumption using previous and current readings, applies per-unit tariffs with tiered pricing, and links the resulting consumption line to billing automatically. This eliminates manual consumption recalculations and tariff errors, especially in buildings with multiple utility types and tiered charges, and ensures residents are billed only for actual recorded consumption.

How to Choose the Right Solution

Start by mapping your highest operational friction. If commercial leases and utility recovery drive revenue, prioritize Rental Contract, Billing & IPL and Utility Management together. If resident repair requests are high-volume, make Maintenance Request and Work Order the core of your evaluation because SLA tracking and technician assignment directly affect service quality. If shared amenities or security incidents create risk, Facility Booking and Security Incident should be included early.

Second, check integration requirements. Building management works best when billing flows to accounting, work order materials connect to inventory, technicians come from HR, and vendor work links to purchase. Confirm the solution supports those paths without manual re-entry.

Third, avoid standalone point tools. A portfolio-wide ERP baseline should start with Property & Tower, Unit Management and Resident Management because every billing, maintenance and security process depends on accurate property, unit and occupant data.

Conclusion

Building management operations need more than a general accounting system. The ten solutions above address the full operational chain: portfolio structure, unit occupancy, residents, leases, IPL and utility billing, maintenance, work execution, facility bookings and security incidents.
