# Maintenance Service Management with Xerpium ERP: From Service Orders to Invoiced Work Orders

Industrial equipment service is rarely a single event. It is a chain of decisions: a customer reports a problem, a service team estimates the work, a quotation is issued, technicians are dispatched, actual costs are captured, and an invoice follows. When these steps live in spreadsheets, emails, and disconnected systems, important details get lost—installed equipment history, warranty dates, competitor pricing, and estimate-to-actual variance. Xerpium ERP addresses this with a structured maintenance service flow that connects service orders, work orders, customer equipment, preventive maintenance schedules, checklists, and financial documents in one system.

This article explains how Xerpium supports maintenance service operations end to end.

Common Challenges in Maintenance Service Operations

Many B2B equipment and field service teams face similar operational friction:

  • Equipment data is scattered across spreadsheets, with no single view of installed units, serial numbers, warranty expiry, or last service date.
  • Service requests arrive as emails or paper notes without structured service type, urgency, priority, or lead source.
  • Technician assignment and scheduling are managed manually, making it difficult to see who is assigned and when work starts or ends.
  • Actual material, labour, equipment, subcontractor, and overhead costs are not linked to the original estimate, so margin leakage goes unnoticed.
  • Preventive maintenance relies on manual tracking, causing overdue service tasks to be missed or generated too late.

How Xerpium ERP Supports Maintenance Service

Service Order Management and Competitor Tracking

Xerpium provides a controlled service order workflow. Each service order includes an auto-sequence service order number, customer information, contact person, phone, and a many-to-many list of customer equipment to be serviced. Teams can record a problem description, select service type—maintenance, repair, overhaul, installation, or inspection—and set urgency as routine, urgent, or emergency. Priority, lead source, date requested, and expected completion date are also captured.

Service orders also support competitor tracking. Users can log competitor name, quoted price, notes on competitor strengths and weaknesses, and a win/loss analysis of the quotation versus competing offers.

The service order follows clear actions: submit estimate from draft to estimated, create quotation to generate a sale order automatically, create work order from quoted status, reset to draft, or cancel.

Work Order Execution and Estimate vs. Actual Variance

Once a service order is quoted, Xerpium can generate a work order with technician assignment and schedule. Work orders include an auto-sequence work order number, a link to the originating service order, related sale order, customer PO/SPK reference, service type, and equipment. Technicians are assigned from employee records through a many-to-many relationship, and schedule fields capture date scheduled, date started, date ended, and date completed.

A key control point is variance analysis. Xerpium tracks actual materials, actual labour on-site, actual labour workshop, actual equipment rental, actual subcontractor, and actual overhead separately from estimate lines. Each actual line can link to its estimate line, so the system can auto-compute total estimate versus total actual and provide profit margin visibility.

Work order state actions include start service, pause service, resume service, complete, create invoice, close, and cancel. This creates a clear lifecycle from confirmed work to invoiced and closed jobs.

Customer Equipment and Rotating Equipment Catalog

Maintenance service quality depends on knowing the equipment being serviced. Xerpium stores customer equipment records with equipment name and code, customer, serial number, model, location at customer site, installation date, warranty expiry, and current condition such as running, stop, or needs monitoring. Technical specs including horsepower, RPM, and capacity are also available. Service count is auto-computed from work orders, and last service date updates automatically after service.

Customer equipment can link to a rotating equipment catalog, which standardizes equipment types such as pump, compressor, turbine, motor, fan, blower, and gearbox. Catalog entries include brand, model, horsepower, RPM, capacity, mounting type, technical specifications, and common spare parts. Equipment brands can be linked to manufacturers and suppliers, with brand names such as Grundfos, Sulzer, KSB, Atlas Copco, Ingersoll Rand, Siemens, and ABB supported without creating duplicate records.

Preventive Maintenance Schedules and Checklist Execution

Xerpium allows teams to configure recurring service schedules. Each schedule includes a name, customer equipment, customer, interval type—days, months, or operating hours—interval value, service type, next service date, last service date, and a flag to auto-generate work orders.

A daily cron job detects overdue schedules where the next service date is on or before today and auto-generate work order is enabled. The system auto-creates a draft service order with a description, auto-submits the estimate to estimated status, recalculates the next service date based on the interval, and tags the source as "Preventive Maintenance Schedule."

Checklist templates support consistent inspections. Templates can filter by equipment type and contain checklist lines with item name, expected value or range, minimum and maximum acceptable values, unit of measure, critical item flag, notes, and sequence. During execution, the checklist links to a work order and records execution date and user. Each line result can capture a measured numeric value or text, pass or fail status, photo attachment, and notes. Critical items can fail the entire checklist if measured values fall outside acceptable range.

Cross-Module Integration and AI-Assisted Lookup

Xerpium connects maintenance service with the broader enterprise. Quotation creation generates a sale order, and customer PO/SPK references flow from service order to work order. Quotation line items can come from estimate lines.

For invoicing, the create invoice wizard generates an outbound invoice with auto-line items for material, labour, and subcontractor lines. If no actual costs exist, a fallback single line uses the invoice amount. Invoice status is tracked as draft, partially invoiced, or invoiced, and the work order state automatically moves to invoiced after invoice creation.

Maintenance also connects to inventory through material lines and common spare parts from the rotating equipment catalog, to HR through technician assignment and labour lines, to analytic accounts through cost center tracking and variance analysis, and to purchasing through material requirements and subcontractor purchase orders.

Xerpium’s assistant tools support equipment lookup, work order status, and schedule checks. Teams can retrieve equipment records by customer and equipment type, review work orders by customer and state, and filter schedules by overdue status.

Business Benefits of Managing Maintenance Service in Xerpium

  • A structured service order lifecycle keeps requests, quotations, work orders, and invoices connected.
  • Installed base visibility improves service planning, warranty tracking, and equipment history.
  • Estimate-to-actual variance analysis helps identify margin erosion before it becomes a pattern.
  • Preventive maintenance automation reduces overdue work and reactive service calls.
  • Invoice generation uses actual material, labour, and subcontractor lines, reducing manual billing effort.
  • Cross-module integration aligns sales, accounting, inventory, HR, purchasing, and analytics.
  • Competitor win/loss tracking gives service teams a clearer commercial view of each opportunity.

Example Workflow: From Preventive Schedule to Invoiced Work Order

A rotating equipment service provider schedules a monthly inspection for customer pump P-101 with a 30-day interval. The daily cron detects the overdue schedule and creates a draft service order tagged "Preventive Maintenance Schedule," then auto-submits the estimate. The planner reviews the estimate and creates a quotation, which generates a sale order and captures the customer PO/SPK reference. After approval, the team creates a work order with a technician and schedule.

The technician executes the inspection checklist, recording vibration, temperature, and oil pressure values. Any critical item outside the acceptable range is flagged, and photos can be attached. Actual labour, material, and subcontractor costs are captured against estimate lines, giving an immediate variance view. When the work is completed, the team runs the create invoice wizard. Material and labour lines flow into the invoice automatically, the work order moves to invoiced status, and the customer equipment service count and last service date update. Finally, the work order is closed.
