10 Best ERP Solutions for Software House Operations

November 26, 2026

Software houses run on a paradox: they build systems that automate other companies, yet their own operations often live in scattered spreadsheets, chat threads, and standalone task boards. Sprint plans sit in one tool, timesheets in another, budgets in a finance file nobody opens until the project is already over. The result is familiar — velocity that nobody can measure, phase approvals that happen verbally, developers double-booked across three projects, and profitability discovered months after delivery. When project data, HR allocation, timesheets, and sales contracts do not share a single source of truth, delivery managers spend more time reconciling numbers than managing delivery. The solutions below, drawn from the Xerpium Software House module, address those gaps as connected parts of one project ecosystem rather than isolated tools.

The 10 Solutions at a Glance

  1. Scrum Framework — Runs product backlog, sprint planning, and velocity tracking in one place.
  2. Waterfall Methodology — Enforces sequential phases with gate approvals and predecessor validation.
  3. Kanban & Hybrid — Gives visual task management with flexible methodology per project.
  4. Budget & Profitability — Links contract type, budget, and actual cost into a computed profitability figure.
  5. Team Allocation — Assigns employees to projects by role, date range, and hours per week.
  6. Technology Stack — Maintains a tech catalog mapped to projects for portfolio and filtering.
  7. User Story & Backlog — Captures stories with acceptance criteria, points, and status flow.
  8. Sprint Management — Controls sprint workflow from planning to close with capacity and velocity history.
  9. Cross-Module Integration — Connects project data to accounting, timesheet, sales, and HR.
  10. Project Reporting — Delivers velocity, profitability, phase completion, and utilization reports.

1. Scrum Framework

The Scrum Framework module turns agile ceremonies into structured data instead of meeting notes. It holds a product backlog of user stories with story points, priority levels (Low, Medium, High, Critical), and acceptance criteria, then feeds those items into sprint planning with capacity measured in story points. Each sprint carries a specific sprint goal, user stories break down into tasks in project.task, and velocity is auto-computed from completed points per sprint alongside a completion rate for backlog items. For software houses that commit to delivery dates without historical evidence, this replaces guesswork with measured team throughput across consecutive sprints.

2. Waterfall Methodology

Not every engagement fits agile — government contracts, regulated clients, and fixed-scope builds still require sequential delivery. The Waterfall Methodology module structures projects into phases with a defined sequence, start and end dates, and milestone markers, moving through states of To Do, In Progress, Review, and Done. Gate approval is required before progressing to the next phase, and predecessor validation automatically blocks a phase from starting before its dependency closes. Gate history records who approved each gate and when, with timestamps, while deliverable documents and phase-linked tasks stay attached to their phase. This eliminates informal sign-offs that later become disputes.

3. Kanban & Hybrid

Many software houses do not run pure Scrum or pure Waterfall — they run both, sometimes within the same client account. The Kanban & Hybrid module provides a visual Kanban board over project.task with task types covering Feature, Bug, Technical Debt, and Spike, so maintenance work and new development share one view. Story points can be estimated directly at task level, tasks can be assigned to a specific sprint, linked back to a user story in the backlog, or linked to a waterfall phase. Methodology is selectable per project, which means teams standardise tooling without forcing one process onto every engagement.

4. Budget & Profitability

Profitability is the number most software houses learn too late. This module ties contract type — Fixed Price, Time & Material, or Milestone Billing — to a monetary project budget and estimated hours, then tracks actual cost automatically from timesheet hours multiplied by a configurable average hourly rate (default Rp 150,000). Profitability is computed as (budget − actual cost) / budget × 100, giving a live percentage rather than a post-mortem calculation. Projects link to sale.order so commercial terms stay connected to delivery, and multi-currency is supported through company currency. Scope creep becomes visible while there is still time to respond.

5. Team Allocation

Resource conflict is the quiet killer of delivery schedules. The Team Allocation module assigns employees to projects with a specific role — Project Manager, Business Analyst, Developer, QA Engineer, DevOps, or UI/UX Designer — across a defined date range with hours per week per allocation. Total hours are computed from weeks multiplied by hours per week, and validation rules enforce that end date falls after start date and weekly hours stay between 0.1 and 168. Notes can be attached per allocation for context. Because it uses existing hr.employee records, allocation reflects the real organisation rather than a parallel staffing list.

6. Technology Stack

Software houses accumulate technology knowledge faster than they document it. The Technology Stack module maintains a catalog of technologies used across the company, organised into categories — Frontend, Backend, Database, DevOps, Mobile, and Other — with visual color tags and a unique-name constraint to prevent duplicate entries. Technologies map to projects through a many-to-many relationship, enabling project filtering by stack and a view of stack diversity across the entire portfolio. The resulting portfolio view can be presented to clients as evidence of capability. For bid preparation and staffing decisions, this turns tribal knowledge into queryable data.

7. User Story & Backlog

Requirements written as feature lists cause rework; requirements written as user stories expose intent. This module captures stories in the standard format — As a… I want… So that… — with acceptance criteria defining the conditions for completion, story points estimated using Fibonacci or linear scales, and priority set to Low, Medium, High, or Critical. Status flows from Backlog to In Sprint, In Progress, and Done, with state updating automatically when a sprint is selected. Multi-tag support draws from project.tags, and each story carries an assigned developer. Backlog grooming becomes a maintained record instead of a recurring rediscovery exercise.

8. Sprint Management

Sprint Management governs the sprint lifecycle through a Planning → Active → Closed workflow, with validation ensuring only one active sprint exists per project at a time — a constraint that prevents the overlapping sprints that distort velocity data. Each sprint defines available capacity in story points, a sprint goal to focus the team, and start and end dates. A sprint close wizard supports structured review and retrospective at the end of each cycle, while velocity history shows performance trends from sprint to sprint. The full task list per sprint stays visible throughout. Commitments become traceable against delivered capacity.

9. Cross-Module Integration

Isolated project tools create reconciliation work. The integration layer connects projects to accounting through budget fields, timesheet-derived employee cost, sale order links for invoicing, and milestone billing. Timesheet integration computes actual hours from account.analytic.line, summing unit amounts per project for hourly cost calculation. Sales integration creates projects from sale.order, with contract type determining billing method — Fixed Price producing a single invoice, Milestone producing phased invoicing. HR integration supplies team allocation from hr.employee with role assignment and hourly rate per employee for capacity planning. Data entered once flows everywhere it is needed.

10. Project Reporting

Reporting closes the loop between delivery activity and management decisions. The module provides a sprint velocity report for capacity forecasting, a project profitability report comparing budget against actual cost, a phase completion report for waterfall engagements tracking progress against gates, and a team allocation utilization report showing how resource commitments compare to availability. Because these reports read directly from sprint, phase, timesheet, and allocation records, there is no manual consolidation step and no version conflict between the operations view and the finance view. Delivery leads and executives review the same numbers from the same system.

How to Choose the Right Solution

Start with your delivery model. If most engagements are iterative, prioritise Scrum Framework, User Story & Backlog, and Sprint Management. If your contracts demand formal sign-offs, Waterfall Methodology with gate approval matters more. Mixed portfolios should begin with Kanban & Hybrid for per-project flexibility.

Follow the money trail. Organisations that cannot explain project margin should implement Budget & Profitability together with timesheet integration first — profitability depends on actual hours being captured accurately.

Check the data foundation. Team Allocation and HR integration rely on maintained hr.employee records. Technology Stack requires a disciplined catalog. Clean master data determines whether reports are trustworthy.

Sequence by pain, not by feature count. Implement the two or three modules addressing your most expensive recurring problem, confirm adoption, then extend. Cross-module integration delivers value proportional to how many modules are actually in use.

Conclusion

Software house operations fail at the seams — between sprint and timesheet, between allocation and capacity, between contract and invoice. The ten solutions above are designed as connected components of a single project ecosystem, which is what separates an ERP approach from a collection of point tools. Whether your immediate priority is measuring velocity, enforcing phase gates, or finally knowing project margin before delivery closes, the right starting point is the one that addresses your costliest blind spot.
