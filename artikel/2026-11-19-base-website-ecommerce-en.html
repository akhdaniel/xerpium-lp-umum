Website & eCommerce ERP: Building a Connected Online Store with Xerpium Software

Most B2B and retail organizations end up running their online presence as a separate business. The marketing site lives with one vendor, the online catalog in a storefront platform, and the actual orders, inventory, and invoices somewhere inside the back office. Every connection between those systems is a manual export, a spreadsheet, or a brittle integration that breaks after an update.

The cost of that fragmentation is rarely obvious on day one. It shows up later as overselling stock that was already committed, pricing on the website that no longer matches the price list finance approved, and month-end reconciliation that takes days because online revenue has to be matched back by hand. Xerpium approaches this differently: the Website & eCommerce module is a base module of the suite, so the storefront is not an add-on bolted onto the ERP — it is part of it.

  • Because it is a base module, product data, orders, and customer records are shared rather than synchronized between systems.

  • Real-time dashboards and reports, plus the Xerpium AI Assistant, give commercial and finance teams a single view of online performance.

Common Challenges in Running an Online Channel

Disconnected product and price data

When the catalog lives outside the ERP, someone has to keep it current. A new product variant, a discontinued SKU, or an approved price change has to be entered twice. For example, a distributor updating a price list for Q1 may update the ERP immediately but only push the website a week later — and sell at the old price in the meantime.

Orders that arrive as data, not as transactions

A web order is only useful once it becomes a confirmed sales order with stock reserved, a delivery scheduled, and an invoice issued. Without integration, that conversion step is manual, which limits how many orders the team can handle without adding headcount.

No single view of performance

Marketing reports on sessions and conversions, finance reports on recognized revenue, and operations reports on fulfilment. When those numbers come from different systems with different cut-off rules, leadership spends meetings reconciling definitions instead of making decisions.

Governance across entities and currencies

Groups that operate several companies or sell in more than one currency need the online channel to respect the same structure. Otherwise, consolidation becomes a manual exercise and audit requests take longer to answer.

A Short Example Flow

Consider a company selling industrial components to both resellers and direct buyers.

A product manager publishes a new item in the catalog through the Xerpium website builder. A reseller finds the item, places an order through integrated checkout, and the order is recorded in the correct company entity and currency.

Because the storefront sits on the same platform as the rest of the suite, the order flows directly into operations and finance without a re-entry step.
