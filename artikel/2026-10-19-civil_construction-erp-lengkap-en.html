Complete ERP solution for Civil Construction: Connecting Budget, Procurement, Site Work Acceptance, and Financial Control

October 19, 2026

Civil construction projects rarely move in a straight line. Contractors manage complex project hierarchies, site-level material consumption, subcontractor claims, milestone billing, work acceptance, tax retention, and financial consolidation—often across separate spreadsheets, procurement tools, and accounting systems. When budget, procurement, and site execution are disconnected, cost overruns surface late, supplier invoices are disputed, and project profitability becomes difficult to measure.

Xerpium ERP provides a complete ERP scope for civil construction that connects project structure, budget control, BOQ-based procurement, material consumption, work acceptance, accounting, tax, and KPI reporting in one system.

Common challenges in civil construction

  • Project structures with multiple sub-projects, revisions, and parent completion rules are hard to maintain outside a structured hierarchy.
  • Project budgets need planned versus practical tracking, analytic allocation, and formal approval states before execution.
  • Material consumption per task must trigger the right stock movement and procurement request without manual duplication.
  • Subcontractor billing and supplier invoices need formal quantity acceptance to prevent overbilling and payment disputes.
  • Financial reporting must combine project costs, heavy equipment assets, construction revenue, PPN 11%, PPh final construction, and multi-company consolidation.

Xerpium solution scope

Project structure and budget control

Xerpium manages projects through a parent-child, WBS-like hierarchy with sub-project counts and parent completion blocking. Each project has a short unique key such as JKT-TOWER, auto-generated from the project name, along with version and revision tracking. Users can configure hierarchical project types, custom statuses, department assignment, and auto-sequence numbering per project.

Budget control uses crossovered budget per project with planned versus practical comparison. Budgets move through a state workflow: Draft, Confirm, Validate, and Done. Analytic accounts enable cost-center tracking across the project. The system also records provisions and reimbursement costs, and each project has a smart button to view linked purchase order counts, totals, and purchase invoices.

Material, procurement, and BOQ integration

For site execution, Xerpium records material consumed per task using product and quantity. Task stock moves capture source and destination locations, picking, and scrap. Each stock move follows a clear state: Pending, Confirmed, Assigned, Done, or Cancelled.

Bill of Quantity integration connects WBS activities to a procurement list. Pre-configured product sets or kits support faster material planning. Auto-procure connects the flow from task material to stock request to purchase request automatically, so site requirements do not sit in a separate spreadsheet. Material consumption is also allocated through analytic lines for cost tracking.

Scheduling, roles, and stakeholder visibility

Project forecasting covers resource and role forecasting, employee capacity, and leave handling. Roles are hierarchical and assigned per user, project, and role. External stakeholders are tracked as partner-based records with role and contact information. Milestone status shows execution percentage based on hours allocated versus hours done, plus dedication percentage per milestone. Task features include parent blocking, automatic parent due dates, stage management, code sequencing, and notes.

Work acceptance and billing control

Work acceptance is a formal sign-off document with Draft, Accept, and Cancel states. Each work acceptance line links to a purchase order line, including quantity and price. Quantity tracking compares qty_accepted versus qty_to_accept per line. Invoice quantity must match the accepted quantity before vendor billing can proceed.

The module supports government milestone sign-off, subcontractor work approval, and material receipt. Xerpium can be configured to require work acceptance before goods receipt and before vendor billing, reducing payment risk in subcontract-heavy projects.

Financial reporting and dashboard KPI

The accounting scope includes a balance sheet covering current assets, fixed assets such as heavy equipment, liabilities, and equity. Profit and loss reports show construction revenue, cost of goods sold, project costs, and net income. Trial balance reporting uses 32 account groups with six-digit codes. Tax handling includes PPN 11%, PPh final construction, and tax retention.

Turbo Report delivers financial reporting in under one minute and supports multi-company consolidation. The dashboard includes 14 KPI widgets: total contract revenue, total contracts, active projects, outstanding AR, total material cost, open tasks, open POs, revenue trend, material cost trend, top clients, and top suppliers.

End-to-end flow

A typical civil construction cycle in Xerpium starts when a service contract is recorded in Sales and a project is created with a unique key and WBS-like hierarchy. The project budget is prepared, confirmed, and validated. BOQ activities generate a procurement list for required materials and subcontract work.

Site teams record task material consumption. Auto-procure converts site requirements into stock requests and purchase requests. Purchase issues POs for material, subcontract services, or equipment parts. Goods are received through task stock moves. Work acceptance records the accepted quantity against each PO line. Vendor invoices are validated only against accepted quantities. Accounting records the costs, input tax, retention, and project revenue. The dashboard then updates total contract revenue, material cost, open tasks, open POs, and AR trends in real time.

Business benefits

  • One connected system from project hierarchy and budget to procurement, site execution, acceptance, and financial reporting.
  • Stronger budget discipline through planned versus practical comparison and analytic allocation.
  • Procurement tied to BOQ and task material requirements reduces manual requisition gaps.
  • Formal work acceptance reduces subcontractor and supplier billing disputes.
  • Project profitability and cash flow become visible through KPI widgets and fast financial consolidation.
  • Structured project versioning, roles, stakeholders, and milestone tracking keep site and head office aligned.

Implementation steps

  1. Prepare master data — define project hierarchy, WBS, BOQ, product and material lists, roles, stakeholders, and chart of accounts.
  2. Configure finance and project parameters — set up the construction chart of accounts, PPN 11%, PPh final construction, tax retention, budget states, and work acceptance requirements.
  3. Pilot a single project — create one project with a unique key, build the hierarchy, confirm the budget, run task materials, procurement, work acceptance, and financial reporting.
  4. Roll out to active projects — replicate the pilot configuration, train project teams, procurement staff, site administrators, and finance users, then migrate active budgets and contracts.
  5. Evaluate and refine — review dashboard KPIs, budget variance, outstanding AR, open POs, and acceptance compliance, then consolidate financial reports across projects or companies.
