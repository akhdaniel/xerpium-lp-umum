Manufacturing ERP Software: How Xerpium Connects BOM, Routing, and Work Orders in One System

Most manufacturers do not lose margin in one dramatic event. They lose it slowly — through a bill of materials that was updated in a spreadsheet but never on the shop floor, a work order closed without recording actual consumption, or a costing report that arrives three weeks after the production run ended.

By the time finance reconciles the numbers, the job is already shipped and invoiced. The variance becomes a lesson learned rather than a decision made.

An MRP module inside a connected ERP system changes the sequence. Instead of reconstructing what happened, production, inventory, and accounting work from the same records as the work order progresses. Common Challenges in Discrete and Process Manufacturing

Bills of Materials That Live in Too Many Places

A BOM is a shared document by nature. Engineering owns the specification, purchasing reads it to plan materials, production follows it on the line, and finance uses it to value output.

When those four groups each keep a copy, revisions drift. For example, engineering substitutes a component to resolve a supply issue, but purchasing continues ordering the original part for another two cycles.

Routing That Exists Only as Tribal Knowledge

Many plants run on routings that were never formally documented. Supervisors know which work centre handles which operation and roughly how long each step takes.

That works until the supervisor is on leave, volume doubles, or a new product line is introduced. Without recorded routing, capacity planning becomes guesswork and quoting becomes optimistic.

Production Costs Calculated After the Fact

If material consumption, labour time, and overhead are only assembled at month end, cost visibility lags production by weeks. Managers end up reviewing jobs they can no longer influence.

Disconnected Entities in a Group Structure

Manufacturers operating two plants under different legal entities, or selling into export markets, face an extra layer. Intercompany transfers, separate books, and transactions in more than one currency do not consolidate cleanly in isolated systems.

Inside the Xerpium Manufacturing (MRP) Module

Bill of Materials

The BOM defines the components and quantities required to produce a finished item. Holding it in Xerpium means it is the same reference used for material planning, work order issuance, and output valuation — not four separate copies.

Routing

Routing defines the sequence of operations and the work centres involved. It provides the structure for scheduling work orders and understanding how a product physically moves through the plant.

Work Orders

Work orders turn a production plan into executable instructions. They track what is being made, in what quantity, and against which BOM and routing, giving production and inventory a shared record of what is in progress.

Production Cost Calculation

Xerpium calculates production costs as part of the module rather than as a separate reconciliation exercise. Because the costing draws on the same BOM, work order, and routing data, cost figures stay aligned with physical activity.

A Short Example Flow

Consider a contract manufacturer producing two product variants across one plant.

Engineering maintains the BOM for each variant in Xerpium. Routing defines the operation sequence through the relevant work centres.

When an order is confirmed, a work order is raised against the correct BOM and routing. As production proceeds, material consumption is recorded against the work order rather than reconstructed later.

The production cost calculation draws on that recorded activity. The plant manager reviews current status on the dashboard during the run, and finance sees costing aligned with the same records — no parallel spreadsheet to reconcile.
