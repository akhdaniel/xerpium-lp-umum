10 Best ERP Vendors for Hotel & Property Businesses: A 2026 Selection Guide

Hotels and property operators run on timing. A late check-in, an uncleaned room, a minibar charge that never reaches the folio, or an OTA booking that arrives after the room is already sold — each one erodes margin and guest trust. Most of these failures are not staffing problems; they are data problems. When reservations live in one system, housekeeping in a spreadsheet, and billing in accounting software, nobody sees the full picture in real time.

An ERP system solves this by putting reservations, rooms, folios, services, housekeeping, maintenance, and accounting on one database. But the software is only half the equation. The vendor you choose determines whether the system matches how your property actually operates, how fast it goes live, and whether anyone picks up the phone when your front desk has an issue at 11 PM. Below is a practical look at vendors worth shortlisting for the hotel and property sector.

What Is an ERP Vendor?

An ERP vendor is the company that develops, licenses, implements, and supports the enterprise resource planning software your business runs on. The vendor's role goes beyond shipping code: it covers requirement analysis, configuration, data migration, user training, integration with existing tools, and ongoing maintenance.

For hotels, vendor fit matters more than raw feature count. A generic ERP can handle purchasing and accounting, but it may have no concept of a room status, a rate plan, or a folio. The right vendor brings hospitality-specific modules plus the financial backbone — and knows how to connect the two.

2. SAP

A long-established enterprise ERP vendor with deep financial, procurement, and asset management capabilities. Commonly chosen by large hotel groups and property portfolios with complex multi-entity consolidation needs. Strengths include financial controls, group reporting, and a broad partner ecosystem.

3. Oracle

Oracle offers enterprise ERP alongside a well-known hospitality software portfolio. Typically considered by large chains that want a single vendor relationship across property operations and corporate finance. Strengths include database scale, revenue management depth, and global support coverage.

4. Microsoft Dynamics 365

A modular business applications suite spanning finance, operations, sales, and customer service, with tight integration into Microsoft 365 and Power Platform. Often selected by property companies already standardized on Microsoft tooling. Strengths include familiar user interfaces and low-code extension options.

5. Odoo

6. Sage

Focused primarily on accounting and financial management, with mid-market ERP offerings. Suited to property businesses whose priority is financial rigour and compliance reporting rather than front-office operations. Strengths include accounting depth and established market presence.

7. Acumatica

A cloud ERP vendor with a consumption-based licensing model and modules for financials, distribution, project accounting, and construction. Property developers and management companies often evaluate it for project-based work. Strengths include cloud-native architecture and project costing.

8. ERPNext

An open-source ERP covering accounting, inventory, HR, and projects. Appeals to organizations with internal technical capability that prefer full source access. Strengths include transparency, community support, and no vendor lock-in on the code base.

9. Infor

10. Epicor

An ERP vendor historically strong in manufacturing and distribution, with growing cloud offerings. Relevant to property groups that operate supporting production, central kitchens, or supply operations. Strengths include supply chain and operations management.

Factors to Consider When Choosing an ERP Vendor

Business Needs

Map your actual workflows first. A single boutique hotel needs fast check-in and clean folios; a multi-tower property group needs floor and building structures, multi-room reservations, and consolidated reporting.

Budget

Account for licensing, implementation, data migration, training, and ongoing support — not just the subscription line. A lower list price with a long implementation often costs more in total.

Features

Verify the specifics: does the system handle rate plans by channel, auto-post room charges at check-in, and block cancellation after check-in? Generic "hospitality support" claims are not enough.

Ease of Use

Front office and housekeeping staff turn over. If a receptionist cannot complete check-in without a manual, the system will be bypassed. Look for wizards and clear status indicators.

Scalability

Confirm the system supports additional properties, floors, room types, and rate plans without re-implementation.

Implementation Support

Ask who runs the project, how long it takes, and what happens after go-live. Configuration-driven setups generally deploy faster than code-heavy customization.

Vendor Reputation

Review references in hospitality or property specifically. Ask about support response times during peak season.

Tips for Choosing the Right ERP Vendor

Run a Scripted Demo

Give each vendor the same scenario: an OTA booking for two rooms, check-in, a laundry charge posted to folio, early check-out, and invoice generation. Watch the clicks.

Start with One Property

Pilot on a single property, validate the folio-to-invoice flow and housekeeping progress tracking, then roll out to the portfolio.

Check the Accounting Link

Confirm folios generate invoices automatically with per-line tax, and that payment status (paid, partial, unpaid) is tracked without re-entry.

Validate Inventory Integration

Minibar and F&B consumption should deduct stock; housekeeping supplies should be trackable. Ask to see it work.

Prioritize Time-to-Value

A system live in weeks starts protecting revenue immediately. Long projects risk scope drift and stakeholder fatigue.

FAQ

Is an ERP different from a PMS?

A PMS manages front-office operations — reservations, rooms, folios. An ERP covers the wider business including finance, inventory, and purchasing. Xerpium delivers the PMS functions inside the ERP, so both share one database.

Can one system handle multiple OTA channels?

Xerpium tracks reservations by channel including Direct, Booking.com, Expedia, Traveloka, Tiket.com, and Agoda, with separate OTA rate plans.

How are guest charges consolidated?

Every charge — room, F&B, laundry, spa, transport, minibar — posts to the guest's active folio as a line, then flows into a single invoice at check-out.

Does the system track housekeeping in real time?

Yes. Housekeeping schedules run by shift with task assignments per room, status flow from Pending to Inspected, and computed progress percentage visible on the dashboard.

What happens when a room needs repair?

Confirming a maintenance request automatically sets the room status to Maintenance, and completing it releases the room back to Available.

Is open-source ERP suitable for hotels?

It can be, provided the hospitality workflows are built properly. Xerpium's Odoo foundation gives flexibility while the hotel module supplies the ready-made PMS logic.
