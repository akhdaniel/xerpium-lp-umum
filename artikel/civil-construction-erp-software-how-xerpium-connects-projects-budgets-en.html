Civil Construction ERP Software: How Xerpium Connects Projects, Budgets, and Procurement in One System

Civil construction companies rarely fail because of engineering. They fail because of visibility. A road package, a drainage system, or a high-rise foundation can be technically sound while the commercial side quietly erodes — unrecorded material consumption, unbilled milestones, retention that nobody tracks, and purchase orders that never reconcile against the budget line they were meant to serve.

The root cause is usually structural. Project schedules live in one tool, Bill of Quantity in spreadsheets, procurement in email threads, and financial reporting in an accounting package that only sees the result three weeks after the fact. By the time the numbers arrive, the decision window has closed.

Xerpium, an Odoo-based ERP suite for construction and contracting businesses, addresses this by treating the project as the central record — and attaching budget, material, procurement, acceptance, and reporting directly to it.

Common Challenges in Civil Construction Operations

  • Fragmented work breakdown. Multi-package civil works need a parent-child project structure, but most systems only offer flat task lists — so sub-project progress never rolls up to the parent.
  • Budget drift with no early warning. Planned versus practical cost is reconciled monthly, long after a purchase order has already committed the spend.
  • BOQ disconnected from procurement. Quantities are estimated in a spreadsheet, then re-keyed into purchase requests, producing over-ordering and site shortages at the same time.
  • Informal work acceptance. Subcontractor progress and government milestone sign-offs are approved verbally or by email, then disputed at invoice time because accepted quantity and billed quantity do not match.
  • Slow financial close. Consolidating multiple project entities and producing a trial balance takes days, making mid-project corrective action impractical.

Relevant Xerpium Modules for Civil Construction

Project Structure: A WBS-Like Hierarchy

Xerpium supports parent-child project hierarchy, functioning as a work breakdown structure. Each parent tracks its sub-project count and can apply parent completion blocking, preventing a package from closing while dependent sub-projects remain open.

Every project carries a short unique key — for example [JKT-TOWER] — auto-generated from the project name, so site teams, procurement, and finance all reference the same identifier. Version tracking records revisions per project, which matters when a design change alters scope mid-execution. Custom statuses are fully configurable (name, sequence, is_closed, fold), and projects can be classified by hierarchical project types, assigned a department, and given auto-sequence numbering.

Budget and Financial Control

Project Budget in Xerpium is a crossovered budget per project, comparing planned versus practical figures. The budget follows a controlled workflow — Draft → Confirm → Validate → Done — so numbers are not altered informally.

Budgets are allocated per analytic account, giving genuine cost center tracking across packages. A smart button on each project exposes linked PO count, PO total, and purchase invoices, so commitment against budget is visible without leaving the project record. Provisions and reimbursement cost tracking covers advances and site expense recovery.

Material and Procurement: From BOQ to Purchase Request

This is where civil construction gains the most. Task Material records material consumed per task by product_id and quantity. Task Stock captures full stock moves originating from a task — source and destination location, picking, and scrap — moving through states of Pending → Confirmed → Assigned → Done → Cancelled.

BOQ Integration converts Bill of Quantity from the WBS activity into a procurement list, so ordering is driven by scope rather than guesswork. Product Sets act as pre-configured kits for repeatable material planning. Auto-Procure chains task material into a stock request and then into a purchase request automatically, while Analytic Lines allocate cost from material consumption back to the correct account.

BOQ-Based Procurement adds requisition of material from the WBS with quantity control and vendor comparison.

Work Acceptance: Formal Sign-Off Before Billing

The Work Acceptance (WA) model runs Draft → Accept → Cancel as a formal sign-off document. Each WA links to a purchase order line with quantity and price, and tracks qty_accepted versus qty_to_accept per line. Validation enforces that invoice quantity must match accepted quantity.

It is configurable: organisations can require a WA before goods receipt and before vendor billing. Typical use cases are government milestone sign-off, subcontractor work approval, and material receipt verification.

Scheduling, Roles, and Stakeholders

Forecast handles resource and role forecasting with employee capacity and leave handling, and can be linked to sales. Role Management provides hierarchical roles per project with user-project-role assignment. Stakeholder tracking covers external partners with role and contact details. Milestone Status measures execution percentage (hours allocated versus done) and dedication percentage. Task Features include parent blocking, automatic parent due dates, stage management, code sequence, and notes.

Client Transparency Through the Portal

A client portal lets owners and principals view project progress directly, with Update Portal pushing project status updates to the client — reducing the reporting burden on project managers.

Financial Reporting and Dashboard Visibility

Xerpium's accounting layer ships with a construction chart of accounts of 179 accounts and a trial balance across 32 account groups with 6-digit codes. Standard outputs include the Balance Sheet (current assets, fixed assets such as heavy equipment, liabilities, equity) and Profit & Loss (construction revenue, COGS, project cost, net profit). Tax handling covers PPN 11%, construction final income tax (PPh final), and tax retention. Turbo Report produces reports in under a minute with multi-company consolidation.

The dashboard delivers 14 KPI widgets, including Total Contract Revenue, Total Contracts, Active Projects, Outstanding AR, Total Material Cost, Open Tasks, Total Clients, and Open POs — plus a 12-month revenue trend area chart, a 12-month material cost trend line chart, and Top Clients and Top Suppliers bar charts with lists.

Business Benefits

  • BOQ quantities drive procurement directly, reducing re-keying errors and over-ordering.
  • Work Acceptance prevents vendor invoices from exceeding verified progress.
  • Analytic allocation gives true cost per package and per cost center.
  • Parent-child hierarchy makes multi-package civil works roll up cleanly.
  • Turbo Report shortens the close cycle and supports multi-company consolidation.
  • The client portal reduces manual progress reporting.

Example Flow: A Multi-Package Civil Works Contract

  1. A contract is recorded in Sales with milestone billing; a parent project [JKT-TOWER] is created with sub-projects per package.
  2. Project Budget is drafted per analytic account and moved through Confirm and Validate.
  3. BOQ from the WBS activity generates a procurement list; quantities are controlled and vendors compared.
  4. Task Material on an earthworks task triggers Auto-Procure — stock request, then purchase request, then PO.
  5. Subcontractor progress is submitted; a Work Acceptance document is accepted, recording qty_accepted.
  6. Goods receipt and vendor billing proceed only against the accepted quantity.
  7. Task Stock moves post analytic lines; the project smart button shows PO count, total, and invoices against budget.
  8. Finance closes with Turbo Report; the dashboard reflects updated material cost and outstanding AR.

Conclusion

Civil construction margin is won in the gap between what was budgeted, what was ordered, what was consumed, and what was accepted. Xerpium closes that gap by binding all four to a single project hierarchy, with Work Acceptance as the control point before money leaves the business.
