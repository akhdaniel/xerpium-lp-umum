Complete ERP solution for Aerospace Manufacturing: End-to-End Configuration, Quality, and Traceability Control with Xerpium ERP

October 19, 2026

Aerospace manufacturing operates under a different set of rules. Every part, revision, serial number, work center, and material batch must be controlled, traceable, and audit-ready. As-designed, as-built, and as-maintained configurations must stay aligned. Engineering changes affect BOMs. Non-conformances trigger corrective actions. First article inspections must comply with AS9102. Supplier certifications and tool calibrations cannot expire unnoticed.

When engineering, production, quality, and finance run on disconnected systems or spreadsheets, these requirements become a daily operational burden. Teams lose time reconciling part revisions, proving material traceability, and preparing for audits. Xerpium ERP replaces that fragmented approach with a single aerospace manufacturing platform that connects configuration management, production execution, quality, supply chain, and finance.

Common Challenges in Aerospace Manufacturing

  • Configuration and revision control across as-designed, as-built, and as-maintained states, with engineering change orders directly linked to BOM revisions and affected part numbers.
  • Full material traceability from raw material batch, heat treatment number, and material certificate through to finished serialized parts.
  • Quality and compliance pressure from non-conformance reports, CAPA, first article inspection, inspection plans, and certificates of conformance.
  • Supplier and work center qualification including AS9100D certifications, supplier tiering, tool calibration, and certified operator assignment.
  • Financial visibility that reflects aerospace-specific cost structures such as WIP, direct labor, factory overhead, engineering, quality, and certification expenses.

Xerpium Solution Coverage

Engineering and configuration control

Xerpium manages aircraft type records with unique codes, manufacturer, category, MTOW, range, engines, seats, and certification standard. Each configuration can be linked to a BOM and tracked as as-designed, as-built, or as-maintained. Part numbers include part type, alternates, drawing number and revision, material specification, ITAR/EAR control, and serial or batch tracking.

Engineering change orders follow a structured workflow from Draft through Under Review, Approved, Implemented, and Closed. Each ECO supports multi-approver sign-off with reviewer and approver roles, timestamps, and approve or reject actions. ECOs link directly to the BOM for revision tracking and to affected part numbers.

Manufacturing execution

Manufacturing orders extend standard production orders with aircraft type, serial number, configuration, customer, and priority such as AOG, Critical, High, Normal, or Low. Each MO can require quality release and sign-off. Work orders capture operator assignment, special instructions, tool tracking, and digital sign-off with timestamp.

Xerpium provides sample work centers for aerospace operations such as Assembly Line 1, Machining 1, Testing Station 1, Paint Shop 1, Quality Inspection 1, and Tooling 1. Each work center includes center type, required certifications, capacity by aircraft type, calibration due dates, and environmental controls. Dynamic stages allow serial numbers and other records to move through configurable lifecycle states such as Draft, Available, Installed, In Repair, and On Hold.

Quality and compliance

Material traceability links batch numbers, heat treatment numbers, material certificates, supplier certificates, chemical composition, and test reports directly to serial numbers. This creates a full audit trail from raw material to finished part.

Non-conformance reports capture auto-numbered NCRs, severity level, root cause, disposition, affected part and serial number, and quantity. Dispositions include use-as-is, rework, repair, scrap, and return-to-supplier. CAPA records manage corrective action plans, assigned owners, due dates, effectiveness verification, and preventive action monitoring. CAPAs can originate directly from NCR findings.

First article inspection follows AS9102 requirements with full, partial, or delta FAI. Inspection lines record nominal, minimum, maximum, and actual values with automatic pass/fail results. Part numbers, serial numbers, and drawing revisions are linked for complete FAI documentation. Inspection plans cover incoming, in-process, and final inspection with points, methods, specifications, and frequency. Quality release records support certificate of conformance workflows from Draft, Pending, Released, and On Hold, with authorized signatory and linked manufacturing order.

Supply chain and work center management

Suppliers are managed by tier, AS9100D certification, quality rating, on-time delivery rate, approved parts, qualification status, and last audit date. Tooling records include unique tool numbers, type, calibration interval, last and next calibration dates, status, and owner. Work order tool tracking assigns specific tools to each work order for complete operational control.

Finance and reporting

Xerpium includes an aerospace-specific chart of accounts covering raw materials, direct labor, factory overhead, WIP, product sales, MRO services, and engineering revenue. Balance sheet accounts include current inventory and WIP as well as fixed assets such as machinery and measuring instruments. The P&L structure includes revenue, COGS, engineering expense, quality, and certification.

KPI dashboards show total manufacturing orders by state, production efficiency, work center downtime trends, open NCRs, and FAI pass/fail rates. The AI assistant helps teams search part numbers, check manufacturing order status, and generate reports.

End-to-End Flow

A representative cycle starts in engineering with an aircraft type and configuration. The BOM contains controlled part numbers with drawing revisions and serial tracking. When an engineering change is needed, an ECO is drafted and routed through review and approval. Once approved, the BOM revision updates and affected part numbers are visible to production.

A manufacturing order is then created from the BOM for a specific aircraft type, configuration, and serial number. The MO carries the customer, priority, and quality release requirement. Work orders assign certified operators and required tools. Material consumed in production is traced by batch and heat number to the serial number being built.

Quality performs incoming, in-process, and final inspections according to the inspection plan. If a non-conformance is found, an NCR is raised with disposition and root cause. A CAPA follows with corrective and preventive actions. For first article requirements, an AS9102-compliant FAI is completed with pass/fail results. After successful sign-off, the quality release is issued. Accounting automatically records WIP and COGS journals, and the KPI dashboard reflects MO status, efficiency, downtime, open NCRs, and FAI results.

  2. Configure aerospace parameters — set up the aerospace chart of accounts, auto-numbering sequences, dynamic stages, ECO approval workflows, quality release requirements, and inspection plans.
  3. Run a pilot — select one aircraft type or product family, create manufacturing and work orders, execute an ECO, complete material traceability, and process at least one NCR, CAPA, FAI, and quality release.
  4. Roll out across the operation — expand to additional aircraft types and configurations, integrate inventory, purchasing, and HR records, and train operators, quality signatories, and planners.
