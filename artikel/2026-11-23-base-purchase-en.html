Common Procurement Challenges in Growing Companies

Requests That Bypass Approval

When purchase requests travel by chat or email, there is no consistent gate. A department head approves verbally, the order goes out, and the commitment only becomes visible when the invoice lands. Budget owners then discover spend they never sanctioned.

Quotation Comparisons Without a Record

Teams often collect vendor quotations informally, pick one, and discard the rest. Six months later, nobody can explain why a particular supplier was chosen or what the alternatives offered. That gap matters during audits and during renegotiation.

Invoices That Do Not Match Reality

The most common leak is the three-way gap: the purchase order says one quantity, the goods receipt says another, and the vendor invoice says a third. Without a systematic check, accounts payable either delays everything for manual verification or pays amounts that were never properly received.

Multi-Entity Complexity

Companies operating several legal entities or transacting in more than one currency face an additional layer. Consolidating spend across entities becomes a manual exercise, and currency differences get reconciled late — or not at all.

How the Purchase & Procurement Application in Xerpium Works

Xerpium's Purchase & Procurement module is designed as a continuous flow rather than a set of disconnected screens. Each stage produces the data the next stage needs.

Purchase Requests (PR)

The flow begins with a purchase request raised by the requesting department. The PR captures what is needed and routes it for approval before any commitment to a vendor exists. This establishes a clear control point at the earliest stage, where changes are cheapest.

Requests for Quotation (RFQ)

Approved requests become RFQs sent to candidate vendors. Because quotations are recorded in the system rather than in individual inboxes, comparisons stay visible and reusable. For example, a procurement officer sourcing packaging materials can review all submitted quotations side by side within the same record, and that comparison remains available for future sourcing rounds.

Purchase Orders (PO)

Once a vendor is selected, the RFQ converts into a purchase order. The PO becomes the formal commitment and the reference document for receiving and invoicing. Nothing is re-keyed, so the quantities, prices, and terms carry forward exactly as agreed.

Vendor Management

Supplier records sit inside the same module, holding the commercial relationship in one place. Over time, this becomes the procurement team's institutional memory — which vendors were used, under what terms, and for which categories.

3-Way Matching

This is the control that protects cash. Xerpium matches the purchase order, the goods receipt, and the vendor invoice before the invoice is cleared for payment. Discrepancies surface at the point of matching rather than during a quarterly review.

A Short Example Flow

Consider a manufacturing company that needs replacement components for a production line.

The maintenance supervisor raises a purchase request in Xerpium. It routes to the plant manager for approval. Once approved, procurement issues RFQs to three qualified vendors, all recorded against the same request.

The team compares the responses and converts the selected quotation into a purchase order. When the components arrive, the goods receipt is logged. The vendor invoice is then matched against the PO and the receipt. Because one line item arrived short, the mismatch is flagged before payment is released — and the vendor is notified while the delivery is still recent enough to resolve easily.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is 3-way matching and why does it matter?

Three-way matching compares the purchase order, the goods receipt, and the vendor invoice before an invoice is approved for payment. It catches quantity and pricing discrepancies at the point they occur. In Xerpium, this check is built into the Purchase & Procurement module rather than performed manually by accounts payable.

How do I evaluate the total cost of a procurement system?

Look beyond the license or subscription figure. Consider implementation effort, data migration from existing spreadsheets, user training, and the cost of integrating with modules you already run. It can. The value of structured PR, RFQ, and PO flows comes less from vendor count and more from having a consistent approval record and reliable invoice matching. A company with ten vendors still benefits from an audit trail and real-time visibility into open commitments.

What reporting is available without custom development?
