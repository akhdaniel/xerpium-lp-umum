# Garment Manufacturing with Xerpium ERP: Controlling BOM, Production, and Cost per Style

Garment manufacturers operate in an environment where profitability depends on controlling fabric waste, direct labor, trim costs, subcontracted processes, and quality consistency. A single style may involve a multi-level bill of material, multiple routing operations, buyer-specific quality standards, and complex tax treatment. Generic ERP systems often struggle because they treat garment production as simple assembly and lack the granular accounts, WIP tracking, and shop-floor controls that apparel businesses need.

Xerpium ERP addresses this with a dedicated garment framework: a 6-digit garment chart of accounts, BOM per style, manufacturing orders, cutting and sewing control, QC checkpoints, and integrated production, quality, and financial dashboards.

Common Challenges in Garment Manufacturing

  • Complex multi-level BOM structures per style, with fabric, trim, thread, labels, packaging, and style-specific scrap allowances.
  • Limited real-time visibility into WIP as bundles move from cutting to sewing to finishing.
  • Difficulty tracking fabric usage and cutting waste, making cost per unit inaccurate.
  • Inconsistent quality control without AQL sampling, structured defect categories, and repair order management.
  • Manual accounting that cannot cleanly separate raw material, WIP, finished goods, and production expense accounts, hiding gross margin per style.

Garment-Specific Chart of Accounts and Production Costing

Xerpium uses a dedicated 6-digit garment COA structure with the prefix ga_XXXXX. Key accounts include:

  • Receivable: ga_120100
  • Payable: ga_210100
  • Inventory: ga_130100–ga_130800
  • Revenue: ga_430100
  • Expense: ga_510100
  • PPN Garment 11%: tax_sale_11 and tax_purchase_11

Inventory accounts are separated into raw material (ga_130100), WIP (ga_130200), finished goods (ga_130300), and consumable materials such as trim (ga_130600). Production cost accounts include direct raw material cost (ga_510200), direct labor (ga_510300), factory overhead (ga_510400), trim cost (ga_510500), makloon cost (ga_510600), and subcontract cost (ga_510700).

Other garment-specific accounts cover freight (ga_520200), sales commission (ga_520300), sales discount (ga_430200), sales return (ga_430300), input VAT (ga_150200), and output VAT (ga_250200). Asset accounts track sewing machines, overlock machines, and cutting equipment under ga_170100, with accumulated depreciation under ga_170200.

Because manufacturing orders can generate production journals automatically, garment businesses can see accurate cost per style without manual reclassification.

BOM per Style, Routing, and Cost Roll-Up

Xerpium supports multi-level garment BOMs per style. BOM components include fabric, trim, thread, labels, and packaging. Each style also has routing operations: cutting, sewing, finishing, and packing. The system supports cost roll-up to calculate cost per garment unit and allows a scrap percentage as an allowance for fabric waste per style.

This combination gives production planning a reliable basis for material requirements, purchasing, and margin analysis.

Production Orders, Cutting, Sewing, and WIP Tracking

Garment production runs through manufacturing orders designed for apparel. Xerpium supports lot sizing per cutting batch and component release from inventory into production. WIP is tracked in real time, and labor hours can be recorded per operation.

On the shop floor, cutting and sewing controls include:

  • Cutting plan with marker plan per lay of fabric
  • Fabric usage tracking per cutting
  • Sewing line allocation per style
  • Bundle tracking from cutting through finishing
  • Defect tracking per bundle or operation

This level of tracking helps manufacturers trace exactly where a style is in production and where material or labor variance occurs.

Quality Control and Defect Management

Quality control in Xerpium is built around checkpoints per operation: cutting, sewing, and finishing. The system supports AQL sampling, with defect categories classified as minor, major, or critical and an action plan for each. Defect goods can be handled through repair orders, while QC pass rate is tracked per style, line, or operator.

Quality KPIs such as pass rate, defect rate, rework cost, and customer return percentage give management a clear view of quality performance.

Production, Financial, Quality, and Supply Chain KPIs

Xerpium includes integrated KPI dashboards for garment operations. Production KPIs include daily output, line efficiency percentage, machine downtime per shift, target vs actual, and SMV tracking.

Financial KPIs include cost per unit, gross margin per style, WIP value in rupiah, and inventory turn for raw materials and finished goods. Quality KPIs include pass rate, defect rate, rework cost, and customer return percentage. Supply chain KPIs include procurement lead time for raw materials, fabric stock per type, OTIF delivery to buyers, and supplier scores for fabric and trim suppliers.

Dashboards include widgets for production, quality, and finance, with drill-down to source documents for faster investigation.

Cross-Module Integration for Garment Operations

Xerpium connects garment operations across core modules:

  • Accounting: Garment-specific COA, separate WIP, raw material, and finished goods accounts, and automatic production journals from manufacturing orders.
  • Inventory: Raw material tracking per fabric type, WIP tracking in stock locations, and finished goods received from production into finished goods inventory.
  • Manufacturing: Multi-level BOM per style, routing for cutting, sewing, and finishing, and support for subcontract and makloon outsourcing.
  • Purchase: Purchase orders for raw materials generated from BOM requirements, purchase orders for trim and accessories, and supplier management for fabric and trim vendors.
  • HR: Operator working hour recording, payroll based on output per sewing line, and attendance per production shift.

Business Benefits

  • More accurate cost per unit and gross margin per style through garment-specific accounts and automatic production journals.
  • Better material control with scrap allowances, fabric usage tracking per cutting, and raw material inventory by fabric type.
  • Real-time WIP visibility and bundle tracking reduce lost or stalled production orders.
  • Consistent quality management using AQL sampling, defect categories, repair orders, and pass rates.
  • Faster purchasing decisions driven by BOM requirements and supplier performance data.
  • Management visibility through integrated production, quality, finance, and supply chain KPIs.

Example Workflow: From BOM to Finished Goods

A garment manufacturer receives a buyer order for a new style. The production team creates a multi-level BOM with fabric, trim, thread, labels, and packaging, and defines routing operations for cutting, sewing, finishing, and packing. The system performs a cost roll-up and suggests purchase orders for raw materials and trim based on BOM requirements.

After materials arrive, raw material inventory is recorded by fabric type. The cutting team creates a marker plan per lay, tracks fabric usage per cutting, and releases components to production. Bundles move from cutting through the sewing line to finishing and packing, with real-time WIP tracking.

QC checkpoints are performed at cutting, sewing, and finishing using AQL sampling. Defects are categorized as minor, major, or critical, repair orders are issued where needed, and pass rates are tracked per operator and style. Labor hours are recorded per operation, and the manufacturing order automatically generates journal entries to raw material, WIP, finished goods, and production expense accounts.

At the end of the process, finished garments are moved into finished goods inventory, and the dashboard shows actual output versus target, line efficiency, cost per unit, gross margin, WIP value, and OTIF performance.
