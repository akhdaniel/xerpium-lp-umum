10 Best ERP Solutions for Electronic OEM Manufacturing Operations

October 21, 2026

Electronic OEM manufacturing operations combine version-controlled bills of materials, customer-specific specifications, export documentation, duty exemption facilities, inbound inspection, lot traceability, and OEM-specific financial structures. When product data, trade documents, production yields, and supplier certificates live in disconnected spreadsheets, teams lose time reconciling HS codes, PEB filings, KITE ratios, and landed costs. A mismatch between the customer PO, BOM version, or export invoice creates customs delays, scrap write-offs, and compliance exposure. The ten solutions below address these workflows as one connected OEM operating system.

The 10 Solutions at a Glance

  1. Product Model & BOM — version-controlled product data and customer specifications to prevent build errors.
  2. Customer & Supplier — audit-ready partner master data, supplier ratings, and certificate expiry tracking.
  3. Export Operations — connected DO, packing list, PEB, CoO, and invoice with CEISA integration.
  4. Trade & Finance — KITE duty-exemption ratio monitoring, LC/TT tracking, and landed cost calculation.
  5. Manufacturing Orders — customer-PO-linked production orders with yield, downtime, and work center control.
  6. Quality Control — AQL inspection, lot traceability, customer complaints, and supplier certificate tracking.
  7. Import & Supply Chain — import PO, inbound AQL inspection, supplier ratings, and landed cost visibility.
  8. Accounting & COA — 168-account OEM chart of accounts, tax handling, and OEM-specific COGS/expense structure.
  9. KPI Dashboard — revenue, open POs, active BOMs, work centers, and top-customer trend views.
  10. AI Assistant Tools — natural-language lookup for product model, supplier, customer PO, HS code, and KITE ratio.

1. Product Model & BOM

Product Model & BOM supports OEM product data that stays linked to customer requirements. Each product model stores model code, customer part number, HS code, FOB price in USD, weight, volume, and linked product. BOM versions are controlled as draft, approved, or obsolete and are unique per model, reducing the risk of building against an outdated revision. BOM lines capture component, quantity, scrap rate percentage, supplier, and notes, while customer specifications attach per BOM version. This gives engineering, purchasing, and production a single source for cost, compliance, and assembly requirements.

2. Customer & Supplier

Customer & Supplier centralizes OEM trading partner data for both sell-side and buy-side operations. OEM customers can be classified as Korean HQ, Korean subsidiary, local, or export, with credit limit, incoterm, and port of loading/discharge. Supplier records cover Korean, local, Chinese, and Singapore partners with supplier code, partner type, and payment term. Supplier ratings score quality, delivery, and price with an average total, while supplier certificates track ISO 9001, ISO 14001, RoHS, REACH, UL, and CE expiry dates. Partner extension fields include OEM flags, parent corporation country, bank account, and SWIFT.

3. Export Operations

Export Operations connects the export delivery order, packing list, PEB, certificate of origin, and commercial invoice. Export delivery orders record container, vessel, port of loading/discharge, and incoterm. PEB documents capture the DJBC registration number and support CEISA integration for submission. Packing lists include total packages, gross/net weight, and volume per product model. Certificates of origin cover Form E for ASEAN-China, Form AK for ASEAN-Korea, and non-ASEAN requirements. Export invoices issue in USD at FOB value with incoterm. This helps electronic OEMs reduce documentation errors and customs clearance delays.

4. Trade & Finance

Trade & Finance handles trade compliance and cost structures specific to export-oriented electronic OEMs. KITE facility tracking manages duty exemption with a target ratio of 1.5 and periodic realization, comparing import value against export value and actual ratio against target. Letter of credit records support sight and usance, import and export, with issuance, expiry, and shipping date fields. TT/remittance tracking covers outgoing and incoming payments, bank references, and status. Landed cost calculation combines FOB, freight, insurance, duty, tax, and handling, giving finance and logistics a consistent view of true product cost.

5. Manufacturing Orders

Manufacturing Orders links production directly to customer demand. Each manufacturing order extension carries the customer PO ID and line, product model, FOB price in USD, and target ship date. Work centers include SMT, assembly, injection molding, calibration, QC inspection, and packing. Shift management controls start/end time and assignment per manufacturing order. Yield records capture input quantity, output quantity, scrap quantity, and yield percentage per order and shift. Downtime records track machine breakdown, setup change, material missing, quality issue, and power outage. Work order fields include cycle time target, scrap budget percentage, and sign-off.

6. Quality Control

Quality Control brings AQL-based inspection and traceability into the OEM process. Inbound AQL inspection supports sampling levels 1.0, 2.5, and 4.0 with sample size and accept/reject results. Quality check records extend from xerpium_mrp_quality with AQL level, sample size, and reject count. Lot traceability links component lots to finished lots, manufacturing orders, and customer POs, enabling fast containment when a defect appears. Customer complaint records classify defects as cosmetic, functional, dimension, missing, or packaging, and capture root cause, corrective action, and status. Supplier certificates for ISO, RoHS, REACH, and CE remain visible with expiry tracking.

7. Import & Supply Chain

Import & Supply Chain supports inbound material control for OEM production. Import purchase orders record supplier PO details in USD, incoterm, port, ETD/ETA, and LC number. Inbound inspection applies AQL sampling at goods receipt and captures pass/fail results before material enters inventory. Supplier ratings assess quality, delivery, and price on a 1-5 scale and calculate an average total score, so procurement can qualify vendors consistently. Landed cost calculation includes FOB, freight, insurance, duty, tax, and handling. Supplier certificate management tracks ISO, RoHS, REACH, UL, and CE expiry dates.

8. Accounting & COA

Accounting & COA provides an OEM-specific general ledger rather than forcing electronic manufacturers into a generic chart of accounts. The 168-account structure covers export revenue from thermostats and parts, local sales, scrap, and discounts. COGS tracks raw material, imported raw material, direct labor, overhead, and variance. Expense accounts include selling costs such as freight and commission, plus admin costs like salary, BPJS, depreciation for SMT, assembly, and calibration machines, and certification costs for ISO, RoHS, and CE. Tax handling includes PPN 11%/12%, PPh 21/23/25/26/Final, and corporate tax.

9. KPI Dashboard

KPI Dashboard gives management a live operational view without manual spreadsheet consolidation. The dashboard shows total revenue month-to-date from sale order amounts, open customer POs not yet shipped or cancelled, active product models, approved and active BOM versions, active work centers, registered OEM customers, registered OEM suppliers, and registered HS codes. A 12-month monthly revenue line chart and a top five customers bar chart by revenue provide trend analysis. This helps OEM managers spot revenue concentration, production workload, and master data gaps in one place.

10. AI Assistant Tools

AI Assistant Tools reduce time spent searching for operational data and export classifications. Electronic product model search finds records by code or name. Electronic supplier search filters OEM suppliers by name or country. Electronic customer PO search locates orders by reference or customer. Electronic suggest HS code recommends an HS code from a description. Electronic check KITE ratio checks duty exemption performance by facility number. Users can query these tools through natural language chat, allowing purchasing, export, and finance teams to retrieve product, supplier, PO, HS code, and KITE information without navigating multiple menus.

How to Choose the Right Solution

Before selecting or implementing these modules, electronic OEM manufacturers should evaluate their most urgent operational risks.

  • Export and duty complexity: If you use KITE facilities, CEISA PEB filing, or Form E/AK certificates, prioritize Export Operations and Trade & Finance together.
  • BOM and specification control: If customer part numbers, HS codes, or scrap rates change between revisions, Product Model & BOM should be the foundation before manufacturing and quality modules.
  • Traceability and supplier risk: If you receive RoHS/REACH-controlled components or face inbound AQL inspection, link Import & Supply Chain with Quality Control and lot traceability.
  • Financial reporting requirements: Choose Accounting & COA when local tax handling such as PPN 11%/12% and PPh 21/23/25/26 and OEM-specific COGS must be reported without manual reclassification.

Conclusion

Electronic OEM manufacturing operations require more than generic production or accounting software. The ten solutions above connect product models, BOM versions, OEM customers and suppliers, export documentation, KITE duty exemption, manufacturing orders, AQL quality, landed cost, OEM accounting, dashboards, and AI-assisted lookup. Teams can reduce manual reconciliation, improve export compliance, and maintain traceability from component lots to customer POs.
