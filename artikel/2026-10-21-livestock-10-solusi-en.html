10 Best ERP Solutions for Livestock Operations

October 21, 2026

Livestock operations combine biological variability, feed costs, health events, traceability demands, halal compliance, and margin pressure. When animal registry, pen assignments, feeding, weighing, health records, and batch costing live in separate spreadsheets, data drifts. Planners cannot see pen utilization or feed conversion, buyers cannot prove origin or halal chain, and finance cannot calculate actual cost per animal. ERP modules designed for livestock connect those records from intake to sale or slaughter. The following 10 solutions address the daily operational reality of cattle and other livestock operations.

The 10 Solutions at a Glance

  1. Livestock Animal Registry — Builds one traceable identity per animal from ear tag, RFID, or QR code to origin and purchase data.
  2. Pen Management — Manages pen type, capacity, utilization, location, and responsible employee to prevent overcrowding.
  3. Batch Management — Controls fattening periods, pen and animal assignments, KPIs, full costing, and cost per animal per batch.
  4. Feed Management — Tracks ingredients, TMR formulas, feed stock, daily feeding records, and dry matter.
  5. Weighing & ADG Tracking — Records routine weighing, computes average daily gain automatically, and plots growth curves.
  6. Health & Medical Records — Logs vaccination, treatment, illness, death, medicine, withdrawal period, and vaccination schedules.
  7. Pharmacy Stock — Tracks medicine stock per pen and warehouse, movements, expiry, low-stock flags, and automatic deduction.
  8. SOP Management — Creates daily checklists for feeding, health, hygiene, weighing, vaccination, biosecurity, and slaughter.
  9. Traceability & Halal Chain — Maintains append-only chain of custody with SHA-256 hash and halal slaughter records.
  10. HPP & Profitability Engine — Computes feed, medicine, labor, overhead, revenue, gross profit, and margin per batch.

1. Livestock Animal Registry

A disconnected animal list makes it hard to answer which animal came from where, when, and under what purchase terms. This module creates one digital identity per animal using ear tag, RFID code, or QR code, and records species and breed including Bali, Madura, PO, Limousin, Simmental, Brahman, Angus, FH, and others. It structures origin tracking by province, district, and source farm, stores photos and documents per animal, and captures purchase information such as supplier, PO, initial weight, and intake date. Operations teams can retrieve an animal record at any point instead of searching paper or spreadsheets.

2. Pen Management

Pen occupancy and responsibility are often tracked by memory or signage, leading to overcapacity or unclear accountability. This module manages pen types such as fattening, quarantine, isolation, and holding. It records capacity in number of animals, current count, and utilization percentage, along with location or block and area in square meters. Each pen can have a responsible employee from HR records, and batch assignment uses a many-to-many relationship to livestock batches. The result is a live view of available space, pen load, and responsibility for daily operations.

3. Batch Management

Without batch-level costing, operations cannot see whether a fattening cycle is profitable. Batch Management tracks the fattening period with start date, end date, and state from draft to active to done. It assigns pens and animals per batch, and monitors performance KPIs such as FCR, average ADG, total weight gain, and mortality rate. It accumulates full costing for feed, medicine, labor, overhead, and purchase, then computes cost of goods sold per animal and per kilogram of live weight automatically. This gives management a single profit and performance view per batch.

4. Feed Management

Feed cost control is critical for batch profitability, but ingredient use and dry matter often stay in notebooks. Feed Management organizes feed ingredients such as forage, concentrate, BIS, bran, tofu waste, molasses, and minerals. It supports TMR ration formulation with dry matter percentage and cost per kilogram. The module also tracks feed stock per pen with quantity on hand, records daily feeding per batch or pen by time such as morning, afternoon, and evening, and automatically tracks dry matter from ingredient or formula. This tightens cost control and ration consistency.

5. Weighing & ADG Tracking

Manual weight logs create duplicate entries and make growth trends hard to detect. This module records routine weighing per animal with date and method such as manual, RFID, or digital. It automatically computes average daily gain from the difference in weight divided by interval days. It also stores previous weight and interval days, validates that duplicate weighing on the same date is rejected, and presents an interactive growth curve as a line chart per animal. Producers can identify slow-growing animals early and adjust feed or health interventions.

6. Health & Medical Records

Health events affect animal welfare, withdrawal compliance, and mortality cost, but paper records are hard to audit. This module logs event types such as vaccination, treatment, illness, death, quarantine, routine check, and surgery. It tracks medicine product, dose, unit, cost, and withdrawal period. The system calculates the withdrawal date, the safe sale date after medicine administration. It also maintains a preventive vaccination schedule per species with cron reminders and supports photo documentation of animal condition before and after medical actions. This protects food safety and treatment compliance.

7. Pharmacy Stock

Medicine stock may sit in pens and a central warehouse without clear visibility, leading to expiry or shortages. Pharmacy Stock manages stock per pen plus central warehouse. It tracks receipt, issue, and adjustment movements for medicines, and marks low stock based on minimum stock levels with an automatic low-stock flag. Expiry dates are tracked per medicine batch. When a medical record is created, stock is deducted automatically. This keeps medicine inventory accurate and supports timely replenishment without manual counting.

8. SOP Management

Daily tasks such as feeding, vaccination, weighing, and cleaning depend on consistent execution, but checklists are often missing. SOP Management defines SOP types for feed, health, hygiene, weighing, vaccination, biosecurity, and slaughter. Each checklist item includes guidance, estimated time, and photo requirements. A daily cron automatically creates SOP execution records for every pen. Execution status moves from draft to in progress to done or skipped, and completion percentage is calculated automatically. This turns standard operating procedures into measurable, auditable daily work.

9. Traceability & Halal Chain

Buyers and auditors increasingly ask for proof of origin and halal compliance. Traceability & Halal Chain maintains an event log per animal from intake to active to sold or slaughter. Records are append-only with SHA-256 hashing and cannot be deleted after hashing. The hash chain with previous hash forms a blockchain-ready structure. Halal slaughter records include ante-mortem, bismillah, qiblat, and post-mortem checklists, and halal certificates can be verified through blockchain. This creates an immutable chain of custody and credible halal claims.

10. HPP & Profitability Engine

Operations may know revenue but not true margin per batch. This engine aggregates feed costs from feeding records, medicine costs from health events, labor assignments per batch, and overhead such as electricity, water, and pen depreciation. It captures total revenue from sold animals and calculates gross profit and margin percentage per batch automatically. The module also produces cost of goods sold per animal and per kilogram of live weight. Finance and operations can review actual profitability per batch without manual cost allocation.

How to Choose the Right Solution

Start with the operational data you cannot afford to lose: animal identity, movement, and health history. If you need halal or export traceability, choose a solution with immutable chain-of-custody and slaughter records early. If feed conversion and daily gain drive your margin, prioritize feed management and weighing modules that calculate FCR and ADG automatically. If you run multiple pens or locations, confirm that pen capacity, batch assignment, and cost allocation work together, and that the system can compute HPP per batch from actual feed, medicine, labor, and overhead rather than estimates. Avoid modules that duplicate data or require manual re-entry between registry, feeding, health, and accounting.

Conclusion

ERP selection for livestock operations should not be generic. The 10 solutions above cover animal identity, housing, batch performance, feed, growth, health, pharmacy, SOP, traceability, halal chain, and profitability.
