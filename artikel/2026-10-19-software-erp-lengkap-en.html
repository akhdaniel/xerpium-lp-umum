Complete ERP solution for Software House: Aligning Agile, Waterfall, Budgets, and Teams in One Platform

Software houses operate in a uniquely complex space. A single portfolio can include fixed-price waterfall projects, sprint-driven agile products, and hybrid engagements where a discovery phase runs through gates before development moves into Kanban or Scrum. When delivery teams use one tool for backlogs, finance uses another for budgets and invoices, HR uses a spreadsheet for allocation, and reporting is assembled manually, profitability and delivery risk become difficult to see until it is too late. Xerpium ERP addresses this by connecting the project delivery lifecycle with sales, timesheets, HR allocation, technology portfolio management, and accounting in one system.

Common Challenges in Software House

Software houses face a set of recurring operational frictions that generic ERP or separate project tools do not solve well:

  • Delivery teams often mix Scrum, Waterfall, Kanban, and hybrid approaches, but disconnected tools create inconsistent tracking and duplicated data.
  • Project profitability is hard to measure because actual cost, effort, and budget are maintained in different places.
  • Resource allocation is frequently planned in spreadsheets, making role conflicts, over-allocation, and capacity gaps difficult to detect.
  • Technology stack information is scattered across CVs, proposals, and documents, so portfolio capabilities are hard to present consistently to clients.
  • Finance and delivery are disconnected: timesheet data does not automatically drive cost recognition, and contract billing does not always map to project milestones or phases.

Xerpium Solution Coverage

Xerpium ERP gives a software house a connected project delivery and operations model. The relevant areas of the solution can be grouped into four major capability blocks.

Methodologies: Scrum, Waterfall, Kanban, and Hybrid

Xerpium supports multiple delivery methodologies in one project workspace. For Scrum teams, the system manages product backlog, user stories, story points, priorities, acceptance criteria, sprint planning, sprint goals, task breakdown, velocity tracking, and completion rate. User stories follow the format "As a... I want... So that..." and move through statuses from Backlog to In Sprint, In Progress, and Done. Sprint capacity is expressed in story points, and only one active sprint is validated per project. The sprint close wizard supports review and retrospective, while velocity history shows performance from sprint to sprint.

For Waterfall projects, Xerpium structures work into sequential project phases with start date, end date, milestone, deliverables, phase tasks, and gate approvals. Gate approval history records who approved a gate and when. The system validates that predecessor phases are completed before the next phase can start, using phase states such as To Do, In Progress, Review, and Done.

For Kanban and hybrid delivery, the solution provides a visual task management board within project tasks. Tasks can be typed as Feature, Bug, Technical Debt, or Spike. Story points can be estimated at task level, and tasks can be assigned to a specific sprint, linked to a backlog user story, or connected to a waterfall phase. Methodology is selected per project, so the team can choose the most appropriate model for each engagement.

Budget, Cost, and Profitability

Xerpium connects project delivery to financial control. Each project can use a contract type such as Fixed Price, Time & Material, or Milestone Billing. The project holds a monetary budget, estimated hours, and a link to the originating sale order. Actual hours are automatically computed from timesheet entries through account.analytic.line, and unit amounts are summed per project. Hourly cost is calculated using a configurable average hourly rate, with a default of Rp 150.000. Profitability is computed as (budget - actual cost) / budget × 100. Multi-currency is supported through the company currency setting. This means a project manager and finance team see the same budget, actual cost, and profitability data without manual spreadsheet consolidation.

Team Allocation and HR Integration

Resource planning is built on the existing employee master data from HR. For each project, the system supports employee allocation with a specific role, date range, hours per week, total hours, and notes. Supported roles include Project Manager, Business Analyst, Developer, QA Engineer, DevOps, and UI/UX Designer. Validation ensures end date is after start date and weekly hours are between 0.1 and 168. Total hours are computed from weeks multiplied by hours per week. Hourly rate per employee and capacity planning per role are also part of the model, enabling allocation decisions to be based on actual capacity and cost.

Technology Stack and Portfolio View

The suite includes a technology catalog with categories such as Frontend, Backend, Database, DevOps, Mobile, and Other. Each technology has a unique name and a color tag for visual coding. Technologies are mapped to projects through a many-to-many relationship, which enables filtering projects by technology stack, viewing stack diversity across all projects, and presenting a portfolio view of stack capabilities to clients. This turns internal technical knowledge into a reusable, client-ready asset.

User Story, Sprint, and Backlog Controls

Backlog management is integrated with delivery execution. User stories support acceptance criteria, story points, priority levels from Low to Critical, a tag system, and assignment to a developer. The state changes automatically when a sprint is selected. Sprint workflow runs from Planning to Active to Closed, with capacity planning based on available story points and a sprint goal to focus the team. Tasks can be linked back to the backlog or to a phase, creating traceability across hybrid engagements.

Connected Reporting and Cross-Module Flows

Xerpium’s reporting covers sprint velocity, project profitability, phase completion, and team allocation utilization. The connected flows between modules include:

  • Project and Accounting: budget tracking, timesheet to employee cost, profitability, and milestone billing integration.
  • Project and Timesheet: actual hours auto-computed, hourly cost calculation, and unit amount sum per project.
  • Project and Sales: project created from sale order, with contract type determining billing method. Fixed price supports single invoice, while milestone supports phased invoicing.
  • Project and HR: team allocation from employee records, role assignment, hourly rate per employee, and capacity planning per role.
  • Project and Technology: technology master data, project mapping, stack portfolio view, and project filtering by technology.

End-to-End Flow

A typical software house cycle in Xerpium might look like this. A client signs a sale order with milestone billing. The project is created from that sale order, inheriting the contract type and budget. The delivery manager selects a hybrid methodology: a waterfall discovery phase with gate approval, followed by Scrum sprints for build. The team defines product backlog user stories with story points, acceptance criteria, and priorities. Sprint planning allocates capacity, sets a sprint goal, and breaks stories into tasks. Developers, QA engineers, and other roles are allocated with date ranges and weekly hours. As work progresses, timesheets automatically feed actual hours and cost into the project. For waterfall phases, gates are approved with timestamps before the next phase can start. At sprint close, the team reviews completed points and velocity. Finance sees actual cost against budget, profitability updates from timesheet data, and milestone invoices are triggered according to the sale order. Management views phase completion, sprint velocity, profitability, and utilization from connected reports.

Business Benefits

  • One source of truth connects delivery, sales, HR, timesheet, technology, and accounting data.
  • Profitability is computed from actual timesheet cost rather than manual estimates.
  • Mixed delivery models can run side by side without forcing the company into a single methodology.
  • Gate approvals and predecessor validation create stronger governance for waterfall phases.
  • Allocation and capacity planning use real employee records and role-based weekly hours.
  • Technology capabilities are consistently documented and can be filtered, reviewed, and presented to clients.

Implementation Steps

  1. Persiapkan data master. Import or confirm employee records, role assignments, technology catalog, project tags, and customer or sale order data needed for project creation.
  2. Konfigurasi parameter akuntansi dan proyek. Set company currency, average hourly rate, budget fields, cost tracking preferences, and billing methods such as fixed price, time and material, or milestone billing.
  3. Jalankan pilot project. Select one agile, one waterfall, or one hybrid engagement. Configure backlog, phases, team allocation, budget, and timesheet flow. Validate that actual hours, cost, and profitability update as expected.
  4. Rollout bertahap. Extend the setup to more projects, connect sale orders, assign methodology per project, and train project managers, finance, HR, and delivery leads on their specific workflows.
  5. Evaluasi dan optimasi. Review sprint velocity, profitability, phase completion, and utilization reports. Adjust hourly rates, allocation rules, phase structures, or backlog practices based on actual data.
