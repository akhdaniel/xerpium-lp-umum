10 Best ERP Solutions for Hospital & Pharmacy Operations

October 21, 2026

Hospital and pharmacy operations run on tightly coupled workflows: a patient registers, sees a doctor, receives a prescription, gets medicine dispensed, may be admitted, and then must be billed correctly. When these steps use disconnected spreadsheets or separate legacy applications, staff repeatedly re-enter demographics, miss allergy warnings, create duplicate medical records, lose queue visibility, and struggle to reconcile pharmacy stock with billing. The result is longer wait times, revenue leakage, and higher compliance risk. An integrated ERP with hospital and pharmacy modules removes those handoffs by keeping patient, clinical, pharmacy, inventory, and accounting data in one system.

The 10 Solutions at a Glance

  1. Patient & Medical Record — gives each patient a single lifetime profile with MR number, insurance, allergies, and visit history.
  2. Doctor & Polyclinic — manages doctor credentials, consultation fees, departments, and active practice status.
  3. Queue Management — automates daily queue numbers, priority, status flow, and visit creation per polyclinic.
  4. Outpatient Visit — records vital signs, SOAP notes, ICD-10 diagnoses, and referral status in one encounter.
  5. Prescription & Resep — turns doctor orders into dispense-ready prescriptions with dosage, route, allergy notes, and workflow.
  6. Medicine Catalog — maintains drug categories, generic names, BPOM registration, dosage form, strength, and expiry tracking.
  7. Drug Dispensing — links prescriptions to pharmacist dispense, batch/lot, quantity, and automatic stock picking.
  8. Inpatient Admission — manages ward, bed, admission and discharge diagnoses, length of stay, and discharge condition.
  9. Billing & Invoice — combines consultation, procedure, medicine, lab, room, and nursing charges into one patient bill.
  10. Hospital ↔ Inventory — keeps pharmacy stock synchronized with dispensing, min-stock alerts, batch/lot, and expiry dates.

1. Patient & Medical Record

Patient & Medical Record gives each patient a single, lifetime profile that extends the standard partner record with MR number, NIK, BPJS number, insurance name and policy number. It stores demographics such as age, gender, blood group, religion, and nationality, plus medical information including allergies, chronic conditions, and medical notes. An automatic sequence generates the medical record number, while emergency contact details remain available for urgent care. The module consolidates visit count, admission count, and bill count so front-desk and clinical staff no longer search across systems or risk duplicate patient files. It removes a major source of registration errors and safety gaps.

2. Doctor & Polyclinic

Doctor & Polyclinic manages the clinical service catalog behind outpatient care. It maintains doctor master data with name, SIP and STR numbers, specialization, department, consultation fee, phone, email, and photo. License tracking records SIP and STR expiry dates, helping hospitals prevent inactive or expired practitioners from being scheduled. Departments such as Rawat Jalan, Rawat Inap, UGD, Lab, Radiology, Pharmacy, Surgery, and ICU can have a designated head of department. Active or inactive status controls doctor availability, while computed visit counts give operations teams visibility into workload. This keeps polyclinic scheduling and fee capture aligned with current provider data.

3. Queue Management

Queue Management automates the patient queue per date and polyclinic. The system generates a daily queue number that increments per department, records arrival time, and supports priority levels such as Normal, Urgent, and Emergency. Staff can track each patient through a status flow from Waiting to Called, In Progress, Done, or Skipped, and see the time the patient was called. A queue entry can be converted directly into an outpatient visit, with payment type captured as Umum, BPJS, or Asuransi. This gives the front office and polyclinic a shared, real-time queue instead of paper numbers or ad-hoc whiteboards.

4. Outpatient Visit

Outpatient Visit captures the clinical encounter from registration through diagnosis and disposition. Each visit records the date, doctor, polyclinic, and payment type, alongside vital signs such as blood pressure, pulse, temperature, respiratory rate, SpO2, weight, height, and BMI. Clinicians document anamnesis, assign multiple ICD-10 diagnoses with a primary flag, and complete SOAP notes covering subjective, objective, assessment, and plan. The visit status moves through Draft, Waiting, In Examination, Done, or Cancelled. Referrals to specialists, hospitals, lab, or radiology can be recorded in the same visit. This gives doctors a structured medical record without separate forms.

5. Prescription & Resep

Prescription & Resep turns a doctor order into a dispense-ready instruction. It captures the medicine, dosage such as 3x1 or 2x1, route including Oral, IV, IM, SC, and Topical, duration, and use instructions before or after meals. Each prescription can be linked to the related diagnosis, and the system checks patient allergy notes so pharmacists see warnings before dispensing. A configurable workflow moves prescriptions from Draft to Confirmed, Dispensing, Dispensed, or Cancelled. This reduces handwriting errors, speeds pharmacy handoff, and adds an important safety check at the point of prescribing.

6. Medicine Catalog

Medicine Catalog is the pharmacy master data layer for safe and traceable medication management. It organizes products by category including obat keras, bebas, bebas terbatas, narkotika, psikotropika, alkes, and BHP, and stores generic name, BPOM registration, dosage form, strength, and therapeutic class. Dosage forms cover tablet, capsule, syrup, injection, cream, suppository, drops, inhaler, and patch. Minimum stock levels trigger replenishment alerts, while expiry tracking reduces the risk of expired stock reaching patients. This gives pharmacists one controlled catalog instead of separate spreadsheets for product identity, regulatory data, and stock thresholds.

7. Drug Dispensing

Drug Dispensing controls the actual release of medicine from the pharmacy to the patient. It links the dispense action to the originating prescription and records the responsible pharmacist. Batch or lot numbers and expiry dates are tracked, while quantity tracking compares prescribed quantity against dispensed quantity. The module integrates with stock by automatically creating a stock picking from the pharmacy, so inventory is updated without manual entry. Dispensing status moves through Draft, Dispensed, Partially Returned, or Cancelled, and unit price comes from the product list price. This closes the loop between clinical prescribing, pharmacy fulfillment, and inventory control.

8. Inpatient Admission

Inpatient Admission manages the patient journey from admission to discharge. It records the admitting doctor, ward, bed number, and payment type, plus admission and discharge diagnoses using ICD-10. A configurable status flow moves the patient through Draft, Admitted, In Ward, ICU, Discharged, or Cancelled, while length of stay is computed automatically. Expected discharge date can be set for bed planning, and discharge condition is documented as Recovered, Improved, Referred, AMA, or Died. Anamnesis and treatment summary remain part of the record. This gives nursing and billing teams a single source for inpatient status and finalization.

9. Billing & Invoice

Billing & Invoice consolidates all hospital and pharmacy charges into one patient bill. Bill lines can include consultation, procedure, medicine, lab, radiology, room, and nursing charges, while the bill calculates subtotal, discount, tax, total, paid, and due amounts. Payment methods cover cash, transfer, BPJS, Asuransi, and debit/credit card, with insurance coverage recorded separately from the amount due. Room charges can be added automatically at admission discharge, and procedures or dispensed medicines can be added to the bill. The module can generate an account.move invoice, so clinical activity flows into accounting without duplicate data entry.

10. Hospital ↔ Inventory

Hospital ↔ Inventory keeps pharmacy and medical consumables stock aligned with patient activity. It uses the stock picking created during drug dispensing to update inventory automatically, so pharmacy stock levels reflect actual patient usage. Minimum stock alerts for medicine and BHP help pharmacy teams reorder before shortages interrupt care. Batch and lot tracking, together with expiry date tracking, supports quality control and recall readiness. The module provides pharmacy stock management across the medication catalog, helping hospitals avoid both stockouts and overstock while maintaining a clear audit trail from receipt to dispense.

How to Choose the Right Solution

When evaluating modules for hospital and pharmacy operations, start with the patient flow: the solution should connect patient identity, outpatient visits, admissions, prescriptions, and dispensing in one timeline. Second, check pharmacy safety controls: batch/lot, expiry tracking, controlled substance categories, and automatic stock movement should be native, not bolted on. Third, confirm financial integration: clinical and pharmacy transactions should create billing lines and accounting invoices without rekeying. Fourth, assess compliance and configurability: the system should support ICD-10 diagnosis coding and allow stage-aware workflows for visits, admissions, and prescriptions. Choose the combination that removes manual handoffs rather than adding another front-end layer.

Conclusion

Hospital and pharmacy operations fail most often at the handoffs between registration, clinical care, pharmacy, and billing. The ten solutions above map directly to those handoffs: patient identity, provider and queue management, outpatient clinical documentation, prescribing, pharmacy catalog and dispensing, inpatient admission, billing, and inventory. Together they give hospitals a clear chain from arrival to final invoice while preserving patient safety and stock accuracy.
