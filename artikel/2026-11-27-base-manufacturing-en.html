Manufacturing ERP Software: How Xerpium MRP Connects BOM, Routing, and Production Costing

Manufacturers rarely struggle because they lack data. They struggle because the data lives in too many places — bills of materials in spreadsheets, work orders on printed sheets, production costs reconstructed by finance weeks after the batch has shipped. By the time the numbers are consolidated, the production decisions they were meant to inform have already been made.

The result is familiar: material shortages discovered on the shop floor, routing changes that never reach the costing model, and margin figures that are educated guesses rather than measurements. An integrated manufacturing ERP system addresses this by keeping the bill of materials, the routing, the work order, and the cost calculation in one connected flow. Xerpium approaches MRP as a base module that sits inside the broader ERP suite rather than as a standalone shop-floor tool.

Common Challenges in Discrete and Process Manufacturing

Fragmented product structure data

When the BOM exists in a spreadsheet and the routing lives in a separate planning document, every engineering change becomes a manual synchronisation exercise. A substituted component may be updated in procurement's list but not in the costing sheet, so the standard cost quietly drifts away from reality.

Work orders that are disconnected from inventory and finance

Shop-floor paperwork that is not tied to the system creates a reporting lag. Material consumption is recorded late, inventory balances are overstated, and finance closes the period using estimates instead of posted transactions.

Costing that arrives too late to be useful

Production cost is often calculated after the fact, by assembling material issues, labour time, and overhead from different sources. Useful cost control requires the calculation to follow the work order itself, not trail behind it.

Multi-entity complexity

Groups that run several companies or plants face an additional layer: consolidating production and cost data across entities that may transact in different currencies, while still keeping a defensible record of who changed what.

How the Xerpium Manufacturing Module Works

Bill of materials and routing

The BOM defines what goes into the product; the routing defines the sequence of operations and work centres that transform it. Holding both in the same module means a change to either one is visible to planning, inventory, and costing at the same moment.

For example, when an engineering team adds an inspection step to a routing, that operation becomes part of the production order structure rather than an undocumented habit on the floor.

Work orders

Work orders turn planned production into executable instructions tied to the BOM and routing. Because the work order is a transactional document inside the ERP, material movements and operation progress are captured against it rather than reconstructed later.

Production cost calculation

Production costing in Xerpium is part of the same module, so costs are derived from the same BOM, routing, and work order records used to run production. This removes the reconciliation step between what the shop floor did and what finance records.

Multi-company, multi-currency, and audit trail

The module supports multi-company and multi-currency operations, which matters for manufacturers with more than one legal entity or cross-border supply arrangements. The audit trail records changes, which supports internal control reviews and external audit requests without manual document chasing.

Real-time dashboards and reporting

Dashboards and reports update in real time, so production status and cost figures are available as work progresses rather than at period close.

A Short Example Flow

Consider a contract manufacturer producing an assembled component for two customers under different legal entities in the same group.

  1. The engineering team maintains the BOM and routing for the component in Xerpium.
  2. A confirmed order generates a work order referencing that BOM and routing.
  3. Materials are issued against the work order; operations are recorded against the defined work centres.
  4. Production cost is calculated from the same work order record.
  5. Finance sees the cost in the relevant company's books, in the relevant currency, with the audit trail intact.
  6. Management reviews output and cost on a real-time dashboard rather than waiting for a monthly summary.

Business Benefits

  • Visibility during the period, not after it. Real-time dashboards let supervisors and managers act while the batch is still running.
  • Group-level workability. Multi-company and multi-currency support, with an audit trail, suits manufacturers operating more than one entity.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Xerpium Manufacturing handle both the BOM and the routing?

Yes. The module covers bills of materials, routing, work orders, and production cost calculation. Keeping these together means a routing change is reflected in the work order structure and the cost calculation that follows it.

Can the module support a group with several manufacturing companies?

The module supports multi-company and multi-currency operations. This allows entities within a group to run production in their own books and currencies while remaining within one system.

How should we evaluate the total cost of ownership of a manufacturing ERP?

Look beyond licensing at implementation effort, data migration, and ongoing maintenance. Integration cost is often the largest hidden item — a system where manufacturing is a base module connected to inventory and finance generally avoids the interface work that standalone MRP tools require. Ask vendors to scope these elements explicitly before comparing figures.

What does the audit trail actually record?

The audit trail maintains a record of changes within the system, which supports internal control reviews and audit requests. For manufacturers, this is most relevant where BOM revisions or cost adjustments need to be traced to a source.

How does the AI Assistant fit into production work?

The Manufacturing module is integrated with the Xerpium AI Assistant, giving users conversational access to production and cost data held in the system. It is a faster route to existing information, not a replacement for planning decisions.

Is Xerpium suitable if we only need production management today?

It can be, but the module is designed as a base module that integrates with the rest of the suite. If you expect inventory, procurement, or finance to come into scope later, starting within an integrated suite avoids a second integration project.
