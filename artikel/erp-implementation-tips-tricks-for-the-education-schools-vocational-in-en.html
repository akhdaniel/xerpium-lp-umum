ERP Implementation Tips & Tricks for the Education (Schools & Vocational) Industry with Xerpium

Schools and vocational institutions run on deadlines that cannot be moved. Admissions close, semesters start, report cards are printed, and tuition invoices go out whether or not the administrative system is ready. That is precisely why ERP implementation in education carries a different kind of risk than in most industries: a delayed go-live does not just slow operations, it collides with an academic calendar that has no slack.

Most failed school ERP projects do not fail because the software lacks features. They fail because everything was switched on at once, master data was migrated late, and teachers were asked to change their daily routine during exam week. This article outlines practical tips for implementing Xerpium ERP — built on Odoo — in schools and vocational institutions so the rollout is fast, controlled, and low-risk.

Common Challenges in Education ERP Projects

  • Fragmented student data. Admissions records, parent data, addresses, and documents often live in spreadsheets, paper forms, and separate admin PCs, making a single student master record hard to assemble.
  • Academic complexity. Subjects, teacher assignments, KKM thresholds, assessment types, weighting rules, and grading scales differ per level and per academic year — and they change between semesters.
  • Low teacher adoption. Teachers are domain experts, not system users. If grade input or attendance takes longer than the old paper process, the system is quietly abandoned.
  • Manual and untraceable finance. Monthly SPP, re-registration fees, and education fees are reconciled by hand, with no audit trail linking a payment to an invoice.
  • Parents left in the dark. Schedules, attendance, grades, and outstanding bills are communicated through chat groups, which creates repeated administrative load for the front office.

Tip 1: Phase the Rollout Around the Academic Calendar

Do not attempt a big-bang go-live. Map Xerpium modules to natural academic milestones.

Start with admissions (PSB) ahead of the intake season: define the admission process per academic year with waves and capacity, register prospective students with NISN, NIS, birth data, gender, blood type, religion and ethnicity, capture father/mother/guardian details, and collect documents (KTP, KK, birth certificate, diploma, photos) through structured uploads. Admissions is self-contained, time-boxed, and delivers visible value fast.

Next, move to placement and selection: PSB scores with five configurable score fields, two configurable contribution fields, admission groups with capacity and filled counts, then the placement workflow from Draft → Open → Done that filters passing students and assigns them to destination classes. When placement is marked done, student-class records are created automatically and academic data becomes active — which is exactly the foundation the next phase needs.

Only then activate curriculum, assessment, attendance, and finance.

Tip 2: Make Master Data the First Deliverable, Not an Afterthought

The single biggest schedule risk is data. Treat it as a workstream with its own owner and deadline.

Prioritise in this order: address master data (province, city, district, sub-district, postal code using tree selection), religion and ethnicity master data, subjects with codes and mandatory/additional flags, teacher records extended from hr.employee with NIP, NUPTK, NRG, academic titles, department and teacher status, then learning hours (period 1 to N with start and end times) and days (Monday to Saturday, configurable).

Clean this layer first and the rest of the configuration — KKM per subject, level and academic year; assessment types such as UTS, UAS, quizzes, assignments and practicals; assessment aspects covering affective, cognitive, concept comprehension, psychomotor and practical — falls into place quickly.

Tip 3: Build a Small Cross-Functional Team with Clear Ownership

An education ERP project needs four roles represented from day one: an admissions/front-office owner, an academic/curriculum owner, a homeroom or teacher representative, and a finance owner. Each owns the configuration decisions in their domain — grading bands (A 90–100, B 80–89, C 70–79, D 60–69, E 0–59 are configurable), weighting rules for quizzes, assignments, practicals, UTS and UAS with percentage weights that move from draft to verified, and comment predicates for subject and attitude assessment.

Decisions made by committee late in the project are the main source of rework.

Tip 4: Use Odoo Workflow States as Your Change Management Framework

Xerpium's education processes follow consistent state transitions — Draft → Open → Done for assessments, report card grades, class attendance, class transfers, grade promotion, graduation and transcripts; Draft → Done for student mutations. Train staff on the states, not on individual screens. Once a user understands that Draft means editable, Open means in progress and Done means locked and committed, every module becomes familiar.

Pair this with the mail thread: all processes are recorded in the chatter, giving supervisors an audit trail without requiring extra reporting discipline from teachers.

Tip 5: Deliver Quick Wins Through the School Information & Management System Portals

Nothing builds sponsor confidence faster than visible value for parents and students.

The student portal gives learners a dashboard with schedules, latest grades and announcements, plus academic views for schedule, attendance and grades, assignment lists with answer uploads, grade history and online report cards, monthly attendance recaps, and messaging with teachers and homeroom teachers.

The parent portal shows the child's schedule, attendance recap, grades and report cards per semester, bills, statistics charts for grade and attendance development, a PPDB registration path for other children, and direct chat with the school.

Combine this with the Xendit payment gateway — public key, secret key and webhook token configuration, invoice creation via Xendit API v2 with a redirect payment URL, and webhook handling where PAID triggers done plus auto-reconciliation and email, PENDING sets pending, and EXPIRED or FAILED cancels the transaction. Recurring monthly SPP, education fees and re-registration can then be collected through QRIS, virtual accounts or credit card.

Tip 6: Reduce Teacher Workload Before You Add Requests

Teachers accept a system that saves them time. Use the attendance "find students" button to auto-populate all students in a class, rely on automatic calculation of effective school days per month and automatic per-student recaps of present, sick, permitted, absent and leave counts. Use AI RPP to generate lesson plan drafts via the GPT model, and AI Soal to generate questions linked to surveys for online exams. Let teacher schedule views and the teaching hours recap (Draft → Open → Close) replace manual tally sheets.

Business Benefits

  • Faster, auditable admissions from registration through placement into active student records.
  • Consistent academic standards via KKM, weighting rules and configurable grading scales.
  • Automatic report card calculation producing both numeric and letter grades.
  • Reduced front-office load through self-service student and parent portals.
  • Reliable tuition collection with automatic reconciliation through Xendit webhooks.
  • Complete audit trail across every process through the chatter.

Example Implementation Flow

A vocational school begins in month one by configuring the admission process with two waves and capacity limits. Prospective students register, upload documents, and sit the entrance test; scores are entered into the five configurable PSB score fields. Placement is opened, passing students are filtered and assigned to destination classes, and on completion student-class records are generated.

In month two, subjects, teacher-subject assignments with SK and validity dates, KKM, learning hours and class schedules are configured. Teachers begin recording lesson attendance per session with period, material and notes.

In month three, assessment and report card workflows go live alongside Xendit invoicing for monthly SPP. Parents are onboarded to the portal to view schedules, attendance, grades and bills — and to pay online.

Conclusion

Successful education ERP implementation is less about software capability and more about sequencing: admissions first, master data early, workflow states as training, and parent-facing quick wins to sustain momentum.
