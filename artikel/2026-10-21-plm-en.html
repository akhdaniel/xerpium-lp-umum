PLM in ERP: How Xerpium Manages ECOs, BOM Revisions, and Change Approvals

October 21, 2026

# PLM in ERP: How Xerpium Manages ECOs, BOM Revisions, and Change Approvals

Product development rarely stops after the first release. Components change, routings shift, suppliers update specifications, and production teams need answers fast. Without a structured product lifecycle management (PLM) process, engineering changes stay trapped in email threads, spreadsheets, or disconnected documents. The result is confusion on the shop floor, outdated bills of materials, and approvals that drag on without clear ownership.

Xerpium ERP addresses this by embedding PLM capabilities directly into the ERP environment. Engineering change orders (ECOs), BOM revisions, approval workflows, impact analysis, and document controls all work from the same system that manages manufacturing and inventory. That connection helps teams move from change request to approved, applied revision with a clear audit trail.

Common challenges in product lifecycle management

Many manufacturing and product teams face similar issues when managing product changes:

  • ECOs are created in documents or email, with no automatic numbering, status tracking, or connection to the BOM.
  • BOM revisions are applied manually, leading to version confusion and no reliable link between old and new bills of materials.
  • Change impact is unclear because component, routing, and by-product differences are not calculated automatically.
  • Approval steps are inconsistent, and there is no stage gating to prevent premature progress.
  • Documents such as SOPs or spec sheets are not synchronized when a new BOM revision becomes active.
  • Multi-company organizations struggle to isolate ECOs and documents by company.

Xerpium PLM is designed to remove these disconnects by keeping product change, manufacturing data, and approvals in one controlled flow.

Xerpium PLM: core capabilities

Engineering Change Orders with structured tracking

Xerpium PLM provides engineering change orders as structured records. Each ECO receives an auto-generated reference using the ECO/##### sequence. Teams can set a description, assign a Normal or High priority, and define whether the change applies to the Bill of Materials or the Product Only.

Effectivity can be set to As soon as possible or At Date for planned changes. Tags and colored labels help categorize ECOs, while kanban states — Normal, Approved, or Blocked — give a visual view of where each change stands.

ECO types add another layer of control. Each type can represent a category such as BOM Change or Product Update, with its own stage mapping. A mail alias can be configured so that an email to that alias automatically creates an ECO of the corresponding type. Smart counters track the number of ECOs, approvals, validations, and approvals assigned to the current user. ECO types are company-scoped for multi-company environments.

BOM revision and controlled apply

Starting a revision from an existing BOM creates a working copy with version incremented and active status set to false. The previous BOM is linked through a previous_bom_id field, preserving the audit trail. When the change is approved and applied, the new BOM becomes active, the old BOM is deactivated, and operation documents are synchronized to the new BOM operations.

Xerpium PLM also supports a will_update_version flag. This allows teams to apply changes without bumping the product version when appropriate. When versioning is enabled, product.template.version increments automatically on apply.

Change impact analysis

A key part of managing ECOs is understanding exactly what changes between BOM revisions. Xerpium PLM automatically calculates component differences, including adds, removals, and updates with quantity and unit-of-measure deltas. By-product changes and operation changes are also tracked, including routing or workcenter differences and time cycle deltas.

The impact view is color-coded: green for additions, red for removals, and blue for updates. Rebase tracking uses previous_change_ids and bom_rebase_ids to detect conflicts when the upstream BOM changes while an ECO is still in progress.

Manufacturing and product integration

Because Xerpium PLM sits inside the ERP, it connects directly to manufacturing and product records. A user can create an ECO from a Manufacturing Order with one click, and the ECO automatically fills the related BOM and product. The Manufacturing Order links to the ECO through production_id and eco_ids. BOM records expose eco_ids and eco_count, while product templates include an ECO tab, eco_ids, and an action to view related ECOs.

When an ECO is applied, operation documents can sync to the new BOM operations, so production work instructions and SOPs stay aligned with the active revision.

Approval workflow and dynamic stages

Approvals are not left to chance. When required, Xerpium PLM auto-generates approval records and mail activities for the assigned approvers. Stage advancement is blocked if mandatory approvals are not complete. Only users listed in the approval template can approve or reject. Moving a stage closes processed approvals and removes pending ones, while mail activities create to-do items for approvers.

Approval types include:

  • Mandatory — must be approved before the stage can advance.
  • Optional — can be approved but does not block progress.
  • Comment — adds feedback without affecting approval status.
  • Constraint — a user cannot be assigned more than once per stage.

Dynamic stage features make the flow configurable rather than hard-coded. Stages are independent per model, with Python hooks executed when a record enters a stage, server actions triggered automatically, and RBAC groups controlling who can move a record to a given stage. Stages sync natively with state fields such as confirmed, progress, and done. Back navigation can be enabled, and default stages can be assigned automatically.

Rebase and conflict handling

Product data can change while an ECO is still being processed. If the upstream BOM changes during an open ECO, the ECO enters a rebase state. If automatic handling is not possible, it moves to a conflict state for manual resolution. The current_bom_id field tracks the intermediate BOM, while bom_rebase_ids and previous_change_ids compute the difference automatically. The full changelog remains available in the mail thread chatter.

Documents, links, and notes

Xerpium PLM supports three document types in one polymorphic model. Files can be uploaded as attachments with automatic mimetype detection and copied on BOM revision. External links can point to Google Slides or other URLs without requiring a file upload. Text notes support plain text content and inline editing for quick instructions.

Documents can attach to any model through res_model and res_id. Kanban views render differently based on document type. Operation documents can be linked to BOM operations for SOPs, and on ECO apply, _sync_operation_documents() copies documents to the new BOM. Cover images and multi-company isolation are also supported.

PLM dashboard

A dedicated PLM dashboard provides a visual overview using Dashboard Studio infrastructure. It refreshes automatically every 300 seconds, applies filters on open, and combines ORM sources such as ECOs, BOMs, and products with SQL-based aggregate sources. The green theme stays consistent with PLM branding, and the dashboard is visible to all PLM users.

Business benefits

Using Xerpium ERP for product lifecycle management delivers practical benefits:

  • Faster ECO processing through auto-generated references, mail aliases, and structured stages.
  • Fewer production errors because BOM revisions and operation documents stay synchronized.
  • Clear accountability with mandatory approvals, user restrictions, and mail activities.
  • Better change visibility through automatic component, by-product, and routing impact analysis.
  • Full audit trails from BOM history, ECO links, and chatter-based changelogs.
  • Multi-company control with company-scoped ECO types, documents, and record rules.
  • Cross-functional visibility with PLM data connected to Manufacturing Orders, products, and dashboard KPIs.

Example workflow in Xerpium

A production engineer notices a component substitution on a Manufacturing Order. From the MO, they create an ECO with one click. The ECO is linked to the BOM and product automatically. The engineer sets the ECO type to BOM Change, assigns a High priority, and starts a BOM revision.

Xerpium PLM creates a working copy with version +1 while the current BOM remains inactive. The change impact view shows the removed component, the added component, and the quantity delta in green, red, and blue. Since the change affects routing, the operation time delta is also visible.

The ECO enters an approval stage. Mandatory approvers receive mail activities. The stage cannot advance until the required approvals are complete. Once approved, the ECO is applied: the new BOM becomes active, the old BOM is deactivated, operation documents sync to the new BOM operations, and the product version increments. The PLM dashboard updates with the latest ECO and BOM data.

Conclusion

Product lifecycle management works best when it is not treated as a separate island from manufacturing and ERP data. Xerpium ERP connects ECOs, BOM revisions, approvals, documents, and change impact analysis directly to the product and production records your team already uses. The result is a more controlled, auditable, and efficient way to manage product changes from request to applied revision.
