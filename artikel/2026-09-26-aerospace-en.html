# Aerospace Manufacturing ERP: How Xerpium Brings Configuration Control, Traceability, and Compliance to Every Serial Number

Aerospace manufacturing operates in an environment where a single drawing revision, material certificate, or calibration record can determine whether an aircraft is safe, compliant, and airworthy. Managing aircraft configurations, serialized parts, engineering changes, supplier certifications, and quality documentation with disconnected spreadsheets or generic ERP systems creates risk, delays, and audit exposure.

Xerpium ERP addresses these challenges with a dedicated aerospace manufacturing approach. It combines aircraft type and configuration management, part and serial number tracking, engineering change control, material traceability, manufacturing execution, and quality management in one enterprise platform. The result is a controlled, transparent, and audit-ready production environment for aerospace manufacturers and MRO operations.

Common Challenges in Aerospace Manufacturing

Aerospace manufacturers face a demanding set of operational requirements:

  • Maintaining configuration control across as-designed, as-built, and as-maintained aircraft records.
  • Tracking every part, serial number, material batch, and test report from raw material to finished assembly.
  • Controlling engineering changes through structured multi-approver workflows without losing revision history.
  • Managing non-conformance, corrective actions, first article inspection, and certificates of conformance.
  • Ensuring supplier certifications, tool calibration, and operator qualifications remain current and traceable.

Without a unified ERP solution, these requirements become fragmented across departments, making audits difficult and increasing the chance of non-compliance.

Xerpium ERP Modules for Aerospace Manufacturing

Aircraft Type and Configuration Control

Xerpium captures aircraft type records with a unique code, manufacturer, category such as fixed wing, rotary wing, UAV, or glider, and key technical data including MTOW, range, cruise speed, engines, engine type, seats, and certification standard. Each aircraft type can be linked to configurations that distinguish as-designed, as-built, and as-maintained states. Configuration records include a variant code, description, and a direct BOM link per configuration, so every build or maintenance scenario uses the correct structure.

Part Number and Serial Number Management

Part number records support raw materials, components, assemblies, sub-assemblies, tooling, and consumables. Each part can include alternates, drawing number and revision, material specification, weight, ITAR/EAR control flags, and serial or batch tracking requirements. Serial numbers are unique per part and linked to the product, aircraft type, and lifecycle status such as available, installed, in repair, or scrapped. Flight hours, cycles, and overhaul due dates are also captured on the serial number level for full component history.

Work Center, Tooling, and Supplier Control

Work centers extend the standard production work center with aerospace-specific attributes such as center type, required certification, capacity by aircraft type, calibration due tracking, and environmental controls. Typical work centers include Assembly Line 1, Machining 1, Testing Station 1, Paint Shop 1, Quality Inspection 1, and Tooling 1. Tooling records identify each tool by unique number and type, including fixture, gauge, special, standard, and measuring. Calibration intervals, last and next calibration dates, status, and owner are tracked. Supplier records include tier level, AS9100D certification, quality rating from 0 to 100, on-time delivery rate, approved parts, qualification status, and last audit date.

Engineering Change Order (ECO)

Engineering changes are managed through a structured ECO workflow: Draft, Under Review, Approved, Implemented, and Closed. ECO types cover design, process, material, tooling, and documentation changes. Each ECO links to the affected BOM, part number, and aircraft type. Xerpium supports multi-approver control with reviewer and approver roles, timestamps, and approve or reject actions. BOM impact is connected directly to the production BOM for revision tracking, and part numbers can be linked many-to-many to show all affected items in one change order.

Material Traceability and Test Documentation

Full material traceability starts with batch number, heat treatment number, material certificate, and supplier certificate. Chemical composition and mechanical properties are recorded alongside test report references and dates. Material records link directly to the aerospace serial number, creating a complete audit trail from raw material to finished part. This supports the rigorous traceability required in aerospace manufacturing and supplier audits.

Manufacturing and Work Order Execution

Manufacturing orders extend the production order with aircraft type, serial number, configuration, customer, and priority levels including AOG, Critical, High, Normal, and Low. Quality release and sign-off requirements can be flagged at the order level. Sequence auto-numbering and mail thread tracking provide structured order control. BOM integration creates manufacturing orders from the BOM specific to each aircraft type and configuration. Work orders extend further with operator assignment from qualified employees, sign-off requirements, tool assignment, and special instructions. A digital sign-off action records the operator, timestamp, and completion, ensuring accountability at the work order level.

Quality Management: NCR, CAPA, FAI, Inspection Plan, and Quality Release

Non-conformance reports are generated automatically with severity level, root cause, and disposition. Each NCR links to the manufacturing order, affected part number, serial number, and quantity. Disposition options include use-as-is, rework, repair, scrap, and return-to-supplier. CAPA records provide corrective action plans, ownership, due dates, and effectiveness verification. Preventive actions include recurrence prevention and monitoring plans. CAPA can originate directly from NCR findings.

First article inspection supports AS9102 full, partial, and delta FAI. Inspection lines capture nominal, minimum, maximum, and actual values with automatic pass or fail results. Each FAI links to the manufacturing order, part number, serial number, and drawing revision. Inspection plans define incoming, in-process, and final inspection points per part number, including method, specification, and frequency. Quality release records provide a certificate of conformance with draft, pending, released, and on-hold stages. Released records identify the authorized signatory, timestamp, and linked manufacturing order.

Aerospace-Specific Chart of Accounts

Xerpium includes a dedicated chart of accounts for aerospace manufacturing. Cost accounts cover raw materials, direct labor, factory overhead, and work in process. Revenue accounts support aerospace product sales, MRO services, and engineering revenue. Balance sheet accounts include current assets such as inventory and WIP, as well as fixed assets like machinery and measuring instruments. P&L structure covers revenue, COGS, engineering expense, quality expense, and certification-related costs.

KPI Dashboard and Cross-Module Integration

Aerospace KPIs provide real-time visibility into total manufacturing orders by state, production efficiency against target, work center downtime trends, open NCRs, and first article inspection pass or fail rates. Cross-module integration connects accounting with automatic WIP and COGS journal entries, inventory with serial number and lot traceability, purchasing with supplier management and approved parts, and HR with operator assignment, sign-off, and certification tracking. The Xerpium AI assistant supports daily workflows by searching part numbers, checking manufacturing order status, and generating reports.

Business Benefits

  • Single source of truth for aircraft configuration, BOM revision, and engineering change control.
  • Complete traceability from raw material certificates to serialized finished parts and installed status.
  • Improved supplier and tooling control through certification, calibration, and qualification tracking.
  • Structured quality management across NCR, CAPA, FAI, and certificate of conformance.
  • Aerospace-specific financial visibility with automatic WIP and COGS accounting.
  • Faster operational decisions through real-time KPIs and AI-assisted reporting.

A Practical Use-Case Flow

A manufacturer receives an AOG manufacturing order for a specific aircraft configuration. The planner selects the configuration and creates the manufacturing order from the approved BOM, linking the aircraft type and target serial number. Work orders are generated with operator assignments, required tooling, and special instructions. Operators perform digital sign-off at completion. During inspection, a non-conformance is recorded, linked to the affected part and serial number. A CAPA is initiated from the NCR, and a delta FAI is completed for the changed characteristic. Quality release issues a certificate of conformance once the final inspection passes. Raw material batch, heat treatment, and test report records remain linked throughout the process. Accounting journals update automatically, and the dashboard shows the open NCR, FAI result, and production efficiency.
