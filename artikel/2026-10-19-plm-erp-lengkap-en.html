Complete ERP solution for PLM (Product Lifecycle Management): End-to-End Change Control, BOM Revision, and Manufacturing Alignment with Xerpium ERP

October 19, 2026

Product lifecycle management rarely happens in isolation. A single engineering change can affect bills of materials, routings, work instructions, approval chains, and active manufacturing orders. When ECOs, BOM revisions, and product data live in spreadsheets or disconnected PLM tools, manufacturers face revision mismatches, approval delays, and document drift between engineering and production. Xerpium ERP addresses this with a complete PLM scope inside the ERP environment: change control, dynamic approval stages, BOM revisioning, impact analysis, document management, manufacturing integration, and multi-company governance.

Common Challenges in PLM (Product Lifecycle Management)

  • Uncontrolled ECO numbering and fragmented change history make audit trails difficult.
  • BOM revisions are often copied manually without clear links to the previous version.
  • Change impact on components, quantities, by-products, and operations is not visible before approval.
  • Approval workflows are informal or manual, with no stage gating or user restrictions.
  • Operation documents and work instructions are not synchronized when a BOM changes.
  • Multi-company product data and change records lack consistent isolation and access rules.

Xerpium Solution Coverage

ECO Core, Change Types, and Mail Aliases

Xerpium starts with structured engineering change orders. ECO references are generated automatically through ir.sequence as ECO/#####. Each ECO can carry a description, priority such as Normal or High, and a change type that applies to the Bill of Materials or the product only. Effectivity can be set as “as soon as possible” or at a specific effective date via At Date. Tags and colour coding support categorization, while Kanban states — Normal, Approved, Blocked — provide visual tracking.

ECO Types allow teams to categorize changes such as “BOM Change” or “Product Update”. Each type can have its own stage mapping. Mail aliases connect directly to this structure: sending an email to an alias automatically creates an ECO with the corresponding type. Smart counters like nb_ecos, nb_approvals, nb_approvals_my, and nb_validation give quick visibility into workload. ECO types also support multi-company scoping, so different entities can maintain their own configurations.

BOM Revision, Apply, and Change Impact Analysis

When an ECO moves into revision work, Xerpium can start a BOM revision. The system copies the BOM into a working copy, increments the version by one, and sets the working copy as inactive. A previous_bom_id link preserves the audit chain. On apply, the new BOM becomes active, the old BOM is deactivated, and operation documents are synchronized to the new BOM operations. A will_update_version flag allows applying changes without bumping the version when needed. Product versioning is also automated: applying an ECO increments product.template.version.

Change impact analysis is built into the same workflow. Xerpium automatically diffs component changes, showing additions, removals, and updates with quantity and unit-of-measure deltas. It also tracks by-product changes on the BOM and operation or routing changes with time cycle deltas. Colour coding helps reviewers quickly interpret the diff: green for added, red for removed, and blue for updated. Rebase tracking through previous_change_ids and bom_rebase_ids supports conflict handling when upstream data changes. If an upstream BOM changes while an ECO is in progress, the record can enter a rebase state. In more complex cases, a conflict state is used for manual resolution, while current_bom_id tracks the intermediate BOM. The full changelog remains available through the mail thread chatter.

Approval Mechanism and Dynamic Stages

Approval management is automatic and stage-aware. The system creates approval records and mail activities through _create_approvals(). Stage advancement is blocked if mandatory approvals are not complete. Approve and reject actions are restricted to users listed in template.user_ids. Moving to another stage closes processed approvals and removes pending ones. Approvers receive auto-created todo activities through mail activities.

Approval types add flexibility. Mandatory approvals must be approved before a stage can advance; optional approvals are allowed but do not block the flow; comment approvals only capture input without influencing status; and constraints prevent the same user from being assigned more than once per stage. Dynamic stages are configurable per model, in this case xerpium.plm.eco. Stages can trigger Python hooks through enter_method and server actions automatically when a record enters a stage. RBAC groups restrict who can move a record to a specific stage. Native state synchronization keeps the stage and state field aligned across confirmed, progress, and done states. Back navigation is configurable with allow_back, and approval templates can be attached to stages via xerpium_dynamic_stage_plm.

Documents, External Links, and Operation Documents

Xerpium PLM supports three document types in one polymorphic structure. File documents upload PDFs and images through ir.attachment, with automatic mimetype detection, copy during BOM revision, and cover image support. External links open Google Slides or any external URL without uploading a file. Text notes provide plain text content for quick instructions or notes.

Documents can be attached to different models through res_model and res_id. Kanban views render different card layouts based on document type. Operation documents can be linked to a specific bom_operation_id for SOPs per operation. When an ECO is applied, _sync_operation_documents() copies operation documents to the new BOM. File-type documents are duplicated with their attachments during copy, and multi-company isolation is applied through company_id.

Manufacturing, Product, Dashboard, and Multi-Company

Manufacturing integration keeps PLM and MRP aligned. Users can create an ECO directly from a Manufacturing Order with one click, and the system auto-fills the BOM and product. The ECO stores production_id, while the manufacturing order shows related eco_ids. Applying an ECO activates the new BOM version and synchronizes operation documents, including work center documents in mrp.routing.workcenter.document_ids.

Product integration is equally direct. Product forms display eco_ids and an ECO notebook tab for change history. Product version numbers update automatically when an ECO is applied. Spec sheets and datasheets can be attached as documents. The PLM dashboard is built on Dashboard Studio infrastructure and includes auto-refresh every 300 seconds, auto-applied filters, dual ORM and SQL data sources, and a green theme consistent with PLM branding. Multi-company record rules isolate ECOs, documents, and ECO types by company_id, while PLM Users have read, write, and create access without delete rights, and PLM Managers have full CRUD.

End-to-End Flow

A manufacturing engineer identifies a required BOM change from an active Manufacturing Order. From the MO, the engineer creates an ECO with one click. Xerpium auto-fills the BOM and product, applies the correct ECO type, and generates the ECO/##### reference. The ECO enters the first stage defined by the type. Mandatory approvers receive mail activities and approve or reject through the stage-gated workflow. If an upstream BOM changes while the ECO is open, the system moves the ECO into rebase or conflict state for review. Once approved, the engineer applies the change: the working BOM becomes active, the previous BOM is deactivated, product version increments, and operation documents sync to the new BOM. The dashboard and product form reflect the updated ECO history immediately.

  3. Pilot: Run a pilot on a selected product family or manufacturing line. Test ECO creation from MO, BOM revision, impact analysis, approval gating, and document sync.
  4. Rollout: Extend access to PLM Users and Managers across relevant teams. Train on Kanban states, stage navigation, and rebase or conflict handling.
  5. Evaluasi: Review dashboard KPIs, audit trails, and stage performance. Adjust approval templates and stage rules, then expand to additional companies or product lines.
