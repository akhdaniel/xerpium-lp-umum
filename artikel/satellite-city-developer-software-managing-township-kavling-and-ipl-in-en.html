Satellite City Developer Software: Managing Township, Kavling, and IPL in One ERP System with Xerpium

Developing a satellite city is not a single project — it is dozens of parallel projects running at once. A developer may be selling kavling in one cluster, pouring roads in another, handing over units in a third, and billing monthly maintenance fees across everything already occupied. Most teams manage this with a patchwork of spreadsheets, a standalone accounting package, and WhatsApp groups for site progress.

The result is predictable: sales admin does not know which lots are truly available, finance cannot reconcile installment payments against PPJB terms, and management has no single view of infrastructure progress versus budget. An integrated ERP system built around the township lifecycle addresses this by keeping the master plan, the unit inventory, the sales agreement, and the ledger in one data model.

Key Takeaways

  • Satellite city development spans project, cluster, kavling, PPJB, payment schedule, infrastructure, handover, facility, and IPL billing — these should live in one connected system, not separate files.
  • Xerpium's Satellite City modules (sc.project down to sc.ipl.billing.line) model each of these as linked records with computed statuses, so sold/available counts and progress percentages update automatically.
  • Sales agreements can auto-create a sale order and a payment schedule, with each installment term invoiced separately through Accounting.
  • Property-specific accounting is covered: auto-journals on kavling sales, infrastructure capitalization, PPN 11%, PPh Final 4%, and e-Faktur via Coretax integration.

Common Challenges in Satellite City Development

Unit Inventory That Drifts Out of Sync

A township can hold thousands of kavling across many clusters. When availability is tracked in spreadsheets, double-booking and stale "available" lists are routine. The underlying problem is that status changes happen in sales, finance, and site operations at different times, with no shared record.

Payment Schedules Disconnected From the Ledger

A PPJB with a 36-month installment scheme produces a down payment plus dozens of due dates. If those terms are not linked to actual invoices, collection monitoring becomes manual reconciliation, and late-payment fines are tracked informally.

Infrastructure Progress Without Cost Context

Roads, drainage, water supply, and electricity networks each have their own contractor, budget, and timeline. Reporting progress as a percentage is easy; tying that percentage to actual cost versus budget is where most tracking breaks down.

Recurring IPL Billing at Scale

Once occupancy grows, monthly maintenance (IPL) billing involves hundreds of owners. Generating invoices one by one is not sustainable, and skipped or duplicated lines are hard to detect after the fact.

How the Xerpium Application Structures a Township

Project, Cluster, and Kavling Hierarchy

sc.project holds the township master record — auto-sequenced as SC.PRJ/####, with project type (Residential, Commercial, Mixed-Use, Industrial), location, total area in hectares, master plan document, timeline, and the owner linked as a res.partner. State moves from draft to planned, in progress, and completed.

sc.cluster sits beneath it with its own code, area, and type. Its kavling_count, available_count, and sold_count are computed fields, so cluster-level statistics reflect unit activity without manual tallying.

sc.kavling is the individual unit: lot number unique per cluster, land price plus building price auto-computed into total price, and physical attributes such as land area, building area, facing direction, corner lot, and facing road. Status flows from Available to Reserved, Sold, Handed Over, or Cancelled.

Kavling Types as Reusable Templates

sc.kavling.type stores standard dimensions — building area, land area, bedrooms, bathrooms, floors — and links to a product.product so the type can flow into a sale order. For example, a "Type 45/90" record displays as Type Name (100m² / 50m²) format and can be used to generate many kavling with consistent specifications.

Sales, PPJB, and Payment in the Odoo-Based System

Customer Agreement (PPJB)

sc.customer.agreement carries an auto-sequenced number (SC.AGR/####) with tracking enabled. It records buyer, project, kavling, and related kavling type, then computes net price from land price, building price, and discount. Payment scheme options include Cash, Installment over 12/24/36/48/60 months, or Custom.

The down payment percentage automatically derives the DP amount from net price, and per-installment amount is computed as (net − DP) ÷ installment count. The state flow runs Draft → Confirmed → Approved → Payment In Progress → Completed → Handed Over.

Automatic Payment Schedule Generation

When an agreement reaches completed, sc.payment.schedule lines are generated automatically. A cash scheme produces a single down payment line equal to net price; an installment scheme produces a DP line at sequence 10 followed by installment lines at 11, 12, 13, and so on.

Each line carries a due date, amount, and currency, and can be invoiced through account.move via the invoice_id field. The is_paid flag is computed from the invoice payment state, and separate fine lines can be added with their own payment type.

Flow Into Sale Orders and Tax

On approval, the agreement can auto-create a sale.order, using the product from kavling_type_id.product_id at net price. Coretax integration supports auto-generated e-Faktur PPN for kavling sales, PPh Final 4% calculation, NPWP validation, and invoice returns.

Infrastructure, Handover, and Community Operations

sc.infrastructure covers Road, Bridge, Water Supply, Electricity, Drainage, Gas Distribution, Telecom, and Green Space/Park. Each item records length, width, budget amount, actual cost, construction start, target and actual completion, and the contractor as a res.partner.

Progress is not typed in manually — progress_pct is computed from the average of linked sc.infra.milestone records, each with its own progress percentage, target, completion flag, and expense amount.

sc.handover closes the sales cycle with SC.HDO/#### numbering, planned and actual dates, a condition assessment (Good, Minor Defect, Major Defect), and a checklist with attachment count. Completing a handover updates the kavling to handed over and the agreement to handed over in one action.

sc.facility tracks community assets — club house, swimming pool, gym, playground, mosque, park, security post, commercial area — with capacity, operational status, opening date, and monthly maintenance cost.

Example Flow: From Reservation to Monthly Billing

A buyer reserves kavling A-01 in Cluster A; its status moves to Reserved. Sales creates a PPJB with a 36-month installment scheme — the system computes DP amount and per-installment value from net price.

On approval, a sale order is created from the kavling type's linked product. Once the agreement is completed, the payment schedule generates a DP line plus 36 installment lines, each invoiceable separately.

After construction, a handover record is completed with a condition assessment and signed checklist; kavling and agreement both move to handed over. From the next period, the IPL bulk generator includes A-01 in monthly billing automatically.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the system handle multiple townships under different legal entities?

Yes. The accounting layer supports multi-company operation so each project can run under a separate company, with consolidation reports available across projects.

How are installment payments tracked against the agreement?

Each payment schedule line links to its own account.move invoice via the invoice_id field. The is_paid status is computed from the invoice payment state, so collection status reflects actual ledger data rather than a manually updated column.

Does infrastructure spending flow into the books?

Infrastructure items record both budget amount and actual cost, with expense amounts captured per milestone. The accounting feature set includes infrastructure capitalization to assets.

How should we evaluate cost and total cost of ownership?

Scope matters more than module count — count the processes you will actually run in year one, such as kavling sales, payment schedules, and IPL billing, and the number of users per role. Xerpium uses RBAC groups (Manager, User, Viewer), so licensing and training effort scale with how many people genuinely need edit rights. Ask any vendor to quote implementation, data migration, and ongoing support separately.

Is Indonesian property tax handled?

The accounting scope includes PPN 11%, PPh Final 4% for property, and e-Faktur. Coretax integration adds auto-generated e-Faktur PPN for kavling sales, PPh Final 4% calculation, NPWP validation, and invoice returns.

Can the sales approval workflow be customized?

The agreement workflow is configurable through Dynamic Stage, including Python hooks on state transitions. This lets you add approval conditions specific to your organization without replacing the standard state flow.
