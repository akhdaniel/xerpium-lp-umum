Complete ERP solution for Maintenance Service with Xerpium ERP

October 19, 2026

Maintenance service teams often run their operations across disconnected spreadsheets, standalone quoting tools, manual work order trackers, and separate invoicing processes. Customer equipment history lives in one place, actual material and labour costs in another, and preventive maintenance schedules in yet another. The result is slow quote-to-cash, weak control over actual versus estimated costs, and recurring missed service windows.

Xerpium solves this by bringing the entire maintenance service lifecycle into one end-to-end ERP scope. From customer equipment registration and service orders to work orders, checklists, actual cost capture, invoicing, and preventive schedule automation, every step stays connected with the back office.

Common challenges in Maintenance Service

  • Quotations are prepared manually without a direct link to the final work order or actual costs.
  • Customer equipment data is scattered, making service history and warranty tracking unreliable.
  • Actual material, on-site labour, workshop labour, equipment rental, subcontractor, and overhead costs are not systematically compared with estimates.
  • Preventive maintenance schedules are maintained outside the system, so overdue services are easy to miss.
  • Invoicing after service completion is delayed because actual cost lines must be re-entered manually.

Xerpium solution scope

Service Order and competitor tracking

Xerpium manages the full service order lifecycle. Each Service Order captures the customer, contact person, equipment to service, problem description, service type, urgency, priority, lead source, and requested and expected dates. Service types include Maintenance, Repair, Overhaul, Installation, and Inspection.

The system also supports competitor tracking with competitor name, quoted price, notes on strengths and weaknesses, and win/loss analysis. Service order actions follow a controlled sequence: Submit Estimate moves a draft into Estimated, Create Quotation moves it into Quoted and automatically generates a sale order, and Create Work Order moves it into a work order with technician and schedule assignment through a wizard. Orders can also be reset to draft or cancelled.

Work Order management and actual vs estimate

Each Work Order links back to the originating Service Order and related sale order, and carries the customer PO/SPK reference. Technicians are assigned through a Many2many employee relation, and scheduling fields track planned, start, end, and completion dates.

Actual cost capture is line-based. Xerpium tracks actual materials, actual on-site labour, actual workshop labour, actual equipment rental, actual subcontractor costs, and actual overhead. Each actual line can link to its corresponding estimate line, enabling automatic total estimate versus total actual comparison and profit margin calculation. Work order states move from Confirmed through In Progress, On Hold, Completed, Invoiced, and Closed, with cancel available from any state. The Create Invoice wizard pulls actual material, labour, and subcontractor lines automatically into the invoice; if no actual cost exists, a fallback single line is generated.

Customer equipment and rotating equipment catalog

Xerpium maintains a structured customer equipment register. Each unit stores equipment name and code, customer, link to the rotating equipment catalog, type, brand, serial number, model, installation location, installation date, warranty expiry, current condition, and technical specifications such as horsepower, RPM, and capacity. The system automatically calculates service count from work orders and updates the last service date.

The rotating equipment catalog complements this with standard equipment definitions. Catalog entries include equipment type, brand, model, horsepower, RPM, capacity, mounting type, full technical specifications, and common spare parts linked as product records. Equipment types and brands are maintained as controlled master data with unique name constraints.

Preventive maintenance schedules and checklists

Preventive maintenance is automated through schedule configuration. Each schedule defines the equipment, customer, interval type, interval value, service type, next service date, and last service date. Intervals can be based on days, months, or operating hours. When the Auto-Generate Work Order flag is enabled, a daily cron job detects overdue schedules, creates a draft Service Order with automatic description, submits the estimate, and recalculates the next service date. The source is tagged as Preventive Maintenance Schedule.

Checklist templates support structured inspections. Templates can be filtered by equipment type and contain ordered checklist line items with expected value, minimum and maximum acceptable range, unit of measure, critical item flag, and measurement instructions. During execution, technicians fill measured values, pass/fail status, photo attachments, and notes. The system auto-computes whether all checklist items passed.

Integrated back-office flows

Xerpium connects maintenance service with the wider ERP environment:

  • Sales: Creating a quotation from a Service Order automatically generates a sale order. Quotation line items come from estimate lines, and customer PO/SPK references flow into work orders.
  • Accounting: The invoice wizard generates an outbound account.move. Material, labour, and subcontractor lines flow automatically into invoice lines, and invoice status is tracked on the work order.
  • Inventory: Material lines reference product records for spare parts. The rotating equipment catalog lists common spare parts, and stock reservation supports work order material needs.
  • HR: Technician assignment uses the employee master data. Labour lines capture employee, manpower, days, and hours for workforce scheduling and dispatch.
  • Analytic: Cost center tracking is available per work order. Estimate versus actual variance is calculated per line, enabling profitability analysis per service order.
  • Purchase: Material requirements can trigger purchase requests, subcontractor work can be converted into purchase orders, and spare part procurement is supported.

End-to-end flow

A typical repair cycle starts when a customer reports an issue. The team creates a Service Order in Draft, selects the customer equipment, records the problem, sets the service type and urgency, and optionally captures competitor information. After submitting the estimate, the team creates a quotation, which automatically generates a sale order and carries the quotation lines from the estimate. When the customer approves, the Service Order moves into a Work Order with assigned technicians and schedule.

During execution, the technician starts the service, records actual material usage from inventory, logs on-site and workshop labour, and adds any equipment rental, subcontractor, or overhead lines. Each actual line is linked to the estimate line for variance analysis. Upon completion, the work order records the completion date. The invoice wizard then generates an outbound invoice with automatic line items for material, labour, and subcontractor costs. The work order moves to Invoiced and later to Closed. Customer equipment service count and last service date update automatically, and preventive schedules recalculate their next service dates.

Business benefits

  • Faster quote-to-cash through service order, quotation, work order, and invoice integration.
  • Accurate estimate versus actual comparison at line level for better margin control.
  • One customer equipment register with warranty, condition, location, and service history.
  • Automated preventive maintenance generation and overdue detection to reduce unplanned downtime.
  • Structured checklist execution with pass/fail results, critical item flags, and photo evidence.
  • Full alignment with sales, inventory, purchasing, HR, accounting, and analytic cost centers.

Implementation steps

  1. Prepare master data: Load equipment types, brands, rotating equipment catalog entries, common spare parts as product records, and customer equipment with serial numbers, warranty dates, locations, and current condition.
  2. Configure service parameters: Define service types, urgency levels, priorities, checklist templates, preventive schedules, journal and accounting defaults, stock locations, and technician employee assignments.
  3. Run a pilot: Select one service segment or customer group. Process service orders, quotations, work orders, checklists, actual cost capture, and invoicing. Validate variance analysis and preventive schedule automation.
  4. Roll out to the full team: Expand to all maintenance service operations and connect purchasing, inventory reservations, subcontractor purchase orders, accounting postings, and analytic cost centers.
  5. Evaluate and optimize: Review estimate versus actual variance, overdue schedules, work order status, and equipment lookup results. Refine checklists, interval values, spare part catalogs, and service type handling based on operational data.
