# Food & Beverage ERP: Unify Restaurant, Catering, and Food Manufacturing with Xerpium

Food and beverage businesses rarely operate in a single mode. A restaurant group may run dine-in service, takeaway, delivery, event catering, and a central production kitchen at the same time. Without a connected ERP, these operations create fragmented data: front-of-house orders do not flow cleanly into kitchen production, recipe costs drift, stock transfers between branches lack visibility, and franchise or loyalty activity sits outside financial controls.

Xerpium ERP addresses this with a purpose-built F&B suite covering restaurant operations, catering and events, food manufacturing, franchise management, loyalty and promotion, distribution and cold chain, and F&B-specific financial accounting. Below, we outline the common challenges and how the relevant Xerpium modules work together.

Common F&B operational challenges

  • Front-of-house and back-of-house systems are disconnected, causing order delays and limited kitchen visibility.
  • Multi-branch operators struggle to maintain consistent menus, recipes, stock transfers, and service standards.
  • Food cost is difficult to control without accurate recipe costing, batch tracking, and waste measurement.
  • Cold chain and expiry risks increase when temperature logs and shelf-life alerts are managed manually.
  • Franchise and loyalty operations become complex when agreements, royalties, points, tiers, and promotions are not linked to sales and billing.

How Xerpium ERP supports F&B operations

Restaurant operations: tables, orders, kitchen display, and shifts

The restaurant table management module records table code, name, floor or area, branch, capacity, and table type—regular, VIP, outdoor, bar, private room, or takeaway. Each table can have a QR code identifier for self-order and links to multiple POS configurations. Real-time status covers available, occupied, reserved, cleaning, and maintenance.

For order handling, Xerpium manages the workflow from draft to ordering, submitted, serving, and completed. Order types include dine-in, takeaway, delivery, and room service. Order lines capture product, quantity, price, discount, notes, and kitchen order links, with line states moving from pending to sent, preparing, ready, and served. Each order can track guest count, server assignment, and a link to the sale order for invoicing.

The kitchen display module provides a real-time KDS for the kitchen. Orders flow to kitchen stations such as hot, cold, grill, or pastry, with priorities from normal to urgent. Kitchen states move from new to in prep, quality check, ready, and served. The system logs date ordered, date started, and date ready, and supports actions to start prep, run quality check, and mark ready.

Shift management covers morning, afternoon, night, and full-day shifts. Staff assignments, shift manager, opening and closing notes, and shift date per branch are recorded. Shift states move from draft to open to closed, with metrics for total orders and total revenue per shift.

Catering and events

The catering module manages event orders for weddings, corporate events, and seminars. Each catering order includes event name, location, date, guest count, package type—buffet, plated, box, or prasmanan—and menu order lines. States move from draft to confirmed, preparing, delivered, and completed. Delivery tracking and equipment rental for items such as tables, chairs, and chafing dishes are also supported. Multi-event scheduling allows handling several events per day.

Food manufacturing: recipes, batches, quality, and shelf life

Recipe management records recipe code, name, product, yield quantity, ingredients, preparation steps, prep time, and cook time. Categories include main, appetizer, dessert, beverage, sauce, and base. Xerpium automatically calculates total cost from ingredients and tracks waste percentage per ingredient. Recipes can link to MRP BOMs for production.

Production batch management connects each batch to a recipe, product, kitchen, and branch. The batch workflow moves from draft to planned, in progress, completed, QC hold, or rejected. Lot or batch tracking, expiry date tracking, and MRP production links are supported. Quality checks link directly to production batches.

Quality check types include visual, taste, temperature, weight, microbiology, chemical, and packaging. Results can be pass, pass conditional, fail, or pending lab. Inspectors, check timestamps, and measurements such as temperature, actual weight, and standard weight are recorded.

Shelf-life tracking uses product, lot, production date, and expiry date to calculate shelf-life days and days remaining. States include fresh, expiring soon at seven days or fewer, critical at three days or fewer, and expired. Storage conditions cover ambient, chilled at 2–8°C, frozen at -18°C, and dry, with stock location and production batch links.

Franchise management, loyalty, and promotions

Franchise agreements capture the franchisee, operating unit branch, agreement number, territory exclusivity, fee structure, and date range. Fees can include initial fee, royalty percentage, marketing fund percentage, and annual renewal. Agreement states move from draft to active, suspended, or terminated, with subscription billing for recurring franchise fees.

Loyalty programs define program code, name, points per IDR, and IDR per point conversion. Tiers include bronze, silver, gold, and platinum, with tier benefits such as minimum and maximum points, discount percentage, and multiplier. New members can be auto-assigned a default tier.

Loyalty cards record card number, partner, program, current and lifetime points balance, current tier, issue date, and expiry date. Transactions support earn, redeem, adjust, and expire actions.

Promotions support discount percentage, buy X get Y, minimum purchase, happy hour, and combo types. Each promotion can define discount percentage, minimum purchase amount, applicable products, branches, schedule windows, day of week, and terms and conditions.

Distribution, cold chain, and stock controls

Inter-branch distribution plans manage transfers from a source branch to a destination branch. Plans include plan date, route, items with planned and loaded quantities, and states from draft to confirmed, loading, in transit, and delivered. The plans integrate with stock picking for warehouse transfers. Route planning provides estimated hours and distance.

Cold chain logs record temperature, timestamp, location checkpoint, transfer link, product, and operator. Minimum and maximum temperature thresholds can be set, with default values of 2°C and 8°C. The system automatically detects breaches when temperature exceeds the configured maximum. Stock alerts flag low stock, expiring soon, and expired items.

F&B financial accounting and dashboards

The F&B-specific chart of accounts separates revenue streams for dine-in, takeaway, delivery, and catering. Cost of goods sold can be tracked for food, beverage, and packaging. The system also supports depreciation for kitchen equipment, furniture, and POS systems, and deferred revenue for catering advance payments.

The F&B dashboard provides daily and monthly revenue per branch and service type, best-selling menu items, table occupancy and average dining time, kitchen performance metrics such as average prep time and completion rate, stock alerts, and loyalty analytics including new members and points earned and redeemed.

Business benefits of Xerpium for F&B

  • Unifies front-of-house, kitchen, inventory, finance, and loyalty in one system.
  • Reduces order errors and preparation delays through real-time kitchen display and order states.
  • Improves margin control with recipe costing, batch traceability, and waste tracking.
  • Strengthens multi-branch governance with distribution plans, cold chain logs, and franchise agreements.
  • Connects loyalty and promotions directly to sales and financial records.
  • Provides F&B-specific financial views, including separate revenue streams and COGS categories.

Example flow: from table order to central production and billing

A guest scans the QR code at a restaurant table. The table status changes to occupied and the self-order creates a dine-in order. Order lines are sent to the relevant kitchen station, where staff move them from pending to sent and preparing. For a menu item produced in a central kitchen, a production batch is created from the recipe, with lot tracking, expiry date, and quality checks. The finished items are transferred through a distribution plan with cold chain temperature logs to the branch. When the guest pays, the sale order is linked to the dine-in revenue stream, and loyalty points are earned on the member card.

Conclusion

Xerpium ERP brings F&B-specific depth to enterprise resource planning. It connects daily restaurant service, kitchen execution, catering, food manufacturing, franchise management, loyalty, distribution, and financial accounting in a single system. For F&B operators managing multiple branches or production kitchens, that integration reduces manual coordination and improves visibility from table to ledger.
