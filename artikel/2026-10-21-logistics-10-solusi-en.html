10 Best ERP Solutions for Logistics Operations

October 21, 2026

Logistics operations often run on disconnected spreadsheets, manual phone calls, and fragmented systems for shipments, vehicles, containers, and delivery proof. The result is late data, duplicate entries, margin leakage, and no single source of truth for route costs, asset status, or delivery exceptions. A logistics-focused ERP addresses this by connecting shipment execution, pricing, assets, warehouse handling, and last-mile confirmation in one operational backbone. The ten solutions below are selected from Xerpium’s logistics modules because they directly control physical movement, asset utilization, execution, and delivery verification.

The 10 Solutions at a Glance

  1. Shipment — Centralizes shipment types, service types, routes, vehicles, and full status flow.
  2. Route — Plans origin-destination pairs with distance, transit time, route type, and estimated cost.
  3. Rate Card — Automates shipping price calculation by weight, volume, distance, and vehicle type.
  4. Shipment Operations — Manages shipment lines, schedules, actual times, costs, and invoicing.
  5. Fleet Management — Tracks vehicles, drivers, assignments, maintenance, fuel, and utilization.
  6. Container Management — Handles container master data, movement, status, certification, and FCL integration.
  7. Freight Forwarding — Supports multi-modal transport, consolidation, documentation, and customs tracking.
  8. Warehouse Ops — Organizes zones, cross-docking, putaway, picking, and inter-warehouse stock transfer.
  9. Real-time Tracking — Records geo-located tracking events with timestamps, photos, and status indicators.
  10. Proof of Delivery — Captures recipient details, digital signature, POD photos, and auto-completes delivery.

1. Shipment

The Shipment module acts as the operational core for every logistics movement. It supports shipment types such as pickup, delivery, transfer, return, and multimodal, alongside service types including trucking, forwarding, warehousing, last-mile, project, and cold chain. Each shipment follows a defined state flow from draft to confirmed, assigned, in transit, delivered, and completed, with full origin and destination details. The module links each shipment to a route and assigns a vehicle and driver from fleet and partner records. This replaces scattered shipment logs with one structured record that operations, finance, and customers can trust.

2. Route

Route management removes guesswork from lane planning. The module stores origin and destination cities with auto-generated names, distance in kilometers, transit time in hours, and route type covering road, sea, air, rail, and multimodal. It also tracks estimated cost per route and counts how many shipments use that lane. This gives logistics teams a reusable library of known routes with consistent distance and cost baselines. Planners can compare lanes, identify expensive or slow corridors, and avoid re-entering the same route details for every shipment, reducing data entry and improving cost accuracy.

3. Rate Card

The Rate Card module eliminates manual price quoting and inconsistent billing. It supports pricing dimensions by kilometer, kilogram, cubic meter, and hour, with nine vehicle types from motorcycle to container. Each rate includes a minimum charge and a validity period, ensuring outdated prices are not applied. The module provides an auto-compute function, compute_shipping_price(weight, volume, distance), that calculates charges immediately from shipment data. Logistics operators solve the problem of under-quoting complex jobs and over-charging simple ones by enforcing a single, versioned pricing structure across all shipment types and customers.

4. Shipment Operations

Shipment Operations turns a shipment record into a controlled execution and financial document. It manages multiple shipment lines with item, weight, volume, and price per line, then automatically sums total weight, volume, pieces, and price. The module records scheduled pickup and delivery datetimes and captures actual pickup and delivery times for tracking. Profit margin is computed automatically as revenue minus actual cost, and finance can create an invoice directly from a shipment using action_create_invoice. This closes the gap between operations and accounting, giving real-time margin visibility on every job.

5. Fleet Management

Fleet Management connects vehicles and drivers to shipment execution and cost control. It integrates with the vehicle master and manages drivers using an is_driver flag, allowing assignment of a specific vehicle and driver to each shipment. The module tracks maintenance service schedules, fuel consumption, and operational costs, then provides fleet utilization analytics. Logistics teams solve the problem of idle assets, unexpected breakdowns, and hidden fuel or maintenance expenses by seeing which vehicles are assigned, how often they are used, and what each trip costs before and after the fact.

6. Container Management

Container Management gives logistics operators full control over container assets. It supports container types such as 20ft, 40ft, 40HC, and 45HC with ISO standard dimensions and ten categories including dry van, reefer, open top, flat rack, and tank. The module maintains a container master with ISO 6346 numbers, ownership status, and statuses from available to in transit, under maintenance, damaged, lost, or retired. It records location tracking, gate in/out movements, loading, discharge, preventive and corrective maintenance, certification expiry, purchase price, current value, lease rate, and insurance. Full container load shipments integrate through container_ids, linking containers directly to shipment records.

7. Freight Forwarding

Freight Forwarding handles multi-modal international and domestic moves that combine road, sea, air, and rail. The module supports consolidation of multiple shipments into a single container and tracks documentation such as bill of lading, airway bill, and packing list. It also manages customs tracking with PIB/PEB records for international shipments and captures cost components including ocean freight, handling, and documentation fees. This replaces fragmented forwarding spreadsheets with one system that shows the complete move, the documents required, the customs status, and the true landed cost of every consolidated shipment.

8. Warehouse Ops

Warehouse Ops brings structure to storage and fulfillment activities that feed logistics movements. It defines warehouse zones, enables cross-docking for fast transfer from inbound to outbound, and applies putaway strategies to optimize storage allocation. The module supports wave picking and batch picking for outbound orders and manages inter-warehouse stock transfers. Logistics teams solve the problem of delayed picking, misplaced goods, and slow cross-dock handoffs by having a clear, executable warehouse layer that connects directly to shipment and inventory flows, reducing dwell time and handling errors.

9. Real-time Tracking

Real-time Tracking gives internal teams and customers a live operational feed. It records event types including created, confirmed, picked up, in transit, at hub, out for delivery, delivered, exception, and returned. Each event captures latitude and longitude, a real-time timestamp, optional photo evidence, and a status color indicator showing normal, warning, or danger. The module also records which driver or hub personnel reported the event. This solves the problem of invisible shipments and late exception alerts by making the last known location, condition, and status visible without calling drivers or checking separate apps.

10. Proof of Delivery

Proof of Delivery closes the last mile with verifiable evidence. The module captures recipient name, company, and phone number, records the actual delivery datetime, and stores a digital signature from the recipient. It also accepts multiple delivery photos as POD evidence and tracks delivery status as pending, delivered, partial, or refused. When the POD is confirmed, the module automatically completes the shipment and marks it delivered. This eliminates delivery disputes, missing signature claims, and manual delivery confirmation calls by providing a complete, time-stamped, photo-backed record of what was delivered and who accepted it.

How to Choose the Right Solution

Start by mapping your physical flows. If you run multimodal freight and containers, prioritize Freight Forwarding and Container Management. If you handle last-mile and delivery confirmation, Real-time Tracking and Proof of Delivery become critical. Second, check how tightly the ERP connects shipment execution to finance. Shipment Operations, Rate Card, and accounting integration determine whether you can see real margin per shipment and route. Third, assess your asset intensity. Fleet Management and Container Management matter more when you own or lease vehicles and containers than when you fully outsource. Finally, consider exception handling and specialized cargo. If claims or temperature-controlled freight are core to your operation, evaluate how the ERP supports claim management and cold chain modules alongside the ten core solutions above.

Conclusion

Logistics operations cannot run efficiently on disconnected spreadsheets and manual status calls. The ten ERP solutions covered here — Shipment, Route, Rate Card, Shipment Operations, Fleet Management, Container Management, Freight Forwarding, Warehouse Ops, Real-time Tracking, and Proof of Delivery — provide a unified operational backbone from pricing and planning through execution, asset control, visibility, and delivery proof.
