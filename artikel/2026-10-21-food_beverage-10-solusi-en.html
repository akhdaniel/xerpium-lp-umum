10 Best ERP Solutions for Food & Beverage (F&B) Operations

October 21, 2026

Food and beverage operations fail when the front of house, kitchen, production, and distribution teams work from disconnected spreadsheets, paper tickets, and manual logs. A table status is not visible to the kitchen, recipe costs are not linked to batch production, expiry dates are guessed, and inter-branch transfers disappear into phone calls. Those gaps create food waste, slow service, inconsistent quality, and weak traceability. The ten solutions below come from the Xerpium F&B suite and are selected for their direct impact on daily restaurant, catering, production, and distribution operations.

The 10 Solutions at a Glance

  1. fnb.restaurant.table — Real-time table status, capacity, table types, QR self-order, and multi-branch mapping.
  2. fnb.restaurant.order — Structured dine-in, takeaway, delivery, and room-service workflows linked to invoicing.
  3. fnb.kitchen.order — Kitchen display with priority, station assignment, and order states from new to served.
  4. fnb.restaurant.shift — Staff assignment, opening/closing notes, and shift-level order and revenue metrics.
  5. fnb.catering.order — Event catering orders with packages, equipment rental, and delivery tracking.
  6. fnb.recipe — Standardized recipes, ingredients, waste percentage, and automatic cost calculation.
  7. fnb.production.batch — Batch production, lot tracking, expiry dates, and MRP integration.
  8. fnb.quality.check — Visual, taste, temperature, weight, and lab checks with structured pass/fail results.
  9. fnb.distribution.plan — Inter-branch transfer plans with planned/loaded quantities and delivery states.
  10. fnb.shelf.life — Expiry tracking, shelf-life day calculation, and fresh/expiring/critical/expired alerts.

1. fnb.restaurant.table — Table Management

fnb.restaurant.table manages table code, name, floor/area, and branch, along with seating capacity and min/max pax. It supports table types such as regular, VIP, outdoor, bar, private room, and takeaway, and tracks real-time status across available, occupied, reserved, cleaning, and maintenance. Each table can carry a QR code for self-order and can link to multiple POS configurations under the operating unit. This gives front-of-house staff and managers one live view of floor availability, preventing double seating and lost QR orders.

2. fnb.restaurant.order — Order Workflow and POS Integration

fnb.restaurant.order handles the order workflow from Draft to Ordering, Submitted, Serving, and Completed. It records dine-in, takeaway, delivery, and room-service orders, including guest count and order lines with product, quantity, price, discount, notes, and kitchen order links. Line states move from Pending to Sent, Preparing, Ready, and Served, while server assignment links to a user and sale order links to invoicing. This removes manual order tickets by keeping POS, kitchen, and accounting connected through one structured order record.

3. fnb.kitchen.order — Kitchen Display and Order Preparation

fnb.kitchen.order provides a real-time kitchen display for kitchen stations. It supports priority levels normal, medium, high, and urgent, with states from New to In Prep, Quality Check, Ready, and Served. The module links order lines to restaurant order lines, records date ordered, date started, and date ready, and assigns work to kitchens such as hot, cold, grill, and pastry. Actions include start prep, quality check, and mark ready, giving kitchen teams clear sequencing and managers visibility into pending and delayed items.

4. fnb.restaurant.shift — Shift Management and Revenue Metrics

fnb.restaurant.shift structures morning, afternoon, night, and full-day shifts. It assigns staff through many2many users, records a shift manager, and stores opening and closing notes. The state moves from Draft to Open to Closed, and the module captures total orders and total revenue per shift by date and branch. This solves accountability gaps by replacing paper shift logs with a record that ties staff coverage, handover notes, and shift-level performance to operational results.

5. fnb.catering.order — Catering and Event Operations

fnb.catering.order manages event catering for weddings, corporate events, and seminars. It stores event name, location, date, and guest count, and supports packages such as buffet, plated, box, and prasmanan. Order lines include menu items, quantity, and price, while the state moves from Draft to Confirmed, Preparing, Delivered, and Completed. Delivery tracking and equipment rental for tables, chairs, and chafing dishes keep event logistics visible, reducing missed details and unreturned equipment.

6. fnb.recipe — Recipe Management and Cost Control

fnb.recipe manages recipe code, name, product, yield quantity, and ingredients with product, quantity, and unit cost. It includes preparation steps, prep time, cook time, and categories such as main, appetizer, dessert, beverage, sauce, and base. The module automatically calculates total cost from ingredients, applies waste percentage per ingredient, and integrates with MRP BOM for production. This standardizes portions and exposes food cost before production, helping operators prevent margin erosion caused by inconsistent recipes.

7. fnb.production.batch — Batch Production and Lot Traceability

fnb.production.batch tracks production batches per recipe with recipe, product, kitchen, and branch. It integrates with MRP production, links to stock lot tracking, and records expiry dates for shelf life. The state moves from Draft to Planned, In Progress, Completed, QC Hold, and Rejected, with quality checks linked per batch. This creates traceability from a production batch to its stock lot and expiry date, reducing recall scope and preventing failed or unreleased batches from entering service.

8. fnb.quality.check — Quality Control and Food Safety Records

fnb.quality.check supports quality checks for visual, taste, temperature, weight, microbiology, chemical, and packaging. Results include pass, pass conditional, fail, and pending lab, with measurements for temperature, actual weight, and standard weight. Inspectors are assigned per check, and records link to production batch with date and inspector notes. This makes food safety checks repeatable and auditable, creating objective records that help teams act on deviations instead of relying on memory.

9. fnb.distribution.plan — Inter-Branch Distribution and Transfer

fnb.distribution.plan manages branch transfers from a source branch to a destination branch. It stores plan date and route, with line items for product, planned quantity, and loaded quantity. The state moves from Draft to Confirmed, Loading, In Transit, and Delivered, and the module links to stock picking for warehouse transfer. This gives central kitchens and outlets a clear record of what is planned, loaded, and received, reducing stock shortages and manual transfer reconciliation.

10. fnb.shelf.life — Expiry Tracking and Shelf-Life Alerts

fnb.shelf.life tracks expiry by product, lot, production date, and expiry date. It computes shelf-life days and days remaining, with states fresh, expiring soon at seven days or less, critical at three days or less, and expired. Storage options include ambient, chilled at 2–8°C, frozen at -18°C, and dry, with stock location and batch links. This surfaces expiring inventory before it becomes unsellable, helping F&B teams reduce write-offs and protect margin.

How to Choose the Right Solution

Start by mapping your primary operational mode. Service-driven operators should prioritize table management, order workflow, kitchen display, and shift management. Production-driven operators should prioritize recipe management, production batch, quality check, and shelf life. Catering businesses should evaluate event order and equipment rental workflows early.

Second, treat traceability as a non-negotiable selection criterion. Batch production, quality check, shelf life, and distribution plan together create the audit trail needed for food safety and recall readiness.

Third, confirm that the solution supports multi-branch operations. Look for operating unit linkage, inter-branch transfer, stock picking integration, and branch-level metrics. A single-branch system creates rework if you add outlets or central production later.

Fourth, choose modules that follow your actual workflows. If kitchen states or order stages differ, the system should allow configurable stages and clear handoffs between POS, kitchen, production, and delivery.

Conclusion

These ten ERP modules cover the core F&B operational chain, from table and order capture to kitchen execution, recipe costing, batch production, quality control, distribution, and shelf-life management.
