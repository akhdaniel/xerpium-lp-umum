10 Best ERP Solutions for Fishery Operations

October 21, 2026

Fishery operations face a unique set of coordination problems: vessel departures and catch estimates, dock receiving with temperature and freshness checks, grading and pricing, processing yields, cold storage integrity, and export traceability. When these functions run on disconnected spreadsheets or separate point solutions, the same fish lot is re-entered multiple times, grade and price decisions become inconsistent, cold chain gaps appear too late, and accounting cannot tie cost back to a specific vessel or trip. An integrated ERP approach for fishery operations should connect the catch-to-cash cycle—from vessel and trip to receiving, processing, cold storage, traceability and financial posting—without forcing seafood companies to invent workarounds. Below are 10 ERP solutions built for that operational reality, selected from the Xerpium fishery modules and ordered roughly along the physical flow of fish.

The 10 Solutions at a Glance

  1. Fish Vessel — centralizes vessel registration, captain, capacity and fleet integration for trip readiness.
  2. Fishing Trip Management — tracks the full trip lifecycle, crew shares, catch estimates, costs and profitability.
  3. Fish Receiving & Grading — enforces weigh-in, temperature, freshness and grade rules at receiving.
  4. Processing Batch — manages yield, outputs, byproducts, waste and MO integration per processing batch.
  5. Cold Storage & Cold Chain — monitors cold rooms, thresholds, capacity and IoT temperature logs.
  6. Lot & Traceability — links species, vessel, trip, area and grade into parent-child traceable lots.
  7. QC Process Flow — automates grade computation, reject/hold rules and QC status per line item.
  8. Auto Pricing System — computes per-kg price by supplier, grade, size and seasonal date range.
  9. Fishery ↔ Accounting — auto-creates vendor bills, posts picking journals, accrues crew costs and tracks cost per lot.
  10. AI Dashboard Features — generates dashboards and SQL from natural language, with chart insights and copilot chat.

1. Fish Vessel

Fish Vessel provides a central vessel registry for fishery operations, capturing registration number, vessel type, captain relation to hr.employee, and nominal carrying capacity in kilograms. It integrates directly with fleet.vehicle, so vessel maintenance scheduling and operational status are linked to the same record used in trip management. For fishery companies running multiple boats, this removes duplicate vessel data and gives trip planners, receiving clerks and accountants one reliable reference for capacity, captain assignment and fleet upkeep. It solves the operational problem of fragmented vessel information that delays departure planning and cost allocation.

2. Fishing Trip Management

Fishing Trip Management handles the full trip lifecycle from Draft → Departed → Returned → Received → Closed. It manages crew roles such as captain, engineer and crew with share percentages, records catch estimates per species against actual weight, and tracks fuel, ice, logistics, wage and maintenance costs. The system automatically calculates total cost, revenue and profit per trip. This replaces manual trip spreadsheets with a single operational control point, giving fishery managers real visibility into which vessels, areas and crew combinations are profitable, and where fuel or ice consumption is out of line before losses accumulate.

3. Fish Receiving & Grading

Fish Receiving & Grading supports the receiving flow from draft through weighing, QC, approved and posted, with gross/tare/net tracked per line item. It logs temperature and freshness score at receiving, and uses auto-grade rules based on temperature and freshness to assign grades. Pricing rules then compute price per species, grade and size. This solves the problem of subjective manual grading and inconsistent pricing at the dock. Clerks get a repeatable, rule-driven receiving flow that captures catch condition, weight and value in one step, feeding traceability and accounting without rekeying.

4. Processing Batch

Processing Batch manages yield from input weight to output products and waste with yield percentages. It supports process types such as fillet, loin, steak, block, whole frozen and fish meal, multiple output lines, byproducts and waste tracking. Expected yield per species is compared against actual, and the system can auto-create mrp.production from a batch after a required QC step. This gives production managers a clear view of recovery efficiency, byproduct value and batch-level material consumption, replacing end-of-day manual yield calculations that hide shrinkage and profitability gaps.

5. Cold Storage & Cold Chain

Cold Storage & Cold Chain manages chilled, frozen and blast freezer rooms with minimum and maximum temperature thresholds and automatic alerts. It tracks capacity in kilograms against current weight and utilization percentage, supports IoT sensor integration for automatic temperature logging, and records temperature history by source such as manual, IoT or import, including status. Cold chain events are tracked from receiving through storage, transfer and delivery. For fishery operators, this reduces spoilage risk and audit pressure by maintaining continuous temperature evidence and early warning before a cold room drifts out of range.

6. Lot & Traceability

Lot & Traceability extends stock lots with fishery-specific fields: species, vessel, trip, area, grade and temperature. It maintains parent-child relationships for upstream and downstream traceability, auto-generates QR codes for public traceability, and publishes a public URL per lot or QR. Full traceability runs from vessel to receiving to processing to delivery to customer, with cold chain events recorded per lot. This solves recall and buyer confidence problems by letting seafood companies prove exactly which trip, vessel, area and cold chain conditions produced a specific finished lot, without manual paper records.

7. QC Process Flow

QC Process Flow computes grades automatically through sequence-based grading rules using species, temperature and freshness score. The first matching rule selects the grade; if no grade matches, the line can be auto-rejected or placed on hold. Each receiving line item moves through pending → pass/hold/reject status. This brings consistent, defensible quality decisions to fishery receiving and processing, reducing disputes with suppliers and customers. It also turns freshness scoring into an operational control, not a subjective note, so borderline catch can be routed correctly before it enters cold storage or production.

8. Auto Pricing System

Auto Pricing System calculates price per kilogram from the most specific matching rule, using priority logic: supplier match > grade match > size match > default. It supports different prices per supplier for the same species and seasonal date ranges with date_start and date_end. A compute price button applies the rule at the receiving line. For fishery buyers, this eliminates manual price lookups and ensures dock prices follow negotiated supplier-grade-size terms automatically. It connects receiving to payable valuations and cost per lot, even when prices change by season or supplier.

9. Fishery ↔ Accounting

Fishery ↔ Accounting connects operational fish transactions to finance by auto-creating vendor bills from receiving, posting picking and stock movement journals, accruing crew costs per trip, and tracking cost per lot or traceability batch. Multi-currency export transactions are supported, and analytic accounts can follow vessel and trip for cost center tracking. This removes the classic gap between dock operations and the general ledger: receiving, processing and trip costs flow into accounting without manual journal entries, and managers can see margin by vessel, trip or lot instead of a blended company average.

10. AI Dashboard Features

AI Dashboard Features turns natural language into complete dashboards via DeepSeek, converts natural language to real-time SQL queries, and provides chart insights that analyze trends, peaks, anomalies and recommendations. A copilot chat streams context-aware responses with the active dashboard, and conditional formatting highlights values dynamically. For fishery operations, this means operations managers and executives can ask questions about trip profitability, cold storage utilization, yield outliers or supplier pricing in plain language and get live, visual answers without waiting for a BI specialist to build a report.

How to Choose the Right Solution

Match each solution to your biggest loss point first. If spoilage or cold chain failures are the main risk, prioritize Cold Storage & Cold Chain and QC Process Flow. If trip costs are unclear, start with Fishing Trip Management and Fish Vessel. If buyers demand export traceability, Lot & Traceability and Auto Pricing System should come early.

Check integration boundaries before committing. Receiving, processing, accounting and traceability must share one lot master. Evaluate solutions that natively link lot, stock movements and journal entries rather than forcing separate add-ons to be stitched together later.

Assess your traceability depth. Export buyers may require public QR codes and cold chain evidence, while domestic processors may only need batch yield and grade. Avoid paying for traceability depth you will not use, but do not block export growth.

Finally, consider reporting fit. Rule-driven grading, pricing and natural-language dashboards should match the skills of the staff who will use them daily, especially in seasonal peak periods when manual work is most error-prone.

Conclusion

Fishery operations cannot afford disconnected systems between the vessel, the dock, the cold room and the ledger. The 10 solutions above form a connected catch-to-cash chain: vessel and trip management, receiving and grading, processing yield, cold chain integrity, traceability, quality, pricing, accounting and AI-driven visibility.
