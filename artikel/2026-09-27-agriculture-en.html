# Agriculture ERP: How Xerpium Streamlines Harvest Accounting, Biological Assets, and Farm Operations

Agribusinesses in Indonesia operate in a uniquely complex environment. Plantation and farming companies must comply with agricultural accounting standards, manage biological assets such as productive and consumptive plants, track harvest costs separately from other expenses, and coordinate seasonal labor across planting and harvest cycles. Without a centralized system, finance teams struggle with fragmented records, management lacks real-time visibility into revenue and input costs, and operational planning becomes reactive rather than proactive.

Xerpium ERP addresses these challenges with a dedicated agriculture solution that combines localized accounting, agriculture-specific financial reporting, harvest analytics, and workforce management. This article explores how Xerpium supports agribusiness operations from the chart of accounts to the field.

Common Challenges in Agricultural Operations

Agricultural companies often face a distinct set of operational and financial difficulties:

  • Compliance with agricultural accounting standards for biological assets, harvest costs, and government subsidies.
  • Separate tracking of harvest COGS from other operational costs, especially when managing multiple commodities.
  • Input cost management for fertilizers, seeds, and pesticides across different plots and growing cycles.
  • Labor and equipment coordination for permanent and seasonal workers during planting, maintenance, and harvest periods.
  • Limited real-time visibility into revenue, receivables, stock value, and pending purchase orders.

These challenges require more than a generic ERP. They require a system that understands agricultural chart of accounts, biological asset grouping, harvest dashboards, and field operations.

Xerpium ERP Agriculture Modules

Xerpium provides several integrated modules designed specifically for agriculture and plantation businesses.

Localized Agriculture Chart of Accounts

The l10n_id_agriculture module delivers a chart of accounts template built for agriculture and plantation operations in Indonesia. It uses a 6-digit account code structure following Indonesian standards and organizes accounts according to SAK pertanian (agricultural accounting standards).

Key account defaults include:

  • Receivable and payable accounts: ag_120100 for accounts receivable and ag_210100 for accounts payable.
  • Income and expense accounts: ag_410100 for revenue and ag_510100 for cost of goods sold.
  • Tax defaults: PPN 11% pre-configured for both sale and purchase transactions.
  • Currency exchange accounts: ag_710200 for exchange gains and ag_720200 for exchange losses.
  • Cash difference accounts: ag_710300 for cash difference income and ag_720300 for cash difference expense.

The agriculture chart of accounts also includes account groups for biological assets, harvest costs, government subsidies for fertilizer and seeds, land management costs such as rent and land tax, and depreciation of agricultural equipment. Journal defaults for sales and purchases are configured with refund_sequence=True, supporting consistent transaction numbering.

Agriculture Financial Reporting

The report_id_agriculture module provides report structures aligned with agricultural standards. The Balance Sheet includes a biological asset group for productive and consumptive plants or livestock. The Profit & Loss statement separates harvest COGS from input costs, while the Cash Flow report reflects operational agriculture cash flow, investment activities such as land acquisition, and financing activities.

This module automatically syncs account_group_ids from the COA template. It parses vit_report_structure_agriculture.xml for mapping and uses a post-load hook (_post_load_data) to sync data whenever the chart template is applied.

Additional financial reports available in Xerpium agriculture include:

  • Harvest Report – harvest results per period.
  • Input Report – purchases of inputs such as fertilizer, seeds, and pesticides.
  • Land Usage Report – land usage per commodity.

Harvest Dashboard and Analytics

The vit_dashboard_agriculture module provides a dedicated harvest dashboard for agriculture and plantation KPIs. It includes 8 KPI tiles:

  • Total Revenue Panen – SUM(amount_total) from sale_order with state sale or done.
  • Total Penjualan – COUNT of sale order transactions.
  • Biaya Input – SUM of purchase orders for fertilizer, seeds, and pesticides.
  • Piutang Pembeli – SUM(amount_residual) from unpaid out invoices.
  • Tenaga Kerja – COUNT of active employees from hr_employee.
  • Nilai Stok Hasil Panen – SUM of stock quant value at internal locations.
  • PO Input Pending – COUNT of purchase orders with state draft, sent, or to approve.
  • Invoice Bulan Ini – COUNT of out invoices in the current month.

The dashboard also includes 4 charts showing monthly revenue, cost trends, top commodities, and top buyers. Data is sourced from ORM models such as sale_order, purchase_order, stock_quant, hr_employee, and account_move, as well as SQL sources for monthly revenue and cost trends and top product/buyer analytics.

A global date filter applies automatically, and the dashboard refreshes every 5 minutes. The layout uses a 12-column responsive grid from y=0 to y=14, with a green color theme designed for agribusiness.

Analytics capabilities extend beyond the dashboard:

  • Revenue per commodity – top 10 agricultural products by revenue.
  • Monthly trend – 12-month revenue and cost trend chart.
  • Top buyers – 10 largest buyers, including distributors, markets, and exporters.
  • Worker metrics – active workers per department.
  • Stock valuation – value of harvested stock in warehouses.
  • Harvest cycle tracking – per product.
  • Input cost – fertilizer, seed, and pesticide costs per month.

HR and Field Operations

Xerpium supports agricultural workforce and operational management:

  • Worker management for permanent and seasonal employees.
  • Payroll with daily or contract-based wages for harvest workers.
  • Attendance for field and plantation workers.
  • Departments organized per commodity or land area.
  • Equipment tracking for agricultural tools such as tractors and sprayers.
  • Calendar for planting schedules, harvest schedules, and fertilizer application.

Business Benefits

Implementing Xerpium ERP for agriculture delivers several measurable benefits:

  • Accounting compliance with localized 6-digit COA, SAK pertanian structure, and PPN 11% tax defaults.
  • Accurate biological asset valuation through dedicated account groups in the balance sheet.
  • Clear separation of harvest COGS from other expenses for better margin analysis.
  • Real-time operational visibility through 8 KPI tiles and 4 analytics charts on the harvest dashboard.
  • Improved input cost control for fertilizer, seeds, and pesticides across commodities.
  • Streamlined workforce scheduling for daily and seasonal agricultural labor.

Example Use Case: Managing a Harvest Cycle

A plantation manager prepares for a new harvest cycle. Using Xerpium’s agricultural calendar, the team schedules planting and fertilizer application. Purchase orders for seeds, fertilizer, and pesticides are recorded and tracked as pending input orders on the dashboard.

When harvest begins, sale orders are created for distributors and market buyers. The dashboard immediately updates total revenue panen, input costs, and accounts receivable. At month-end, finance generates the agriculture P&L, which shows harvest revenue in ag_410100 and harvest COGS in ag_510100. The balance sheet reflects biological asset values, and the cash flow report separates operational cash from land investment and financing activities.

Meanwhile, HR processes daily or contract-based payroll for seasonal harvest workers, and equipment usage is tracked across departments. The result is a single source of truth for financial, operational, and field data.
