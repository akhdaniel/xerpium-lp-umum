10 Best ERP Solutions for Logistics Operations

November 19, 2026

Logistics teams rarely fail because of a lack of effort. They fail because information is scattered. Pickup schedules live in spreadsheets, driver assignments sit in chat groups, container status is tracked on a whiteboard at the depot, and proof of delivery arrives as a blurry photo days after the trip is closed. When finance finally issues the invoice, nobody can reconcile the quoted rate against the actual cost of the trip. The result is familiar: disputed invoices, margin leakage per route, idle vehicles, late claims, and customers who call to ask where their shipment is because no one can tell them.

An ERP built for logistics closes those gaps by keeping shipment, fleet, warehouse, tracking, and accounting data in one record chain. The ten solutions below are drawn from the Xerpium Logistics ecosystem. They were selected because each one maps to a daily operational task — booking, costing, dispatch, handover, documentation, or compliance — rather than to a back-office reporting need alone. Supporting components such as Service Category accounting mapping and Claim Management are referenced where they intersect with these core modules.

The 10 Solutions at a Glance

  1. Shipment (logistics.shipment) — a single record that carries an order from draft to completion, with totals and margin computed automatically.
  2. Route (logistics.route) — standardises origin–destination lanes with distance, transit time, and estimated cost.
  3. Rate Card (logistics.rate.card) — turns quoting into a calculation instead of a negotiation guess.
  4. Fleet Management (xerpium_logistics_fleet) — assigns vehicles and drivers while tracking maintenance and utilisation.
  5. Container Management (logistics.container) — full lifecycle control of containers, movements, and certification.
  6. Freight Forwarding (xerpium_logistics_freight) — handles multi-modal legs, consolidation, and customs documents.
  7. Warehouse Ops (xerpium_logistics_warehouse) — zones, cross-docking, putaway, and picking strategies in one place.
  8. Real-time Tracking (logistics.tracking) — geo-tagged event history that answers customer status queries instantly.
  9. Proof of Delivery (logistics.proof.of.delivery) — digital signature and photo capture that closes shipments cleanly.
  10. Cold Chain (xerpium_logistics_cold_chain) — temperature monitoring and alerts for temperature-sensitive cargo.

1. Shipment (logistics.shipment)

The shipment record is the operational backbone. It defines the shipment type — pickup, delivery, transfer, return, or multimodal — and the service type, covering trucking, forwarding, warehousing, last-mile, project, and cold chain work. Each shipment moves through a controlled state flow from Draft to Confirmed, Assigned, In Transit, Delivered, and Completed, with origin and destination addresses, an assigned route, and a linked vehicle and driver. Shipment lines hold multiple items with weight, volume, and price, and totals are summed automatically. Scheduled versus actual pickup and delivery times are recorded, profit margin is computed from revenue minus actual cost, and invoices are generated directly from the shipment.

2. Route (logistics.route)

Most costing disputes start with inconsistent lane definitions. The Route module fixes origin and destination cities with auto-generated naming, then attaches distance in kilometres and transit time in hours to each lane. Route type covers road, sea, air, rail, and multimodal, so the same framework serves domestic trucking and international legs. An estimated cost is held per route, giving planners a baseline to compare against actual spend, while shipment count shows how heavily each lane is used. Over time this produces a reliable lane catalogue instead of ad-hoc routing decisions made trip by trip, and it feeds profitability analysis per route.

3. Rate Card (logistics.rate.card)

Quoting breaks down when every salesperson applies a different formula. The Rate Card module supports pricing dimensions by kilometre, kilogram, cubic metre, and hour, so volumetric and distance-based charges follow one consistent logic. Rates are defined against vehicle types spanning motorcycle through to container across nine categories, with a minimum charge per shipment to protect short-haul economics. Validity periods with valid-from and valid-to dates keep seasonal or contractual pricing under control. The compute_shipping_price function derives the price from weight, volume, and distance automatically, and the result flows into sales orders and shipment lines without manual recalculation.

4. Fleet Management (xerpium_logistics_fleet)

Vehicles are the largest controllable cost in most trucking operations, and the least visible. This module integrates the vehicle master through fleet.vehicle and manages drivers as partner records flagged with is_driver, so assignment of both vehicle and driver happens inside the shipment itself rather than through a separate dispatch sheet. Maintenance is handled through service schedules and fuel tracking, which prevents reactive repairs from disrupting committed deliveries. Fleet usage analytics expose utilisation patterns — which units are over-deployed and which are idle. Maintenance, fuel, and driver costs are then allocated back to shipments for accurate per-trip costing.

5. Container Management (logistics.container)

Container operators lose money through units they cannot locate and certifications they discover have expired. The container master records ISO 6346 numbers, ownership as owned, leased, or customer, and type across 20ft, 40ft, 40HC, and 45HC with ISO standard dimensions, spanning ten categories including dry van, reefer, open top, flat rack, tank, and car carrier. Status covers available, in use, in transit, under maintenance, damaged, lost, and retired, with location tracking across depot, port, terminal, warehouse, and customer site. Movements log gate in/out, transfer, loading, and discharge with before and after condition, alongside maintenance, CSC plate certification expiry, and financial values.

6. Freight Forwarding (xerpium_logistics_freight)

Forwarding work involves handoffs between parties that each keep their own records, which is where documents and costs go missing. This module manages multi-modal movements combining road, sea, air, and rail within a single job, and supports consolidation of multiple shipments into one container. Documentation is held against the record, including Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, and packing list, while customs handling covers PIB and PEB tracking for international traffic. Cost tracking captures ocean freight, handling, and documentation fees separately, so the true landed cost of a forwarding job is visible before the customer invoice is raised.

7. Warehouse Ops (xerpium_logistics_warehouse)

Warehousing is where delivery promises are won or lost. The module introduces warehouse zones to structure storage areas within a facility, and putaway strategies that allocate incoming goods to optimised locations rather than wherever space happens to be free. Cross-docking enables fast transfer from inbound to outbound for goods that should never be stored at all, cutting handling time for time-critical freight. Picking supports wave and batch methods so labour is organised around order profiles instead of individual lines. Inter-warehouse stock transfer keeps movements between facilities recorded and auditable rather than informal.

8. Real-time Tracking (logistics.tracking)

Status enquiries consume disproportionate customer service time when visibility is manual. The tracking module records events across a defined set — created, confirmed, picked up, in transit, at hub, out for delivery, delivered, exception, and returned — each with latitude and longitude, a real-time timestamp, and the driver or hub personnel who reported it. Photo evidence can be attached per event, which is invaluable when condition is later disputed. Status colour indicators flag normal, warning, and danger conditions so exceptions surface before customers notice them. The result is an auditable event history rather than a reconstructed account after the fact.

9. Proof of Delivery (logistics.proof.of.delivery)

Unclosed deliveries delay invoicing and weaken the position in disputes. The POD module captures recipient name, company, and phone, a digital signature image, the actual delivery datetime, and multiple delivery photos. Status options cover pending, delivered, partial, and refused, which reflects what actually happens at the dock rather than forcing a binary outcome. When POD is confirmed, the shipment is automatically marked as delivered, removing the lag between field completion and system status. Combined with Claim Management — covering damage, loss, delay, shortage, and wrong delivery — it gives the evidence base claims investigations depend on.

10. Cold Chain (xerpium_logistics_cold_chain)

Temperature-sensitive freight carries compliance obligations that generic logistics systems cannot evidence. This module sets a minimum and maximum temperature range per shipment, flagged through is_cold_chain with validation against the defined range. Temperature logs record readings in real time during transit, and alerts trigger when readings fall outside the permitted band, allowing intervention while the cargo is still recoverable. The resulting log supports cold chain regulatory compliance documentation for food, pharmaceutical, and chemical consignments. For operators moving into these sectors, this is often the capability that determines whether a contract can be bid for at all.

How to Choose the Right Solution

Start with your dominant service type. A domestic trucking operator needs Shipment, Route, Rate Card, Fleet, and POD working well before anything else; a forwarder will prioritise Container Management and Freight Forwarding. Deploying modules you do not operate adds configuration burden without return.

Second, check accounting integration early. Service Category mapping of income, expense, and COGS accounts per service type is what makes margin per shipment and per route trustworthy. Without it, operational data and financial reporting diverge within months.

Third, assess the integration points that matter to you — sales order to shipment auto-creation, stock picking to shipment, fleet cost allocation, and dashboard KPIs for on-time delivery and route profitability. These connections determine whether the system reduces data entry or adds to it.

Finally, consider workflow flexibility. Configurable shipment stages with role-based access control and stage-entry hooks allow the system to follow your process rather than forcing a rewrite of how your dispatch team works.

Conclusion

Logistics operations do not need more tools; they need fewer disconnected ones. The ten solutions above work as a chain — a shipment priced from a rate card, routed on a defined lane, assigned to a vehicle, tracked by event, closed by POD, and invoiced with actual cost against revenue. Each link removes a point where data is re-entered or lost.
