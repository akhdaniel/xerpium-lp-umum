# Agriculture ERP: Managing Plantations, Harvests, and Finances with Xerpium

Agriculture and plantation businesses operate with a unique rhythm. Revenue flows in harvest cycles rather than steady monthly streams, costs are concentrated in inputs like fertilizer, seeds, and pesticides, and a significant portion of the workforce is seasonal. On top of this operational complexity, Indonesian agribusinesses must comply with local accounting standards (SAK) and tax regulations such as PPN 11%. Generic ERP systems rarely account for biological assets or harvest-specific cost structures, leaving finance teams to improvise with manual spreadsheets and disconnected reports.

Xerpium ERP addresses these gaps with a localized agriculture package. Built around Indonesian chart-of-accounts standards, a dedicated harvest dashboard, and agriculture-specific financial reporting, Xerpium gives plantation operators a single system to track revenue, input costs, workers, stock, and compliance from planting to sale.

Common Challenges in Agriculture Operations

Agribusinesses frequently struggle with the following:

  • Seasonal cash flow volatility — Revenue arrives in bursts during harvest, while input costs are spread across the growing cycle, making liquidity hard to forecast.
  • Biological asset accounting — Productive and consumptive plants and livestock require specialized account groups that standard COA templates do not provide.
  • Fragmented cost tracking — Separating harvest COGS (cost of goods sold) from general operating costs is difficult without a tailored account structure.
  • Complex workforce management — Managing a mix of permanent and seasonal workers, with daily or piece-rate payroll, adds administrative overhead.
  • Compliance and localization — Indonesian agribusinesses need 6-digit account codes, SAK-aligned structures, and pre-configured PPN tax defaults to stay compliant.

Relevant Xerpium Modules for Agriculture

Localized Chart of Accounts (l10n_id_agriculture)

Xerpium ships an agriculture-specific COA template using the ag_ prefix and 6-digit account codes aligned with Indonesian standards. Account groups follow SAK for agriculture, and key accounts are pre-mapped: receivables (ag_120100), payables (ag_210100), revenue (ag_410100), and COGS (ag_510100). The template also includes pre-configured PPN 11% defaults for both sale and purchase, plus dedicated accounts for currency exchange gains/losses (ag_710200/ag_720200) and cash differences (ag_710300/ag_720300). Sale and purchase journals are set with refund sequences enabled by default.

Beyond the base structure, the accounting layer provides account groups for biological assets (crops and livestock), harvest cost (Biaya Panen) separated from other COGS, dedicated accounts for government fertilizer and seed subsidies, land management costs such as rent and land tax, and depreciation of agricultural equipment and plantation machinery.

Agriculture Financial Reporting (report_id_agriculture)

This module delivers a reporting structure built to agriculture standards. The Balance Sheet includes a biological asset group, distinguishing productive from consumptive plants. The P&L Statement presents harvest revenue alongside input and harvest COGS, and the Cash Flow report tracks operational, investing (land), and financing activity. Account groups are auto-synced from the COA template through a post-load hook (_post_load_data) and XML parsing of the report structure file, ensuring reports stay aligned with the chart template whenever it is applied.

Operational reports extend this further with a Harvest Report by period, an Input Report covering fertilizer and seed purchases, and a Land Usage report broken down by commodity.

Harvest Dashboard (vit_dashboard_agriculture)

The agriculture dashboard provides a real-time command center with a green agribusiness color theme and a 12-column responsive grid. It presents 8 KPI tiles: total harvest revenue, number of sales, input cost, buyer receivables, active workers, stock value, pending input purchase orders, and invoices for the current month. These metrics are drawn from ORM sources including sale_order, purchase_order, stock_quant, hr_employee, and account_move.

The dashboard also includes 4 analytical charts: monthly revenue, cost trend, top commodities, and top buyers. A global date filter auto-applies across the view, and data refreshes every 5 minutes.

Dashboard Analytics

The analytics layer surfaces insights such as revenue per commodity (top 10 products by revenue), a 12-month revenue and cost trend, the top 10 buyers (distributors, markets, exporters), worker metrics by department, stock valuation of harvested goods, harvest cycle tracking per product, and monthly input cost breakdowns for fertilizer, seeds, and pesticides.

HR & Operations

Xerpium supports worker management for both permanent and seasonal staff, payroll configured for daily or piece-rate harvest wages, and attendance tracking for field and plantation workers. Teams can organize departments by commodity or land block, track equipment such as tractors and sprayers, and use a calendar for planting, harvesting, and fertilizer application schedules.

Business Benefits

  • Faster financial close with SAK-aligned accounts and auto-synced report structures.
  • Clear profitability visibility by separating harvest COGS and input costs from general expenses.
  • Real-time operational awareness through a dashboard refreshed every 5 minutes.
  • Built-in compliance with 6-digit codes and PPN 11% defaults ready out of the box.
  • Better workforce control across permanent and seasonal labor, including daily and piece-rate payroll.
  • Accurate asset reporting with dedicated biological asset groups on the balance sheet.

Example Flow: From Input Purchase to Harvest Sale

Consider a plantation preparing for a new growing cycle:

  1. The procurement team raises purchase orders for fertilizer, seeds, and pesticides. These appear on the dashboard under PO Input Pending and feed the input cost analytics.
  2. As workers are onboarded for the season, they are registered in HR, organized by department per commodity, and tracked through attendance. The active workers KPI updates accordingly.
  3. During the cycle, equipment depreciation and land rent are posted to their dedicated accounts, keeping cost structures clean.
  4. At harvest, stock is received into internal locations and valued automatically, reflected in Nilai Stok Hasil Panen (harvest stock value).
  5. Produce is sold to distributors and exporters. Confirmed sale orders update total harvest revenue, while unpaid invoices appear in buyer receivables.
  6. Finance reviews the P&L with harvest revenue and input COGS, and a cash flow report that separates operations, land investment, and financing.

Conclusion

Xerpium ERP brings these together in one localized package, pairing a SAK-aligned chart of accounts with purpose-built reports and a real-time harvest dashboard.
