10 Best ERP Solutions for Data Center & IT System Operations

November 5, 2026

Data center and IT service operators run two businesses at once: a capital-intensive physical facility and a recurring-revenue service company. Racks, power circuits, cooling capacity, and network gear have to be tracked asset by asset, while colocation, cloud hosting, and managed service contracts generate monthly recurring revenue, usage overages, and SLA obligations that must be measured and invoiced accurately. When facility records live in spreadsheets, tickets sit in a standalone helpdesk, contracts live in a sales folder, and finance works from a generic chart of accounts, the gaps become expensive: unbilled bandwidth overages, uptime credits that nobody reconciles, depreciation schedules that do not match the equipment actually installed, and capacity decisions made without data. The modules below are drawn from the Xerpium Data Center IT solution set and are chosen because each one closes a specific gap between operations and the ledger.

The 10 Solutions at a Glance

  1. xerpium_data_center_it (Core) — Facility, asset, contract, and ticket management in a single operational backbone.
  2. l10n_id_data_center_it — A six-digit chart of accounts purpose-built for IT services and Indonesian tax defaults.
  3. report_id_data_center_it — Financial report structures aligned to data center asset and revenue groupings.
  4. vit_dashboard_data_center_it — A KPI dashboard covering revenue, capacity, uptime, and SLA compliance.
  5. xerpium_dynamic_stage_data_center_it — Configurable lifecycle stages for assets, tickets, contracts, and changes.
  6. llm_tool_data_center_it — AI assistants for ticket patterns, capacity forecasts, contract summaries, and asset lookup.
  7. Accounting Data Center — IT-specific revenue, COGS, depreciation, and deferred revenue handling.
  8. Project & Service — SLA monitoring, incident management, timesheets, and billing in one workflow.
  9. Colocation Services — Rack, power, bandwidth, and remote hands management with usage billing.
  10. Cloud Hosting — VPS, storage, networking, and backup services tied to recurring billing.

1. xerpium_data_center_it (Core)

The core module is the operational foundation. It manages facilities down to rooms, racks, and power/cooling tracking, and maintains IT assets — servers, network, storage, and security equipment — across their full lifecycle. Service contracts for colocation, managed services, cloud, and support are held here with their SLA tiers, while NOC/SOC service tickets handle incidents with automatic escalation. Capacity planning monitors power, cooling, and space utilization; change management routes IT change requests through an approval workflow. Recurring billing runs through xerpium_subscription, and usage billing meters power in kWh, bandwidth in GB, and storage in TB.

2. l10n_id_data_center_it

Generic accounting templates do not describe a data center business. This localization module installs a chart of accounts template specific to data center and IT systems, using six-digit account codes in line with Indonesian practice and account groups structured around IT services. Receivables and payables are pre-mapped to dc_120100 and dc_210100, revenue streams are separated into colocation, cloud hosting, and managed services, and PPN 11% defaults are pre-configured for both sale and purchase. Currency exchange gain and loss (dc_710200, dc_720200) and cash difference accounts (dc_710300, dc_720300) are defined up front, removing the usual post-go-live cleanup work.

3. report_id_data_center_it

Reporting is only as good as its structure. This module defines a report layout that matches data center standards: a balance sheet with IT asset groups for servers and network equipment, a profit and loss statement that presents revenue by service type, and a cash flow report built around IT services operations. Account group IDs are auto-synced from the chart of accounts template, and the structure itself is parsed from vit_report_structure_data_center_it.xml. A post-load hook runs _post_load_data when the chart template is applied, so reports are populated correctly at setup rather than being rebuilt manually by the finance team later.

4. vit_dashboard_data_center_it

Operations managers need numbers without waiting for month-end. This dashboard presents KPI tiles for revenue, contracts, capacity, uptime, and SLA compliance, plus charts for monthly revenue, cost trend, top clients, and service breakdown. Data is drawn from ORM sources including sale_order, project.project, and account_move, with SQL sources supplying monthly metrics, top-client rankings, and service revenue analytics. A global date filter applies automatically and the view refreshes every five minutes. The layout uses a 12-column responsive grid with a sky blue theme for IT services, keeping capacity and commercial indicators visible on one screen.

5. xerpium_dynamic_stage_data_center_it

Data center records move through long, auditable lifecycles, and hard-coded statuses rarely fit every provider. This module makes stages configurable across four flows: asset lifecycle (Draft → Available → Installed → Active → Maintenance → Decommissioned → Disposed), ticket pipeline (New → Open → In Progress → Pending → Resolved → Closed → Cancelled), contract stages (Draft → Active → Expired → Cancelled, with auto-activation on enter), and change workflow (Draft → Submitted → Approved → In Progress → Completed → Cancelled). On-enter methods execute business logic automatically when a record reaches a stage, and per-stage configuration controls user groups, confirmation messages, and whether records may move backward or forward.

6. llm_tool_data_center_it

AI is applied here to the repetitive analytical work that otherwise consumes senior engineers. The ticket pattern analyzer reviews ticket history, auto-categorizes issues, and suggests resolutions, which shortens triage for recurring incidents. The capacity forecast tool predicts power, cooling, and space requirements from usage trends, supporting investment decisions before a facility runs tight. The contract summarizer condenses terms, identifies renewal risks, and highlights SLA obligations that would otherwise be buried in PDF agreements. Asset lookup allows natural language search for IT assets by type, location, or status, which is useful during incident response and audits.

7. Accounting Data Center

This capability set covers the accounting treatments that define IT service economics. All accounts carry the dc_XXXXXX six-digit prefix. Revenue streams are separated into colocation, cloud, managed services, and license, while COGS is broken down into bandwidth, power, cooling, and maintenance — the cost lines that actually move gross margin. Depreciation is configured for servers, network equipment, UPS, and air conditioning. Prepaid revenue handles deferred recognition on annual contracts, and license accounting tracks software license cost and revenue separately. Sale and purchase journal defaults are set with refund_sequence enabled for clean credit note numbering.

8. Project & Service

Service delivery and billing are handled together. SLA monitoring tracks uptime targets, incident response time, and resolution time. Capacity planning covers rack space, bandwidth, power, and cooling. Incident management runs ticket creation, assignment, escalation, and resolution, while a preventive maintenance calendar schedules planned work before failures occur. Project tasks are assigned for deployments and migrations, and engineer timesheets are recorded per project or incident. Billing then consolidates MRR, usage billing, and overage charges. The result is that effort spent on a client is visible next to the revenue that client generates.

9. Colocation Services

Colocation is the most physically constrained revenue line, and it needs precise records. This area manages rack rental by full, half, or quarter rack on a monthly basis, with power allocation tracked as kW per rack and monitored against usage. Bandwidth is handled as an included allowance plus overage billing, and public IP address allocation is recorded per client. Operational services such as 24x7 client access to the server room and remote hands support for hardware maintenance are managed alongside the contract. Connected to usage metering, this prevents consumption from going unbilled.

10. Cloud Hosting

Cloud services introduce elastic consumption that static contracts cannot capture. This area covers VPS and VM provisioning with defined CPU, RAM, and storage; storage tiers across SSD, HDD, and backup; and networking components including VLAN, firewall, and load balancer. Backup services cover daily backups and disaster recovery. Scalability allows CPU and RAM to auto-scale based on usage, and API access supports provisioning and management programmatically. Because these services feed the same subscription and usage billing engine as the rest of the platform, scaling events translate into invoice lines rather than reconciliation disputes.

How to Choose the Right Solution

Start with your revenue mix. A pure colocation provider will prioritize the core module, colocation management, and usage billing. A provider weighted toward managed services and cloud will get more value from contract stages, SLA monitoring, and the dashboard.

Confirm the accounting foundation first. The localization and report structure modules should be installed before transaction volume builds. Migrating a live ledger into a six-digit IT chart of accounts later is far more work than configuring it at the start.

Check the operational handoffs. The value of this stack comes from tickets, contracts, assets, and timesheets sharing one data model. Evaluate whether incident resolution time, engineer hours, and overage consumption actually reach invoicing without manual re-entry.

Match workflow configurability to your process maturity. If your asset or change approval process differs from the defaults, confirm that dynamic stages and on-enter methods can represent it without custom development.

Conclusion

Data center and IT system operations fail commercially not because engineering is weak, but because capacity data, service delivery records, and financial statements are maintained separately. The modules above address that gap at each layer: a core operational model for facilities, assets, contracts, and tickets; a chart of accounts and report structure built for IT services; dashboards that surface SLA and capacity in near real time; configurable lifecycles; and AI tools for forecasting and contract review.
