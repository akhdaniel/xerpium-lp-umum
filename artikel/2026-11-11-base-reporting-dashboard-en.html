Real-Time Reporting & Dashboard ERP: How Xerpium Turns Scattered Data Into Executive Decisions

Most finance and operations leaders do not suffer from a lack of data. They suffer from data that lives in too many places. Sales figures sit in one spreadsheet, inventory counts in another, project margins in a third — and by the time someone consolidates them into a board-ready report, the numbers are already a week old. Decisions get made on stale information, or worse, on intuition dressed up as analysis.

This is the structural problem that a proper reporting and dashboard layer solves. Not by adding another reporting tool on top of the stack, but by sitting inside the ERP itself, reading from the same transactional records that operations teams create every day. Xerpium ERP — built on the Odoo framework — treats Reporting & Dashboard as a base module: a foundation that connects to every other module in the suite rather than a bolt-on that has to be fed manually.

Common Challenges in Enterprise Reporting

Before looking at the solution, it is worth naming the recurring failure patterns that reporting teams encounter across industries:

  • Month-end reporting lag. Closing the books and producing management reports takes days or weeks, which means leadership reviews performance long after the period it describes.
  • Conflicting numbers between departments. Finance reports one revenue figure, sales reports another, because each team maintains its own extract and applies its own cut-off rules.
  • Manual consolidation across entities. Groups operating multiple legal entities or currencies rebuild consolidated reports by hand every cycle, introducing both delay and error risk.
  • Rigid standard reports. Out-of-the-box reports rarely match how a specific business actually measures itself, forcing teams back into spreadsheets to produce anything custom.
  • No audit trail behind the numbers. When a figure looks wrong, nobody can trace which transaction, adjustment, or user produced it — making both internal review and external audit painful.

Each of these problems traces back to the same root cause: reporting that is disconnected from the system of record.

Example Flow: Monthly Performance Review in a Multi-Entity Group

Consider a group operating three entities, each transacting in a different currency.

On the first working day of the month, the group CFO opens the Xerpium dashboard. Consolidated KPIs across all three entities are already available — no analyst has been asked to prepare them. The figures reflect transactions recorded in the operational modules up to that moment, with multi-currency handling applied by the system.

One entity's performance looks out of line with expectation. Rather than requesting a special analysis, the CFO uses cross-module reporting to examine the relationship between operational activity and financial outcome within that entity, then applies a custom report view that the group has defined for this kind of review. A specific figure needs verification, so the audit trail is used to trace it back to the underlying transactions and the users who recorded them.

By the time the management meeting begins that afternoon, the discussion is about causes and corrective action — not about whether the numbers can be trusted.
