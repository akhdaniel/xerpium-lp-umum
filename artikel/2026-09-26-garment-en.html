# Garment ERP: Connecting Style BOM, Cutting, Sewing, and QC with Xerpium

Garment manufacturing operates on tight margins, short delivery windows, and high product variation. Every style has its own bill of materials, routing, fabric consumption, and quality expectations. A generic ERP often struggles to handle multi-level style BOMs, real-time WIP tracking, bundle-level traceability, and garment-specific accounting. Xerpium ERP addresses this with a dedicated garment framework: a six-digit garment chart of accounts, production orders for cutting and sewing, QC checkpoints, and integrated KPIs. The result is a clearer connection between the shop floor, inventory, procurement, and financial reporting.

Common Challenges in Garment Manufacturing

Garment businesses typically face several recurring operational issues:

  • Style-level costing is difficult when fabric, trim, thread, labels, and packaging must be rolled up per style.
  • Work-in-process is hard to trace across cutting, sewing, and finishing, especially when bundles move through multiple production lines.
  • Quality control becomes inconsistent without clear checkpoints, defect categories, and repair tracking.
  • Production performance is not visible enough for daily decision-making around line efficiency, downtime, and target versus actual output.
  • Accounting and tax treatment are complicated by separate raw material, WIP, and finished goods accounts, plus PPN garment at 11%.

Without a system that natively handles these requirements, management often relies on spreadsheets and manual reconciliation.

How Xerpium ERP Supports Garment Operations

Garment-Specific Chart of Accounts and Cost Structure

Xerpium provides a garment chart of accounts with six-digit codes using the ga_XXXXX structure. This includes dedicated accounts for receivables (ga_120100), payables (ga_210100), inventory (ga_130100–ga_130800), apparel and makloon revenue (ga_430100), and garment production expense (ga_510100). For inventory and production, Xerpium separates raw material (ga_130100), work-in-process (ga_130200), finished goods (ga_130300), consumables and trim (ga_130600), garment assets such as sewing, overlock, and cutting machines (ga_170100), and accumulated depreciation (ga_170200).

On the cost side, the chart includes direct raw material cost (ga_510200), direct labor (ga_510300), factory overhead for electricity, water, and gas (ga_510400), trim and accessories cost (ga_510500), makloon service cost (ga_510600), and subcontract cost (ga_510700). Other accounts cover freight (ga_520200), sales commission (ga_520300), sales discount (ga_430200), sales return (ga_430300), input VAT (ga_150200), and output VAT (ga_250200). This structure makes garment-specific financial tracking clear from day one.

BOM per Style and Production Orders

Xerpium supports multi-level bills of material per garment style. Each BOM can include fabric, trim, thread, labels, and packaging, with routing operations for cutting, sewing, finishing, and packing. The system allows cost roll-up to calculate the production cost per unit for each style and includes scrap percentage as an allowance for fabric waste per style.

Production orders are managed as garment-specific manufacturing orders. Planning uses lot sizing for each cutting batch. Component release controls the issuing of raw materials into production, while WIP tracking provides real-time visibility into goods in process. Labor hours can be recorded per operation, enabling more accurate direct labor costing and operator performance analysis.

Cutting, Sewing, and Quality Control

Xerpium supports cutting plans and marker plans per fabric lay, with fabric usage tracking per cutting activity. Sewing lines can be allocated per style, and bundles can be tracked from cutting through finishing. Defect tracking records issues per bundle or operation, so problems are not lost between workstations.

Quality control includes QC checkpoints for cutting, sewing, and finishing. AQL sampling supports Acceptable Quality Level inspection, and defects are categorized as minor, major, or critical with action plans. Repair orders can be created for defective goods, and QC pass rates are tracked per style, line, and operator. This structure turns quality from a final inspection activity into an operational control loop.

Integrated KPIs and Dashboards

Xerpium brings garment KPIs into dashboards with drill-down to source documents. Production KPIs include daily output, sewing line efficiency, machine downtime per shift, target versus actual production, and Standard Minute Value tracking. Financial KPIs include cost per unit, gross margin per style, WIP value in rupiah, and inventory turnover for raw materials and finished goods. Quality KPIs cover QC pass rate, defect rate per style or line, rework cost per period, and customer return percentage. Supply chain KPIs include procurement lead time for raw materials, fabric stock per type, on-time-in-full delivery to buyers, and supplier scores for fabric and trim vendors.

Connecting Garment Operations to Accounting, Inventory, Purchase, and HR

Xerpium connects garment production to the back office without manual rekeying. The garment-to-accounting integration uses the six-digit ga_XXXXX chart of accounts, separates WIP, raw material, and finished goods accounts, and posts production cost journals automatically from manufacturing orders.

The garment-to-inventory connection tracks raw material inventory per fabric type, manages WIP in stock locations, and transfers finished goods from production into the finished goods location. The garment-to-manufacturing connection supports multi-level style BOMs, cutting, sewing, and finishing routings, and subcontracted makloon outsourcing. The garment-to-purchase connection generates purchase orders for raw materials from BOM requirements, handles PO for trim and accessories, and supports supplier management for fabric and trim. The garment-to-HR connection records operator working hours, supports payroll based on output per line, and manages attendance per production shift.

Business Benefits of Xerpium for Garment Manufacturers

  • Clearer style costing through multi-level BOMs, cost roll-up, and cost-per-unit tracking.
  • Stronger WIP control with real-time tracking and bundle traceability from cutting to finishing.
  • More consistent quality through QC checkpoints, AQL sampling, defect categories, and repair orders.
  • Better production accountability with SMV, line efficiency, downtime, and target-versus-actual reporting.
  • Aligned procurement and inventory through BOM-driven purchase requirements, fabric stock visibility, and supplier scoring.
  • Accurate garment accounting and tax handling with separate WIP, raw material, and finished goods accounts, plus input and output VAT accounts for 11% PPN.

Example Flow: From Style BOM to Finished Goods

A garment manufacturer creates a new style in Xerpium and defines the multi-level BOM with fabric, trim, thread, label, and packaging. The routing includes cutting, sewing, finishing, and packing, with a scrap allowance for fabric waste. A production order is created for a cutting batch, and raw materials are released to production. The cutting team records a marker plan and tracks fabric usage. Bundles are allocated to a sewing line and tracked through finishing. QC checkpoints use AQL sampling, and any defect is recorded with a minor, major, or critical category. If repair is needed, a repair order is generated. Completed goods move to finished goods inventory, the cost roll-up calculates cost per unit, and the production cost journal is posted automatically. Managers then review output, efficiency, pass rate, and WIP value from the integrated dashboard.
