10 Best ERP Solutions for Agriculture Operations

October 21, 2026

Agriculture operations run on seasonal cycles, biological assets, field labor, and input-heavy cost structures. Yet many teams still manage harvest results, fertilizer and seed purchases, worker attendance, and financial compliance in disconnected spreadsheets. That disconnect creates late harvest reports, unclear receivables, inaccurate stock valuation, and manual reconciliation under Indonesia’s SAK pertanian and PPN 11% requirements.

Xerpium’s agriculture ERP modules address these operational gaps directly. The solutions below combine localized accounting, role-based dashboards, agriculture-specific financial reports, and field operations tools. Each solution is drawn from Xerpium’s official agriculture brochures and focuses on the workflows that matter most to plantations, farms, and agribusiness operators.

The 10 Solutions at a Glance

  1. Localized Agriculture COA Template (l10n_id_agriculture) — Deploys a 6-digit agriculture chart of accounts with PPN 11% defaults.
  2. Agriculture Report Structure (report_id_agriculture) — Syncs financial report structure to the agriculture COA template automatically.
  3. Agriculture Dashboard Panen (vit_dashboard_agriculture) — Gives operations teams 8 KPI tiles and 4 charts in a green agribusiness dashboard.
  4. Dashboard Metrics — Turns harvest revenue, input cost, receivables, and stock value into fixed KPI calculations.
  5. Accounting Agriculture — Adds biological asset, harvest COGS, subsidy, land, and depreciation accounts under ag_XXXXXX codes.
  6. Dashboard Analytics — Tracks revenue by commodity, monthly trends, top buyers, workers, and input costs.
  7. Financial Reports — Produces agriculture balance sheets, P&L, cash flow, harvest, input, and land usage reports.
  8. Worker Management — Manages permanent and seasonal workers, daily/piece-rate payroll, and attendance by commodity department.
  9. Harvest Report — Delivers period-based harvest results and harvest cycle tracking per product.
  10. Equipment Tracking — Tracks tractors, sprayers, and other equipment alongside planting, harvest, and fertilizer calendars.

The 10 Solutions in Detail

1. Localized Agriculture COA Template (l10n_id_agriculture)

This module provides a chart of accounts specifically designed for agriculture and plantation businesses. It uses 6-digit account codes following Indonesian standards and structures account groups according to SAK pertanian. The template includes receivable and payable accounts such as ag_120100 for AR and ag_210100 for AP, plus revenue account ag_410100 and COGS account ag_510100. PPN 11% sale and purchase tax defaults are pre-configured, while currency exchange gain and loss accounts ag_710200 and ag_720200 handle multi-currency transactions. Cash difference accounts ag_710300 and ag_720300 complete the setup. This solves slow financial setup and manual tax mapping by giving agriculture operations a compliant, ready-to-use accounting structure.

2. Agriculture Report Structure (report_id_agriculture)

This solution structures financial statements according to agricultural reporting standards. It creates a balance sheet with a biological asset group, a profit and loss statement with harvest COGS and input costs, and a cash flow statement for operational agriculture activity. Account group synchronization is automatic: the system maps account_group_ids from the COA template through XML parsing of vit_report_structure_agriculture.xml. A post-load hook runs _post_load_data to sync report structures whenever the chart template is applied. For operations teams, this removes the recurring work of restructuring reports after account changes and keeps agriculture-specific statements consistent with the underlying ledger.

3. Agriculture Dashboard Panen (vit_dashboard_agriculture)

This KPI dashboard is built for agriculture and plantation management visibility. It presents 8 KPI tiles covering revenue, orders, input cost, accounts receivable, active workers, stock value, pending purchase orders, and monthly invoices. Four charts display monthly revenue, cost trends, top commodities, and top buyers. Data is sourced from sale_order, purchase_order, stock_quant, hr_employee, and account_move, with SQL-based analytics for revenue/cost trends and top products or buyers. A global date filter auto-applies with a 5-minute refresh. The 12-column responsive grid uses a green agribusiness color theme and spans y=0 to y=14, giving operations leaders a focused harvest and cost dashboard without building reports manually.

4. Dashboard Metrics

Dashboard Metrics defines the exact calculations behind operational KPIs. Total Revenue Panen sums amount_total from sale_order records in sale or done state. Total Penjualan counts sale_order transactions. Biaya Input totals purchase_order amounts for fertilizer, seed, and pesticides. Piutang Pembeli sums amount_residual from unpaid out_invoice records. Tenaga Kerja counts active hr_employee records. Nilai Stok Hasil Panen sums stock_quant values at internal locations. PO Input Pending counts purchase_order records in draft, sent, or to approve states. Invoice Bulan Ini counts out_invoice records for the current month. This structured approach gives finance and operations teams confidence that every KPI is tied to a clear, audit-friendly source query.

5. Accounting Agriculture

This module extends the localized COA with agriculture-specific accounting dimensions. All agriculture accounts use the ag_XXXXXX 6-digit prefix. A biological asset account group separates productive and consumptive plants and livestock. Harvest COGS is kept apart from other cost of goods sold, so harvest profitability remains visible. Subsidy accounts track government fertilizer and seed subsidy programs. Land management accounts capture land lease and tax costs. Depreciation accounts handle agricultural equipment and plantation asset depreciation. Sale and purchase journal defaults include refund_sequence=True, ensuring consistent journal sequencing for returns. This solves the problem of mixing agriculture-specific costs into generic accounts and gives management clearer subsidy, land, and harvest cost control.

6. Dashboard Analytics

Dashboard Analytics turns operational data into agriculture-specific insight. Revenue per Komoditas ranks the top 10 agriculture products by revenue. A 12-month revenue and cost trend chart shows seasonality. Top Buyers identifies the 10 largest distributors, pasar, or exporters. Worker Metrics counts active workers per department. Stock Valuation shows the value of harvest inventory in the warehouse. Harvest Cycle tracks the cycle per product. Input Cost reports fertilizer, seed, and pesticide expenses by month. These analytics help agribusiness teams monitor commodity performance, buyer concentration, labor allocation, and input spending without exporting data to external BI tools or spreadsheets.

7. Financial Reports

Financial Reports deliver agriculture-specific statements for management, lenders, and compliance. The balance sheet includes biological assets classified as productive or consumptive. The P&L separates harvest revenue from input COGS. Cash flow covers operational, investment—including land—and financing activities. A harvest report shows harvest results per period. An input report tracks purchases of fertilizer, seed, and other inputs. Land usage reporting breaks down land use by commodity. These reports reduce the manual effort of generating financing-ready statements and give operations leaders a single view of biological assets, harvest performance, input spending, and land productivity in one structured reporting pack.

8. Worker Management

Worker Management covers the labor reality of agriculture operations. It manages permanent and seasonal employees and supports daily or piece-rate payroll for harvest workers. Attendance records track workers in kebun or sawah environments. Departments can be structured by commodity or land area, making labor costs easier to allocate to specific crops or blocks. This module comes from Xerpium’s HR & Operations scope and directly addresses the difficulty of reconciling daily harvest wages, seasonal headcount, and attendance without a connected system. Operations managers can finally match labor expenses to harvest activity and land units instead of relying on manual attendance books.

9. Harvest Report

Harvest Report provides period-based reporting on harvest results. It captures harvest output by period so management can compare actual production against planned cycles and sales commitments. Combined with Dashboard Analytics’ harvest cycle tracking per product, this solution gives agriculture operations a clear record of what was harvested, when, and for which commodity. It helps agribusiness teams monitor yield consistency, plan post-harvest handling, and support revenue recognition. Instead of waiting for field reports to be typed into spreadsheets, finance and operations can review structured harvest data from the same ERP system that records sales, inventory, and input costs.

10. Equipment Tracking

Equipment Tracking records agricultural tools such as tractors and sprayers. The module supports equipment records within the HR & Operations scope and connects them to operational scheduling through planting, harvest, and fertilizer application calendars. This helps maintenance planning, asset utilization, and field service coordination. By linking equipment to the farming calendar, operations managers can see when a tractor is scheduled for land preparation, when sprayers are needed for pesticide application, and when harvest equipment is committed. The result is fewer scheduling conflicts and better cost allocation for mechanized field operations without a separate asset-management tool.

How to Choose the Right Solution

Start with financial compliance. If your team struggles with SAK pertanian or PPN reporting, prioritize the localized COA template, report structure, and accounting agriculture modules. They ensure biological assets, harvest COGS, subsidies, land costs, and depreciation are coded correctly from day one.

Then evaluate operational visibility. Operations-heavy teams should implement the dashboard panen, dashboard metrics, and dashboard analytics modules early. These give fixed KPI calculations, harvest trends, buyer analysis, and input cost tracking without manual reporting.

Do not overlook field labor and assets. Worker management, harvest reporting, and equipment tracking are often the fastest operational wins because they replace paper attendance, manual harvest logs, and disconnected equipment schedules with structured ERP records.

Finally, align reports with external stakeholders. Lenders, investors, and tax teams need agriculture-specific balance sheets, P&L statements, and cash flow reports. Choose the financial reporting and harvest reporting modules that match those requirements before rollout.
