Complete ERP solution for Garment: From Fabric to Financial Close with Xerpium ERP

October 19, 2026

Garment manufacturers operate in one of the most detail-heavy environments in B2B production: every style has its own bill of materials, fabric consumption varies by cutting plan, work-in-process moves through cutting, sewing, finishing, and packing, and buyers expect consistent quality with on-time delivery. When accounting, inventory, production, purchasing, and quality live in separate systems, the result is manual reconciliation, delayed cost visibility, and limited confidence in margin per style.

Xerpium ERP addresses this with a single, end-to-end ERP scope for the garment vertical. The platform connects a garment-specific chart of accounts, multi-level BOM and routing, production orders, bundle tracking, quality control checkpoints, KPI dashboards, purchasing, HR, and financial postings in one system.

Tantangan umum di Garment

  • Complex BOM and scrap management — garment styles have multi-level BOMs with fabric, trim, thread, labels, and packaging, plus scrap allowance that varies per style.
  • Production tracking across cutting and sewing — work moves from cutting plans to sewing lines and bundles, making real-time WIP visibility difficult without a dedicated manufacturing flow.
  • Quality consistency — quality checks happen at multiple operations with AQL sampling and defect categories, but disconnected QC data makes pass rate and rework cost hard to measure.
  • Cost and margin visibility — raw material, direct labor, overhead, trim, makloon, and subcon costs must roll up into cost per unit and gross margin per style.
  • Financial integration — garment-specific accounts such as WIP, finished goods, PPN masukan, and PPN keluaran need automatic journal entries from production orders to avoid double entry and reporting lag.

Cakupan solusi Xerpium

Chart of Accounts and production costing built for garment

Xerpium uses a dedicated garment COA structure with 6-digit codes under the ga_XXXXX format. The account set separates receivables (ga_120100), payables (ga_210100), raw material, WIP, and finished goods inventory (ga_130100–ga_130800), apparel revenue and makloon services (ga_430100), production expenses (ga_510100), and PPN at 11% through tax_sale_11 and tax_purchase_11.

Production and inventory accounts include raw materials (ga_130100), WIP (ga_130200), finished goods (ga_130300), consumables and trim (ga_130600), garment assets such as sewing, overlock, and cutting machines (ga_170100), and accumulated depreciation (ga_170200). Production cost accounts separate raw material cost (ga_510200), direct labor (ga_510300), factory overhead (ga_510400), trim cost (ga_510500), makloon cost (ga_510600), and subcon cost (ga_510700).

BOM, routing, and cost roll-up per style

Each garment style has a multi-level BOM with components such as fabric, trim, thread, labels, and packaging. Routing operations include cutting, sewing, finishing, and packing. Xerpium supports cost roll-up to calculate cost per unit garment, along with scrap percentage to allow waste fabric per style. This gives merchandising and production teams a clear estimate before a style enters production.

Production order, cutting, and sewing control

Garment manufacturing orders in Xerpium support lot sizing per cutting batch, component release to production, real-time WIP tracking, and labor hours recording per operation. For cutting and sewing, the system includes cutting plans with marker plans per fabric lay, fabric usage tracking per cutting, sewing line allocation per style, bundle tracking from cutting to finishing, and defect tracking per bundle or operation.

Quality control and repair workflow

Quality control is embedded through QC checkpoints for cutting, sewing, and finishing. The system supports AQL sampling, defect categories classified as minor, major, or critical with action plans, repair orders for defective items, and QC pass rate tracking per style, line, or operator. This provides a closed loop between production defects and corrective action.

Performance management and integrated dashboards

Xerpium brings garment KPIs into integrated dashboards with drill-down to source documents. Production KPIs cover daily output, line efficiency, downtime per shift, target vs actual, and SMV. Financial KPIs include cost per unit, gross margin per style, WIP value in rupiah, and inventory turnover. Quality KPIs include pass rate, defect rate, rework cost, and customer return percentage. Supply chain KPIs cover procurement lead time, fabric stock, OTIF delivery, and supplier score for fabric and trim.

Cross-module flows

Xerpium connects the garment operation with the rest of the ERP suite:

  • Garment &harr; Accounting — garment-specific COA with separate WIP, raw material, and finished goods accounts, plus automatic production cost journals from manufacturing orders.
  • Garment &harr; Inventory — raw material stock per fabric type, WIP tracking in stock locations, and finished goods transfer from production.
  • Garment &harr; Manufacturing — multi-level BOM per style, routing for cutting, sewing, finishing, and subcontract makloon outsourcing.
  • Garment &harr; Dashboard — integrated garment KPI widgets for production, quality, and finance, with drill-down to underlying transactions.
  • Garment &harr; Purchase — purchase orders for raw materials generated from BOM requirements, purchase orders for trim and accessories, and supplier management for fabric and trim.
  • Garment &harr; HR — operator working time recording, payroll based on output per line, and attendance per production shift.

Alur end-to-end

A typical cycle in Xerpium starts with a garment style and its multi-level BOM. The system calculates cost roll-up per unit, including scrap allowance. Purchase suggests raw material and trim requirements from the BOM, and the purchasing team issues POs to fabric and trim suppliers.

Once materials arrive, a manufacturing order is created with lot sizing for the cutting batch. Components are released from inventory to production. The cutting plan tracks fabric usage per layer. Bundles move from cutting through sewing lines to finishing, with defect tracking and QC checkpoints at each stage. Labor hours per operation are recorded for direct labor costing.

Finished goods are transferred from production into finished goods inventory. Accounting journals are generated automatically from the manufacturing order using the garment COA: raw material cost, direct labor, overhead, trim, and subcon costs are posted to their respective accounts, while WIP is cleared. The dashboard updates daily output, line efficiency, QC pass rate, cost per unit, and gross margin per style.

Langkah implementasi

  1. Prepare master data — map the garment-specific COA (ga_XXXXX), set up fabric and trim items, BOMs per style, routing operations, suppliers, and production lines.
  2. Configure parameters — define PPN 11% tax codes, scrap percentage per style, QC checkpoints, defect categories, SMV values, and production KPI targets.
  3. Run a pilot style — select one representative style and execute the full flow from BOM and purchase to production order, bundle tracking, QC, finished goods, and automatic accounting journals.
  4. Roll out to additional lines and styles — expand to more cutting lots and sewing lines, validate labor recording and payroll by output, and tune dashboard KPIs.
  5. Evaluate and improve — review pass rate, defect rate, rework cost, WIP value, inventory turnover, and gross margin per style, then adjust routing, scrap allowance, or QC checkpoints as needed.
