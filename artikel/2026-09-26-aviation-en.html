# Aviation ERP: Unifying Airline, MRO, and Cargo Operations with Xerpium

Aviation is one of the most operationally dense industries in the world. A single flight requires coordination across scheduling, crew qualification, aircraft airworthiness, passenger booking, cargo handling, fuel uplift, and financial reconciliation. When these functions run on disconnected spreadsheets or legacy systems, small errors compound quickly: a missed medical expiry can ground a crew, an unrecorded component life limit can force an AOG, and manual revenue mapping can distort profitability.

Xerpium ERP addresses this by delivering a unified aviation suite that connects flight operations, fleet and MRO management, crew compliance, ticketing, cargo, and accounting in one platform. The result is real-time visibility, automated compliance tracking, and a single source of truth for every flight, work order, and air waybill.

Common Challenges in Aviation Operations

  • Fragmented systems for flight ops, maintenance, and finance create data silos and manual reconciliation errors.
  • Expiring certifications — crew licenses, medicals, qualifications, and A/C checks — can go unnoticed until they cause a disruption.
  • MRO cost overruns occur when work order estimates, actual hours, and spare part requirements are not tracked together.
  • Cargo and load management without automated ULD checks risks overweight or unsafe dispatch.
  • Revenue from multiple service categories (passenger, cargo, MRO, fuel, catering, ground handling) is difficult to map to correct accounts without a dedicated aviation COA.

How Xerpium ERP Solves These Challenges

Airport, Route, and Flight Management

Xerpium provides a master data layer for airports (IATA/ICAO code, city, country, timezone, GPS, elevation, hub flag) and routes (origin &rarr; destination, distance in NM, block time, and type such as domestic, international, regional, or charter). The system enforces constraints like origin not equal to destination and correct code lengths.

Flight Master records include airline IATA, flight type (passenger, cargo, charter, training), STD/STA, duration, and international flag. Schedules and legs track states from scheduled to boarding, departed, and arrived, with actual times, block time, delay, on-time performance (OTP &le;15 minutes = on time), and load factor. When a flight departs, the system automatically updates airframe hours and cycles, keeping maintenance data current without manual input.

Fleet, Airframe, and MRO

The aircraft type record stores ICAO type, manufacturer, MTOW, MLW, maximum seats, range, cruise speed, fuel capacity, and crew requirements. The fleet register uses tail numbers and tracks status (active, maintenance, AOG, stored, retired). Airframe hours and cycles, last flight date, and next A/C-check dates are continuously maintained. A real-time MRO rollup computes year-to-date cost, 30-day cost, hours, and work order count for each aircraft.

Work orders and tasks support A/B/C/D-checks, airworthiness directives (AD), service bulletins (SB), life-limited parts (LLP), and inspections. Each work order has estimated versus actual hours and cost, task cards per mechanic, and a Certificate of Release to Service (CRS) sign-off. States move from Open through In Progress, Pending Parts, Inspection, Released, and Closed.

Components and spare parts are tracked by part and serial number, aircraft position, life limit in hours or cycles, remaining life, and overhaul due. Spare requirements generate purchase orders automatically via cron. Compliance and tooling modules manage AD/SB compliance, tool calibration due dates, and recurring inspections that reset to open after the compliance date.

Crew and Qualification Management

Crew members are defined with role (Captain, First Officer, Cadet, Instructor, Cabin), base airport, license type (ATPL, CPL, MPL), medical class and expiry, and passport. Qualifications include type rating, line check, simulator session, emergency, security, and dangerous goods, each with validity tracking. The expiry alert engine automatically scans medicals, passports, qualifications, and A-check dates, assigning severity levels from info to critical.

Crew assignment per flight leg supports Captain, FO, Relief, Cabin Chief, Cabin Crew, and Flight Engineer. The system automatically validates type rating against the assigned aircraft type and tracks report/release time, block time, flight time, and rest time. Assignment states progress from Assigned to Confirmed, Completed, or Removed.

Booking, Ticketing, Seat, and Loyalty

The booking module generates PNRs automatically (3–8 characters) and tracks state progression from draft to confirmed, ticketed, checked-in, boarded, flown, or cancelled/refunded. E-tickets include TKT number, passenger, cabin, fare, SSR, and baggage. Refund wizard handles cancellation fees and method (original, credit, bank). Check-in wizard supports bulk check-in and seat assignment.

Seat assignment stores row and letter, cabin class, exit row flag, extra legroom, and occupied status, with uniqueness enforced per aircraft and flight. The loyalty system includes Silver, Gold, and Platinum tiers with minimum points, minimum spend, miles multiplier, and benefits such as lounge access, baggage allowance, boarding priority, and upgrades. Miles are calculated as total amount multiplied by tier miles multiplier, and tier upgrades occur automatically from cumulative points.

Cargo, ULD, and Load Sheet

Air Waybill (AWB) records use a 3-digit prefix plus 8-digit serial, with shipper, consignee, agent, origin/destination, and leg. Weight fields include pieces, gross weight, volume, and chargeable weight (maximum of actual weight versus volume &times;167). Special handling codes cover General, Perishable, Valuable, Hazardous, DGR, Human Remains, and Vulnerable. AWB workflow moves from Draft through Booked, Accepted, Loaded, Arrived, and Delivered.

ULD management tracks types (AKE, AKH, AKN, AAP, AMA, PLA, P1P, DQP), maximum weight, tare, maximum volume, fill percentage, and overweight alerts. The load sheet aggregates total passengers, baggage, cargo, and mail weight, calculating Zero Fuel Weight (ZFW), Takeoff Weight (TOW), and Landing Weight (LW) with MTOW compliance checks. Load sheet states progress from Draft to Finalized and Dispatched.

Fuel, Catering, Ground Handling, and Pre-Flight Checks

Fuel uplift records capture liters, density (kg/L), auto-computed mass, price per liter, total cost, and supplier. Catering records include meal counts, special meals, beverages, supplier, and cost. Ground handling tracks full, self, or partial service, passenger and baggage counts, and cost.

Pre-flight checks standardize inspections across types (preflight, hourly, transit, daily, weekly) and nine categories (Exterior, Engine, Cabin, Cockpit, Landing Gear, Fuel, Flight Controls, Safety Equipment, Cargo). Each item has status Pending, OK, Fail, or N/A, with a computed pass rate. Sign-off requires an inspector and date/time, and the system blocks dispatch if a check fails. Standard item templates auto-load on check start.

Accounting, Inventory, Sales, Purchase, HR, and AI

Xerpium integrates aviation operations with core ERP modules. The aviation-specific chart of accounts supports Indonesian aviation with 11% PPN. Revenue from tickets, cargo, MRO, catering, and fuel is mapped automatically to income accounts. Inventory handles MRO spare parts, fuel stock, and catering stock with lot/expiry tracking and component life limits. Sales automates booking to sale order creation and offers a customer portal. Purchasing creates POs for fuel, catering, ground handling, and spare parts, with auto-PO generation from spare requirements. HR integrates crew data directly from hr.employee.

Eight built-in AI tools allow natural language queries: lookup_flight, pax_itinerary, awb_track, wo_status, otp_digest, crew_roster_summary, uld_availability, and parts_lookup. The dashboard provides 22 KPI widgets covering revenue and AR, flight ops OTP, booking, MRO, and cargo metrics.

Business Benefits

  • Improved on-time performance through automated schedule, crew qualification, and pre-flight compliance.
  • Reduced AOG risk with real-time component life limits, work order status, and spare part auto-PO.
  • Stronger safety and compliance via expiry alerts, AD/SB tracking, and CRS sign-off enforcement.
  • Lower MRO costs from accurate estimates versus actuals and real-time cost rollups.
  • Unified financial visibility with automatic revenue mapping and aviation-specific COA.
  • Faster operations using AI assistants for flight, AWB, work order, OTP, and parts lookups.

Example Use Case: A Passenger Flight with Cargo

A domestic airline schedules flight GA201 from CGK to DPS. The crew assignment module validates the Captain and FO type ratings automatically and checks medical expiries. Passengers book through the portal; PNRs are issued, tickets generated, and seats assigned per cabin class. Pre-flight checks are completed and signed off. At departure, the flight leg updates actual times and the airframe hours and cycles increment automatically. Cargo AWBs are accepted and loaded; the load sheet is finalized with ZFW, TOW, and LW within MTOW. Fuel uplift is recorded, and the integrated accounting module maps passenger ticket revenue, cargo revenue, and fuel cost to the correct accounts. The dashboard updates OTP and revenue in real time, while an AI query can retrieve the flight status or AWB tracking instantly.

With built-in AI tools and a comprehensive dashboard, aviation teams can move from reactive firefighting to proactive, data-driven operations.
