Benefits of ERP Software for the Electronic OEM Manufacturing Industry

Electronic OEM manufacturing runs on a razor-thin margin between customer commitment and operational reality. A Korean principal sends a purchase order with a fixed FOB price and a target ship date. Behind that single line sits an imported bill of materials, an SMT line with its own yield curve, an AQL inspection gate, a KITE duty-exemption facility that must hold its ratio, and a PEB submission that has to clear customs before the vessel leaves. Miss one link and the entire chain slips.

Without an integrated system, most of these links live in separate places. BOM versions sit in spreadsheets that nobody is sure are current. Export documents are retyped three times — once for the packing list, once for the PEB, once for the commercial invoice. Yield and downtime are recorded on paper at the work center and summarized weekly, long after the problem has already cost money. Landed cost is estimated, not calculated. Supplier certificates expire quietly until an audit finds them.

Xerpium, built on Odoo, consolidates these processes into one ERP application designed specifically for electronic OEM operations. Below are the concrete benefits that shift delivers, and the modules behind each one.

Common Challenges in Electronic OEM Manufacturing

  • Uncontrolled BOM versions. Engineering changes arrive from the customer, but production keeps building to an outdated structure because there is no single approved source.
  • Fragmented export documentation. Delivery order, packing list, PEB, certificate of origin and commercial invoice are prepared separately, creating mismatched weights, volumes and values between documents.
  • Blind spots in shop-floor performance. Yield, scrap and downtime are known at month-end, not at shift-end, so corrective action always arrives late.
  • Manual trade compliance tracking. KITE realization ratios, LC expiry dates and supplier certificate validity are monitored in spreadsheets with no alerting.
  • Unclear true product cost. FOB plus freight, insurance, duty, tax and handling are rarely consolidated, so margin per model is a guess.

Operational Efficiency Through a Single Integrated Application

The first benefit is simply eliminating duplicated work. In Xerpium, the Product Model record holds the model code, customer part number, HS code, FOB price in USD, weight, volume and linked product. Every downstream document pulls from that record instead of being retyped.

The same logic applies across the export chain. An Export DO carrying container, vessel, port of loading and discharge, and incoterm data feeds the Packing List, which aggregates total packages, gross and net weight and volume per product model. That data then flows into the PEB for registration with DJBC through CEISA integration, the Certificate of Origin (Form E for ASEAN-China, Form AK for ASEAN-Korea, or Non-ASEAN), and the Export Invoice in USD. One entry, five consistent documents.

On the production side, the MO Extension links each manufacturing order directly to the customer PO and PO line, carrying the product model, FOB price in USD and target ship date. The order no longer floats free of the commitment it was created to fulfill.

Real-Time Visibility from the SMT Line to the Boardroom

Visibility is where an ERP system earns its keep daily. Xerpium captures Yield Records with input quantity, output quantity, scrap quantity and yield percentage per MO and per shift, alongside Downtime Records categorized by machine breakdown, setup change, material missing, quality issue or power outage. Work Orders carry cycle time targets, scrap budget percentages and sign-off.

Because this data is structured per work center — SMT, Assembly, Injection Molding, Calibration, QC Inspection and Packing — supervisors can see which station is losing yield and why, within the shift rather than after it. Shift management records start and end times with assignment per MO, so performance is always attributable.

At management level, the KPI Dashboard surfaces total revenue month-to-date, open customer POs not yet shipped or cancelled, active product models, approved and active BOM versions, active work centers, registered OEM customers and suppliers, and registered HS codes. A twelve-month monthly revenue line chart and a top-five customers bar chart give the commercial picture without a reporting request.

Cost Control and Accurate Margin per Model

Pricing an OEM contract correctly requires knowing the real cost, not the invoice cost. Xerpium's Landed Cost calculation builds the figure from FOB plus freight, insurance, duty, tax and handling, so imported component costs reflect what the material actually cost to bring into the plant.

This connects to a chart of accounts of 168 accounts purpose-built for electronic OEM operations. Revenue is separated into export thermostat and parts, local sales, scrap and discount. COGS is broken into raw material, import raw material, direct labor, overhead and variance. Expenses distinguish selling costs such as freight and commission from administrative costs including salary, BPJS, depreciation of SMT, Assembly and Calibration machines, and ISO, RoHS and CE certification. The balance sheet separates raw, WIP and finished inventory, and fixed assets by machine category.

BOM Lines also carry scrap rate percentage per component, so material planning reflects expected loss rather than theoretical consumption.

Compliance and Audit Readiness Built Into the Workflow

Trade and quality compliance are not reporting exercises in an OEM plant — they are licence-to-operate conditions.

The KITE Facility module tracks duty exemption with a ratio target of 1.5 and periodic realization, while KITE Realization compares import value against export value and actual ratio against target. The AI assistant tool electronic_check_kite_ratio lets staff check a facility's ratio by facility number in plain language.

On the documentation side, LC records cover sight and usance, import and export, with issuance, expiry and shipping dates. TT/Remittance tracks outgoing and incoming payments with bank reference and status.

Quality compliance is equally structured. AQL Inspection supports inbound sampling at levels 1.0, 2.5 and 4.0 with sample size and accept/reject outcome. Lot Traceability links component lot to finished lot, MO and customer PO — the chain an auditor or a complaining customer will ask for. Customer Complaints are logged by defect type (cosmetic, functional, dimension, missing, packaging) with root cause, action and status. Supplier Certificates for ISO 9001, ISO 14001, RoHS, REACH, UL and CE are tracked with expiry dates and attachments.

BOM Versions are controlled as draft, approved or obsolete, unique per model and linked to mrp.bom, with Customer Spec documentation attached per BOM version.

Faster Decisions with an Odoo-Based AI Assistant

Xerpium includes five AI tools accessible through natural language chat: electronic_product_model to find a model by code or name, electronic_supplier to search OEM suppliers by name or country, electronic_customer_po to locate a customer PO by reference or customer, electronic_suggest_hs_code to suggest an HS code from a description, and electronic_check_kite_ratio. Classification and lookup tasks that normally interrupt a specialist become a question anyone can ask.

Scalability Across Customers, Suppliers and Markets

Growth in OEM means more counterparties, not just more volume. Xerpium's OEM Customer model distinguishes Korean HQ, Korean Subsidiary, Local and Export customers with credit limit, incoterm and port of loading and discharge. OEM Suppliers are classified as Korean, Local, Chinese or Singapore with supplier code, partner type and payment term, scored through Supplier Rating on quality, delivery and price with an average total score.

Partner Extension flags whether a partner is an OEM customer or supplier and records parent corporation country, bank account and SWIFT. Adding a new principal or a new sourcing country becomes configuration, not a new spreadsheet.

Business Benefits at a Glance

  • Consistent export documents generated from one dataset, reducing customs rejection risk
  • Shift-level yield and downtime visibility for faster corrective action
  • Accurate landed cost and margin per product model
  • Continuous KITE ratio, LC and certificate expiry monitoring
  • Full lot traceability from component to finished goods and customer PO
  • Objective supplier selection through quality, delivery and price scoring

Example Flow: From Customer PO to Export Shipment

A Korean HQ customer issues a PO for a thermostat model. The MO Extension links it to the PO line, product model, FOB price and target ship date. Imported components arrive under an Import PO with USD currency, incoterm, port, ETD/ETA and LC number, then pass Inbound Inspection via AQL sampling. Landed cost is applied. Production runs across SMT, Assembly and Calibration work centers, with yield and downtime captured per shift and Quality Checks recording sample size and reject count. On completion, the Export DO is raised with container and vessel details, the Packing List consolidates weights and volumes, the PEB is submitted to DJBC through CEISA, Form AK is issued, and the Export Invoice is generated in USD. The transaction posts to the OEM chart of accounts, and the KITE Realization record updates import versus export value.

Conclusion

The benefit of ERP in electronic OEM manufacturing is not automation for its own sake — it is the removal of the gaps where cost, compliance risk and delivery slippage hide.
