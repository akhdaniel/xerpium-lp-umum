# Xerpium for SMBs: One ERP for Sales, Inventory, and Accounting Without the Enterprise Price Tag

Small and medium-sized businesses often reach a familiar breaking point: sales records live in one application, inventory in another, and financial data in spreadsheets or separate accounting software. The result is duplicated data entry, slow reconciliation, and decisions based on fragmented information. Xerpium ERP offers a different approach for SMBs—bringing Sales, CRM, Purchasing, Inventory, and Accounting into one centralized system, supported by an AI assistant and a per-user pricing model designed to be accessible.

This article explains the operational challenges SMBs commonly face and how Xerpium’s core modules, onboarding, pricing, and AI capabilities help address them.

Common SMB operational challenges

Many small and medium-sized businesses experience similar friction as they grow:

  • Sales, stock, and finance are managed in multiple separate applications, creating data silos.
  • Teams spend time manually moving data between systems instead of acting on it.
  • Business owners struggle to get a single, current view of sales performance and inventory health.
  • Traditional ERP adoption can feel complex, expensive, or too heavy for a smaller team.
  • Daily operational tasks—such as reviewing sales data or preparing reports—consume time that could be spent on customers or growth.

These challenges are not about a lack of effort. They are usually the result of tools that were never designed to work together. Xerpium addresses this by consolidating core business functions in one ERP platform.

How Xerpium supports core SMB needs

Xerpium is built around the idea that SMBs should not have to stitch together separate tools for selling, purchasing, stock control, and accounting. The platform unifies the following modules:

Sales and CRM

Xerpium brings Sales and CRM into the same centralized environment. Instead of maintaining customer conversations in one tool and transaction records in another, teams can manage customer-related activities and sales processes in a connected system. This reduces the need for manual cross-checking and helps keep customer information aligned with actual sales activity.

Purchasing and Inventory

Purchasing and Inventory are also part of the same ERP core. When purchasing data and stock levels live in the same system, it becomes easier to maintain consistent records and see how incoming purchases relate to inventory. For SMBs that previously tracked stock in separate applications or spreadsheets, this centralization removes a common source of errors and duplicated work.

Accounting

Accounting is included alongside the operational modules. Because the accounting function sits within the same platform as sales, purchasing, and inventory, financial data is not left isolated in a separate application. This connected structure helps SMB teams reduce manual reconciliation and keeps core financial processes aligned with day-to-day business activity.

AI Assistant

Xerpium includes an AI assistant directly inside the ERP. It is designed to help with daily activities such as:

  • Analyzing sales data.
  • Automating operational tasks.
  • Helping business owners and teams read and understand business data.

Rather than requiring SMBs to export data and analyze it elsewhere, the AI assistant provides support within the ERP itself, making business insights more accessible for teams without dedicated analysts.

Pricing built for SMB budgets

One of the main barriers to ERP adoption for SMBs is cost. Xerpium uses a per-user pricing model that starts at Rp150,000 per user per month for XERPIUM Base. This starting plan includes 500 AI credits per company per month, giving businesses access to AI-assisted features without needing a separate subscription.

A key point for growing businesses is that one Xerpium license covers all modules. There are no hidden fees tied to unlocking Sales, CRM, Purchasing, Inventory, or Accounting separately. This structure makes the cost more predictable and reduces the risk of receiving an ERP proposal that becomes expensive as modules are added later.

Instant onboarding for faster time-to-value

Traditional ERP implementations are often seen as long and complicated. Xerpium takes a different path with an onboarding flow designed to get SMBs operational faster.

When a business starts with Xerpium, it selects its business type—such as retail, services, or distribution. Based on that choice, Xerpium automatically prepares the chart of accounts (COA), relevant applications, and baseline system settings. Teams can then import their data and begin transacting without building the entire system from scratch.

This approach is especially relevant for SMBs that do not have large IT teams or dedicated ERP consultants. The baseline setup gives a starting point that matches the type of business, while still allowing the company to begin using the system in a practical way.

Business benefits of Xerpium for SMBs

For SMBs evaluating whether an ERP is worth the change, the practical benefits of Xerpium include:

  • Unified core operations: Sales, CRM, Purchasing, Inventory, and Accounting sit in one system, reducing reliance on disconnected applications.
  • Predictable per-user cost: XERPIUM Base starts at Rp150,000 per user per month, with one license covering all modules and no hidden fees.
  • Faster setup: Business type selection generates the COA, applications, and baseline system automatically, so teams can import data and start transactions sooner.
  • Built-in AI support: The AI assistant helps with sales analysis, operational automation, and reading business data without requiring a separate analytics tool.
  • Connected financial data: Because accounting is part of the same platform as operational modules, finance teams have a more consistent view of business activity.

Example use case: a distribution SMB getting started

Consider a small distribution business that has been managing customers in a spreadsheet, inventory in a separate app, and finances in accounting software. The team wants one system that can connect these areas without a lengthy implementation.

With Xerpium, the business selects “distribution” as its business type during onboarding. Xerpium prepares the chart of accounts, relevant applications, and baseline system settings automatically. The team imports its existing customer and product data, then begins recording transactions in the ERP.

From that point, sales and customer-related activities are managed in the Sales and CRM modules, while Purchasing and Inventory stay connected to those transactions. Accounting data is no longer maintained in isolation. When the business owner wants a quick view of sales performance, the AI assistant can help analyze sales data and support better daily decisions—without exporting files or building custom reports from scratch.

This example reflects the core premise of Xerpium for SMBs: connect essential business functions first, reduce manual handoffs, and let the system support daily operations rather than adding complexity.
