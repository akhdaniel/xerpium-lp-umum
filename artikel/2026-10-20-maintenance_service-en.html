# Maintenance Service Management with Xerpium ERP for Industrial Equipment Teams

Last updated: 20 October 2026

Industrial maintenance and service teams rarely operate in a straight line. A customer reports a problem, a service order is created, a quotation may be needed, technicians are dispatched, actual work is performed, checklists are completed, and an invoice is raised. Without a connected system, each of those steps creates friction: customer equipment details get lost, estimates diverge from actual costs, preventive schedules are missed, and invoicing becomes a manual follow-up exercise.

Xerpium ERP addresses this with a structured maintenance service workflow that connects service orders, work orders, customer equipment, preventive maintenance schedules, digital checklists, actual cost tracking, and invoicing in one system.

Common Maintenance Service Challenges

Many industrial service teams face similar operational issues:

  • Fragmented service requests with limited visibility into customer equipment history, location, or warranty status.
  • Difficult competitor and win/loss tracking during quotation and estimation.
  • Inconsistent recording of actual materials, labour, equipment, subcontractor, and overhead costs.
  • Manual preventive maintenance scheduling that leads to missed service dates.
  • Slow invoice creation after service completion, causing delayed revenue recognition.
  • Poor documentation of inspection results, especially when critical measurements are out of acceptable range.

How Xerpium ERP Supports Maintenance Service

Service Order Management

Xerpium provides a central service order record for incoming maintenance and repair requests. Each service order includes an auto-sequence service order number with tracking, customer information limited to company records, contact person, phone, and the equipment to be serviced through a many-to-many link to customer equipment.

Teams can classify work using standard fields such as service type — Maintenance, Repair, Overhaul, Installation, or Inspection — and urgency levels such as Routine, Urgent, or Emergency. A separate priority field supports sequence and visual prioritisation.

Xerpium also records lead source, date requested, and expected completion date. For competitive situations, the system tracks competitor name, quoted price, competitor strengths and weaknesses in notes, and provides a win/loss analysis of the offer versus the competitor.

Service order actions follow a controlled workflow:

  • Submit Estimate moves the order from Draft to Estimated using a confirmation wizard.
  • Create Quotation moves the order from Estimated to Quoted and automatically generates a sale order.
  • Create Work Order moves the order from Quoted to Work Order using a wizard that captures technician and schedule details.
  • Reset to Draft and Cancel provide control for revisions and terminations.

Each of these actions also synchronises the service order stage with its state through stage sync hooks.

Work Order Execution and Variance Analysis

Once a work order is created, Xerpium links it back to the originating service order and related sale order. The work order carries forward customer PO or SPK reference, service type, and equipment information from the service order.

Work orders include technician assignment through a many-to-many relationship with HR employees, as well as scheduled dates, start date, end date, and completion date. The work order lifecycle is managed through clear state actions:

  • Start Service sets the date started and moves the order from Confirmed to In Progress.
  • Pause Service moves In Progress work to On Hold.
  • Resume Service returns On Hold work to In Progress.
  • Complete sets the completion date and moves In Progress work to Completed.
  • Create Invoice moves Completed work to Invoiced using a wizard.
  • Close moves Invoiced work to Closed.
  • Cancel is available from any state.

A key control point is actual versus estimate variance analysis. Xerpium captures actual materials, actual on-site labour, actual workshop labour, actual equipment rental, actual subcontractor cost, and actual overhead. Actual lines are linked to their corresponding estimate line through the estimate line reference, allowing the system to automatically compute total estimate versus total actual and show profit margin.

Preventive Maintenance Scheduling

Xerpium supports recurring service through schedule configuration. Each schedule can be linked to customer equipment and includes an interval type such as Days, Months, or Operating Hours, with an interval value like 30 days, 3 months, or 500 hours.

Schedules also define the service type and next service date. Last service date updates automatically after service is performed. A boolean option allows the system to auto-generate work orders through a daily cron job.

The cron generation function detects overdue schedules where the next service date is today or earlier and auto-generate work orders is active. It creates a draft service order with an automatic description, submits the service order directly to the Estimated state, recalculates the next service date based on the interval, and tags the source as "Preventive Maintenance Schedule."

Digital Checklists and Inspection Evidence

For maintenance, repair, and inspection tasks, Xerpium includes checklist templates. Templates can optionally filter by equipment type and contain multiple checklist lines. Each line includes an item name, expected value or range, minimum and maximum acceptable values, unit of measure, critical item flag, and notes or measurement instructions.

During execution, technicians link a checklist to a work order, select the template, and record the execution date and executed-by user. The system automatically computes whether all checklist items passed.

Individual checklist results capture numeric measured value, text measured value, pass/fail status, notes, and photo attachments up to 1920×1920 pixels. Results remain linked to the original checklist template line for traceability.

Customer Equipment and Rotating Equipment Catalog

Xerpium maintains a customer equipment register that includes equipment name and code, customer, serial number, model, location at customer site, installation date, warranty expiry, current condition such as Running, Stop, or Needs Monitoring, and technical specifications such as horsepower, RPM, and capacity.

Customer equipment records are linked to a rotating equipment catalog. The catalog stores equipment name, code, equipment type, brand, model, horsepower, RPM, capacity, mounting type, full technical specifications, and common spare parts through a product many-to-many relationship.

Equipment type and brand are structured master data. Equipment type includes names such as Pump, Compressor, Turbine, Motor, Fan, Blower, and Gearbox. Equipment brand includes manufacturer names such as Grundfos, Sulzer, KSB, Atlas Copco, Ingersoll Rand, Siemens, and ABB, with optional manufacturer link and website.

Invoicing and Cross-Module Integration

The invoice wizard in Xerpium allows selection of journal, invoice amount, and note. Auto-line items are created from material, labour, and subcontractor actual lines. If no actual cost exists, the wizard falls back to a single line using the invoice amount.

Generated invoices are account moves with out_invoice type and the work order name as invoice origin. Invoice status is tracked as draft, partially invoiced, or invoiced, and the work order state is automatically set to Invoiced after invoice creation.

Xerpium connects maintenance service with other business areas:

  • Maintenance to Sales: service orders can generate sale orders automatically, and quotation line items come from estimate lines.
  • Maintenance to Accounting: invoice wizards generate out_invoice records with work order reference and invoice status tracking.
  • Maintenance to Inventory: material lines reference spare part products, and stock reservation supports work order materials.
  • Maintenance to HR: technician assignment and labour lines use employee records with manpower, days, and hours.
  • Maintenance to Analytic: cost centre tracking, estimate versus actual variance, and profitability analysis are available per service order.
  • Maintenance to Purchase: material requirements can flow to purchase requests, and subcontractor work can trigger purchase orders for subcontracted services.

AI-Assisted Maintenance Lookups

Xerpium includes maintenance tools that support operational visibility. One tool performs equipment lookup by customer name and equipment type, returning equipment name, code, customer, type, brand, model, serial number, location, condition, and last service date, up to 50 records. Another tool retrieves work order status by customer name and state, returning work order name, customer, service type, state, scheduled date, total cost, and invoice status. A schedule check tool accepts customer name and an overdue-only filter, returning schedule, customer, next service date, and overdue status based on next service date compared with today.

Business Benefits

  • Centralised service order, work order, equipment, and schedule data for better operational control.
  • Clear estimate versus actual tracking that exposes cost overruns and protects profit margin.
  • Automated preventive maintenance generation that reduces missed service dates.
  • Structured quotation and invoicing flows that shorten the service-to-cash cycle.
  • Digital checklists and photo evidence that improve inspection quality and compliance.
  • Cross-module integration that links maintenance with sales, inventory, purchasing, HR, accounting, and analytics.

Example Use Case: Monthly Pump Inspection

A customer has a pump under a preventive maintenance schedule with a 30-day interval. When the next service date is due, Xerpium’s daily cron creates a draft service order, submits it to Estimated, and recalculates the next service date.

The service team creates a quotation, which automatically generates a sale order. After approval, a work order is created with an assigned technician and schedule. The technician starts the work order, selects the pump inspection checklist, and records measured values for vibration, temperature, and oil pressure. If all critical items pass, the work order can be completed. Actual materials and labour are captured, variance against estimate is calculated, and the invoice wizard creates an out_invoice with auto-generated line items. The work order moves to Invoiced and then Closed, while the equipment’s last service date updates automatically.

Conclusion

Maintenance service is more than scheduling a technician. It requires disciplined handling of customer equipment, estimates, actual costs, checklists, preventive schedules, and invoices. Xerpium ERP brings those processes into a connected maintenance service workflow, giving industrial service teams the visibility and control they need without building separate point solutions.
