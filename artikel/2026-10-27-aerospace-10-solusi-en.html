10 Best ERP Solutions for Aerospace Manufacturing Operations

October 27, 2026

Aerospace manufacturing operates under constraints few other industries face: full material traceability from raw stock to finished part, AS9100 and AS9102 compliance, serialized component tracking across flight hours and overhaul cycles, and zero tolerance for undocumented change. When these requirements are scattered across spreadsheets, disconnected quality tools, and a generic production system, the result is predictable — lost traceability records, FAI failures, unapproved engineering changes slipping into the BOM, and AOG orders stuck behind routine work. The following ten solutions, drawn from the XERPIUM AEROSPACE module, address these problems as connected parts of one manufacturing ecosystem rather than isolated point tools.

The 10 Solutions at a Glance

  1. Aircraft Type & Configuration — Defines aircraft specifications and as-designed, as-built, and as-maintained configurations.
  2. Part Number & Serial Number Tracking — Serialized identity for every part with ITAR/EAR flags and overhaul tracking.
  3. Work Center & Tooling Management — Certified work centers with calibration-controlled tooling.
  4. Supplier Qualification — Tiered supplier management with AS9100D certification and quality ratings.
  5. Engineering Change Order (ECO) — Controlled change workflow linked directly to the BOM.
  6. Material Traceability — Batch, heat, and certificate records from raw material to finished part.
  7. Manufacturing Order (MO) — Production orders extended for aircraft type, priority, and quality release.
  8. Work Order with Digital Sign-off — Operator-assigned work orders with tool tracking and sign-off.
  9. Quality — NCR, CAPA, FAI, Inspection — Non-conformance, corrective action, and AS9102 inspection.
  10. KPI Dashboard & Cross-Module Integration — Real-time metrics and automated accounting, inventory, and HR links.

1. Aircraft Type & Configuration

This module establishes the foundational master data for every program. Each aircraft type carries a unique code, manufacturer, category (fixed_wing, rotary_wing, uav, or glider), and key specifications such as MTOW, range, cruise speed, engine count and type, seats, and certification standard. Configurations are tracked in as-designed, as-built, and as-maintained states, each with its own variant code, description, and linked BOM. This solves the common problem of ambiguous build definitions — when teams cannot reconcile what was engineered against what was actually assembled, rework and certification gaps follow. Here, every configuration maps to a specific BOM revision.

2. Part Number & Serial Number Tracking

Part numbers are classified by type — raw, component, assembly, sub-assembly, tooling, or consumable — and carry alternates, drawing number and revision, material specification, weight, and critical ITAR/EAR control flags. Serialized or batch tracking is defined at the part level. Each serial number is unique, linked to its product and aircraft type, and carries a status (available, installed, in_repair, scrapped) along with accumulated flight hours, cycles, and overhaul due dates. This directly addresses the loss of individual part history. Without serialized identity, overhaul scheduling becomes guesswork and export-controlled parts risk mishandling — both resolved by tracking identity at the unit level.

3. Work Center & Tooling Management

Work centers extend the standard MRP work center with aerospace-specific attributes: center type (assembly, machining, testing, paint, inspection, tooling), required certifications, the aircraft types each center can handle, calibration due dates, and environmental controls. Tooling is managed separately with unique tool numbers, type classification (fixture, gauge, special, standard, measuring), calibration intervals, last and next calibration dates, status, and owner. Sample centers include Assembly Line 1, Machining 1, Testing Station 1, Paint Shop 1, Quality Inspection 1, and Tooling 1. This prevents a frequent failure point — using out-of-calibration tooling or uncertified work centers, which invalidates inspection results.

4. Supplier Qualification

Aerospace supply chains depend on verified, auditable suppliers. This module classifies suppliers by tier (1, 2, or 3) and records certifications such as AS9100D, a quality rating from 0 to 100, on-time delivery rate, the list of approved parts, qualification status, and the last audit date. By keeping qualification data alongside approved parts, procurement teams avoid ordering from unqualified or lapsed sources. The problem it solves is opaque supplier risk: when approval status and audit history live in email threads rather than the system, non-conforming material enters production unnoticed. Centralizing this data makes supplier compliance enforceable at the point of purchase.

5. Engineering Change Order (ECO)

Uncontrolled engineering change is one of the most costly risks in aerospace manufacturing. The ECO module manages change types — design, process, material, tooling, and documentation — each linked to the affected BOM, part numbers (via a many-to-many relationship), and aircraft type. A defined workflow moves each ECO through Draft, Under Review, Approved, Implemented, and Closed, with multi-approver sign-off capturing reviewer and approver actions and timestamps. Because the change order connects directly to the MRP BOM for revision tracking, approved changes propagate to production with a clear audit trail. This eliminates the scenario where drawings and BOMs drift apart after an undocumented revision.

6. Material Traceability

Full traceability from raw material to finished part is a regulatory baseline, and this module delivers it. Each material trace record captures batch number, heat treatment number, material certificate, and supplier certificate. It stores chemical composition and mechanical properties, plus test report references, dates, and notes. Critically, every trace record links directly to the aerospace serial number, closing the loop between material lot and installed part. The operational problem here is the audit gap — when a supplier recalls a heat lot, manufacturers must identify every affected serial quickly. With a complete audit trail, that investigation becomes a query rather than a crisis.

7. Manufacturing Order (MO)

The manufacturing order extends the standard MRP production order with aerospace context: aircraft type, serial number, configuration, customer, and priority levels ranging from AOG through Critical, High, Normal, and Low. It flags whether a quality release is required and links to the aerospace quality release record. Sign-off fields capture whether approval is needed, who signed off, the sign-off date, and the actual completion date, with auto-numbering per company and mail thread tracking. MOs are generated from the BOM for a given aircraft type and configuration. This solves scheduling blindness — teams can finally distinguish an AOG order from routine work and release it accordingly.

8. Work Order with Digital Sign-off

Work orders extend the MRP work order to assign an operator from the HR employee records, enforce sign-off requirements, and record who signed off and when. Each work order supports many-to-many tool assignment from the tooling registry and includes a dedicated field for special instructions. A digital sign-off action sets the operator, applies a timestamp, and marks completion. Operators are assigned based on their qualifications. This addresses accountability gaps on the shop floor — when there is no record of who performed an operation with which tool under which instructions, non-conformances cannot be investigated. Here, every step is attributable and traceable.

9. Quality — NCR, CAPA, FAI, Inspection

This integrated quality suite manages the full compliance cycle. NCRs capture auto-generated non-conformance numbers, severity, root cause, and disposition (use-as-is, rework, repair, scrap, return-to-supplier), linked to the manufacturing order, affected part, serial number, and quantity. CAPA records derive from NCR findings with action plans, assignees, due dates, effectiveness verification, and preventive monitoring. FAI supports full, partial, and delta inspections per AS9102, with inspection lines comparing nominal, min, max, and actual values for automatic pass/fail. Inspection plans cover incoming, in-process, and final checks, feeding into certificate-of-conformance quality releases. This consolidates fragmented quality records into one closed-loop system.

10. KPI Dashboard & Cross-Module Integration

The final solution ties the ecosystem together. A KPI dashboard tracks total manufacturing orders by state, production efficiency against a 100% target, work center downtime trends, open NCRs, and FAI pass/fail rates. Cross-module integration automates WIP and COGS journals against an aerospace-specific chart of accounts, maintains serial number and lot traceability in inventory, connects supplier management and approved parts to purchasing, links operator assignment and certification tracking to HR, and offers an AI assistant to search part numbers, check MO status, and generate reports. This solves the disconnection problem at the root — one data flow, not many.

How to Choose the Right Solution

Evaluate your compliance scope first: if you must meet AS9100D and AS9102, prioritize modules with built-in FAI, NCR, and certificate-of-conformance workflows rather than bolted-on quality tools. Second, assess traceability depth — confirm the system links material batches and heat lots to individual serial numbers, not just finished orders. Third, weigh change control; an ECO workflow that connects directly to the BOM prevents revision drift. Finally, consider integration: accounting, inventory, purchasing, and HR should share one data model so sign-offs, costs, and supplier ratings reinforce each other instead of requiring duplicate entry.

Conclusion

Aerospace manufacturing punishes disconnected systems with failed inspections, lost traceability, and uncontrolled change. The ten solutions above form a single ecosystem — from aircraft configuration and serialized tracking through ECO, material traceability, quality, and automated accounting — so that compliance and production reinforce one another.
