# Agriculture ERP for Plantations and Farms: Managing Harvests, Costs, and Compliance with Xerpium

Agriculture and plantation businesses operate with a level of complexity that many generic ERP systems are not built to handle. Biological assets grow and change value over time, harvest costs must be separated from other operational expenses, input purchases such as fertilizer, seeds, and pesticides need direct cost tracking, and seasonal labor creates distinct payroll and attendance requirements. In Indonesia, agricultural companies also face specific financial reporting and tax obligations, including agricultural accounting standards and PPN 11%.

Xerpium addresses these requirements with an agriculture-focused ERP configuration that combines localized accounting, agricultural financial reports, operational dashboards, and workforce management. The result is a system that reflects how farms, plantations, and agribusiness companies actually operate, from harvest cycles to buyer relationships.

Common Challenges in Agriculture and Plantation Operations

Before selecting an ERP, agricultural companies often deal with several recurring issues:

  • Accounting does not align with agricultural standards. Biological assets, harvest COGS, government subsidies, land costs, and equipment depreciation require a specialized account structure.
  • Reporting is manual and fragmented. Balance sheets, profit and loss statements, and cash flow reports are often built outside the ERP or mapped inconsistently.
  • There is limited real-time visibility into harvest revenue, input costs, stock value, receivables, pending purchase orders, and workforce numbers.
  • Seasonal and daily workers are difficult to manage. Payroll may be daily or contract-based, attendance is field-level, and labor is spread across different commodities or land areas.
  • Field operations such as planting, fertilizer application, harvesting, and equipment usage are rarely connected to financial and inventory data.

How Xerpium ERP Addresses Agricultural Requirements

Localized Agricultural Chart of Accounts

Xerpium provides a dedicated agriculture and plantation chart of accounts through its agriculture localization. The account codes use a 6-digit standard with an ag_ prefix, creating a consistent structure for agricultural financial data.

Key pre-configured accounts include receivable ag_120100, payable ag_210100, revenue ag_410100, and harvest or input COGS ag_510100. The account groups follow agricultural accounting standards, while PPN 11% defaults are already configured for both sales and purchases. Currency exchange gains and losses are mapped to ag_710200 and ag_720200, and cash differences are captured in ag_710300 and ag_720300.

Agricultural Financial Reports

Financial reporting for agriculture is structured according to the same agricultural standards. The report structure includes a balance sheet with a biological asset group, a profit and loss statement that separates harvest COGS from other costs, and an operational cash flow statement designed for agricultural activities.

When the agricultural chart of accounts template is applied, account groups automatically sync to the report structure. This happens through XML parsing of the agricultural report structure and a post-load hook that activates during the chart template application. This reduces manual mapping and helps ensure that reports reflect the correct agricultural account groups from day one.

Agriculture KPI Dashboard

Xerpium includes an agriculture dashboard with a green agribusiness theme and a responsive 12-column layout. The dashboard consolidates operational and financial information into eight KPI tiles: total harvest revenue, total sales orders, input cost, receivables from buyers, active workers, stock value of harvested products, pending input purchase orders, and invoices this month.

Four charts support deeper analysis: monthly revenue, cost trends, top commodities, and top buyers. The dashboard draws from ORM sources such as sale orders, purchase orders, stock quantities, employees, and invoices, with SQL sources supporting monthly revenue, cost trends, top products, and buyer analytics. A global date filter applies automatically and refreshes every five minutes, keeping the dashboard relevant for daily operational decisions.

Agricultural Accounting Controls

The accounting configuration goes beyond basic receivables and payables. Xerpium provides a biological asset account group for productive and consumptive plants and livestock. Harvest COGS is kept separate from other cost of goods sold accounts, so plantation owners and finance teams can see the direct cost of each harvest.

The system also includes accounts for government subsidies for fertilizer and seed, land management costs such as rent and land tax, and depreciation for agricultural tools and plantation equipment. Sales and purchase journals are pre-configured with refund_sequence=True, supporting controlled transaction sequencing for agricultural operations.

Commodity, Stock, and Buyer Analytics

Xerpium’s agriculture analytics help users understand what is driving revenue and cost. The dashboard can display revenue per commodity for the top 10 agricultural products, a 12-month revenue and cost trend, and the top 10 buyers such as distributors, markets, or exporters. Worker metrics show active employees per department, while stock valuation tracks the value of harvested products in warehouses.

Harvest cycle tracking provides visibility into each product cycle, and input cost reporting tracks fertilizer, seed, and pesticide spending per month. These analytics connect financial outcomes directly to operational and sales activity.

Workforce, Equipment, and Field Operations

Agriculture operations depend on people and equipment. Xerpium supports worker management for both permanent and seasonal employees. Payroll can handle daily wages and contract-based payments for harvest workers, and attendance tracking covers field workers across kebun or sawah locations.

Departments can be organized by commodity or land area. Equipment tracking covers agricultural tools such as tractors and sprayers. A shared calendar supports scheduling for planting, harvesting, and fertilizer application. In addition to standard financial statements, Xerpium provides agricultural reports for harvest results, input purchases, and land usage per commodity.

Business Benefits of Xerpium Agriculture ERP

  • Local accounting and tax compliance from the start, with a 6-digit agricultural chart of accounts and pre-configured PPN 11%.
  • Faster report generation through automatic account group synchronization and agricultural report structures.
  • One operational dashboard for harvest revenue, input costs, receivables, stock value, labor, and pending purchase orders.
  • Better cost control by separating harvest COGS, biological assets, subsidies, land costs, and depreciation.
  • Stronger commercial decisions using revenue per commodity, monthly cost trends, and top buyer analytics.

Use Case: Managing a Plantation Harvest Cycle

A plantation company applies the Xerpium agriculture chart of accounts template. The agricultural account groups sync automatically to the report structure, and PPN 11% is already configured on purchase and sales transactions.

The purchasing team creates purchase orders for fertilizer, seeds, and pesticides. These input costs flow into the dashboard’s input cost KPI and into the profit and loss statement under the input and harvest COGS accounts. Harvest sales are recorded under agricultural revenue, and stock value is updated in internal warehouse locations. Biological assets are reported on the balance sheet, and equipment depreciation is posted through the agricultural account groups.

Field supervisors manage seasonal workers with daily attendance and daily or contract-based payroll. Managers review the agriculture dashboard to see monthly revenue, cost trends, top commodities, and top buyers. Before the next planting cycle, the team reviews harvest reports, input reports, and land usage reports to plan more accurately.
